**How to read a normative statement here.** Every statement in this section is one of four things, and each one says which it is:

- **Consolidated.** The statement quotes or restates an existing artifact and cites it. The cited artifact remains the normative source; this section only gathers it.
- **Derivation.** The statement follows by an inference the sentence states, and it is marked `Derivation:`. Its basis is either a cited artifact or the shipped code, and the two carry different force. A derivation from a cited artifact carries that artifact's force. A derivation from shipped code reports what the code does today and carries none: the artifact-flow invariant in `CLAUDE.md` states that "Code does not inform specs," so no statement in this section becomes normative by being true of the code.
- **Open question.** Either no artifact answers the question, or two artifacts answer it differently, or an artifact answers it and the shipped code does something else; §27.7 records the question, marks which of those three it is, and names the human decision it waits on. This section never fills such a gap with an invented construction, domain separator, or verifier rule.
- **Ruling.** A human decided a question this section covers. The statement names the human, gives the date, and states the decision. Where the corpus holds the human's own words the statement quotes them; where the corpus holds a statement of the decision and not its wording, the statement says so. A ruling binds because the human made it, so every downstream artifact conforms to one: an ADR, a story, or a shipped type that contradicts a ruling changes. §27.4.6 carries the ruling on the key-destruction family and §27.3.6 restates it beside that family's construction. Recording a ruling here settles nothing about which file the corpus finally keeps a family's normative text in, which open question OQ-38 asks a human to choose, and a ruling travels with its family's sections wherever that answer sends them.

Where two artifacts disagree and the artifact flow in `CLAUDE.md` orders them, that invariant decides which one governs. It states the order — "plans → specs → ADRs → stories → source code" — and states that "Upstream artifacts govern downstream artifacts. Never the reverse. Code does not inform specs." So a spec governs an ADR that contradicts it, a spec governs a story that contradicts it, and a spec governs shipped code that contradicts it: the upstream artifact stands and the downstream one changes. A human decides only whether to amend the upstream artifact instead, and §27.7 marks each such entry `Settled upstream; the downstream artifact diverges`, naming the diverging artifact. Where two artifacts the flow does not order disagree — two spec sections, or a spec section and an audit recommendation — no invariant decides between them, and §27.7 marks those entries `Undefined` and names the human decision each waits on.

## 27.1 Scope and taxonomy

### 27.1.1 What the word means

The trust spec states the meaning directly (§7.4 of `.docs/specs/07-trust-validation-and-capabilities.md`, Layer 3: Attestation Authenticity):

> Attestations are signed claims by identities about something. The protocol verifies their authenticity — that the claim was really made by the stated issuer — but not their truth.

The same spec places attestation above any one subsystem (§7.6, Attestation as Protocol Primitive):

> Attestation is not a feature of any single section of SCP — it is a primitive used by every layer

and asserts that one envelope unifies them:

> The common envelope format (§7.4.1) unifies these under a single verifiable structure. The verification mechanics are the same regardless of attestation type: check signature, check evidence, check expiry, check revocation. What varies is the claim content and how it's evaluated.

**Both claims range over a narrower set than the eight families below.** The §7.6 bullets enumerate what "these" covers before the unification sentence: identity links, agent capability metadata, role assignments, outlet integrity attestations, UCAN capability tokens, endorsements, provenance chains, and shadow identity claims. The Trust bullet's participation clause reads, verbatim, "Participation records are computed from verified event attestations" (`.docs/specs/07-trust-validation-and-capabilities.md:1035`), which places the attestations upstream of a participation record rather than calling the record one. §7.4.1 scopes itself the same way, listing eight `type` values — `identity_link | capability_delegation | outlet_integrity | endorsement | role_assignment | agent_capability | context_endorsement | participation_witness`. Its `participation_witness` value names tag 7 of the shipped F2 `AttestationType` enum (§27.3.2), not F8's `ParticipationProfile`. Device attestation (F3), key-custody attestation (F4), KeyPackage attestation (F5), key-destruction attestation (F6), custody-violation attestation (F7), and participation profile (F8) appear in neither list, so neither section claims the envelope covers them.

*Derivation:* of the eight families §27.1.3 lists, F1 and F2 carry the §7.4.1 envelope and the other six do not — two of the six carry no signature field at all, one carries a signature that no construction defines, and the verification mechanics differ in every family. §27.2 gives the field-by-field evidence. That is a statement about the eight families this section gathers, not a falsification of §7.6, because whether F3 through F8 belong to the attestation category is open question OQ-1, and whether §7.6's bullets and §7.4.1's `type` list reach F8 is open question OQ-35. Open question OQ-35 asks whether §7.6 and §7.4.1 should narrow their unification claim to the constructs they enumerate, or whether the other six families should be brought onto the §7.4.1 envelope.

### 27.1.2 The membership criterion is undecided

The §7.4 sentence is the only membership sentence the corpus states, and it does not decide membership. Applied literally, "signed claims by identities about something" admits every signed structure the security spec tabulates (§9.5.2 of `.docs/specs/09-security-model.md`, the signed-structure definitions): an `InnerEnvelope` is a signed claim by an identity about a payload, a `SignedVote` is a signed claim by an identity about a proposal, a `BlockNotification` is a signed claim by an identity about another identity. §9.5.2 tabulates eleven structures, of which ten are signed — the eleventh row, `GovernanceProposal ID`, is labelled "(hash, not signature)" and produces a SHA-256 digest that no identity signs, so the §7.4 sentence does not reach it. Three of the ten are the families F2, F5, and F8 below, and the sentence admits the other seven.

Calling those seven non-targets is the author's selection, not a corpus statement, so it does not falsify the §7.4 sentence on its own. No artifact calls `InnerEnvelope`, `BroadcastEnvelope`, `SenderKeyEpochAdvance`, `SenderKeyRequest`, `BlockNotification`, `AccessKeyRequest`, or `SignedVote` an attestation, and no artifact calls F8's `ParticipationProfile` one either — §27.1.1 quotes the §7.6 Trust bullet placing the attestations upstream of a participation record — yet §27.1.3 counts F8 among the eight. A name-and-usage test applied consistently therefore moves F8 out of the candidate set as well, which is why the preamble to §27 states that "A name test does not produce it in either direction." What the §7.4 sentence does establish on its own is narrower: it states a property ten of the eleven §9.5.2 structures satisfy, and it states no test that separates the ten from each other. OQ-1 carries to the human both the question of whether the seven are non-targets and the question of what test decides membership.

**The upstream planning session states the same proposition §7.6 of the trust spec carries, and it is not a second criterion.** Planning session 02 reads (`.docs/planning-sessions/planning-session-02.md:637`): "The key realization: attestation is not a feature of one section — it's a primitive used across the entire protocol. Identity links, UCAN delegation tokens, tool integrity proofs, agent capability metadata, endorsements, role assignments, context endorsements — they're all attestations. Different claim content, same envelope structure, same verification mechanics." The paragraph enumerates seven named constructs and reports two properties they share. It states no test a reader applies to an eighth construct, and the session's next heading confirms the enumeration is the whole set (`:643`): "Seven attestation types were identified."

*Derivation:* §7.6 of the trust spec is that same paragraph carried downstream, so the two artifacts carry one proposition rather than two opposed criteria. Four sentence pairs match: session `:637` "attestation is not a feature of one section — it's a primitive used across the entire protocol" against §7.6 "Attestation is not a feature of any single section of SCP — it is a primitive used by every layer" (`.docs/specs/07-trust-validation-and-capabilities.md:1030`); session `:639` "a single common envelope format" against §7.6 "The common envelope format (§7.4.1) unifies these under a single verifiable structure" (`:1039`); session `:639` "The verification flow is always: check signature → check evidence → check expiry → check revocation" against §7.6 "check signature, check evidence, check expiry, check revocation" (`:1039`); and session `:637` "Different claim content, same envelope structure, same verification mechanics" against §7.6 "What varies is the claim content and how it's evaluated" (`:1039`). The artifact flow in `CLAUDE.md` runs "plans → specs → ADRs → stories → source code," so the planning session sits above §7.6 and governs it; the two do not conflict, so nothing turns on that ordering here.

*Derivation:* the corpus therefore states one criterion — §7.4's "signed claims by identities about something" — and one enumeration of seven constructs that share an envelope and a verification flow. Neither decides whether F3 through F8 belong to the attestation category, because six of the eight carry neither the §7.4.1 envelope nor F2's verification mechanics (§27.2.2) and none of the six appears in the enumeration. OQ-1 asks the human for a criterion the corpus does not yet carry, and OQ-35 asks whether §7.6 and §7.4.1 should narrow their unification claim to the constructs they enumerate.

**Open question OQ-1 in §27.7 asks a human to state the criterion.** Until they do, this section treats the eight families as the set its author selected, listed in §27.1.3, not as instances of a rule. The types the selection left out are named in §27, and OQ-1 covers them too.

They do not decide membership, and a new construct that exhibits all three is a candidate for the human's ruling, not an automatic member. *Derivation from the field matrix of §27.2.1:* each indicator states which families exhibit it, and no property in the matrix separates all eight from the other structures of §9.5.2 of the security spec — which is itself the evidence OQ-1 rests on:

### 27.2.2 How far each property generalizes

*Derivation from the matrix above, ordered by how many families carry the property.*

3. **A named subject — six of eight.** F3 and F4 name none. The `Subject named` row above leaves both cells blank, and §27.1.2 indicator 1 states the same count.

**A signature is not universal.** F3 and F4 carry no signature field. Both rely on the enclosing DID document instead:

- F4's module documentation states the mechanism (`crates/scp-did/src/attestation.rs:8`): "The attestation is signed by `#0` (Identity Key) as part of the DID document publication." The DID-document self-certification that makes this work is the BEP44 record signature described in §9.6.1 of the security spec.
- F3's storage helper writes the raw token into a DID-document service entry (`DidDocument::set_device_attestation_token`, `crates/scp-did/src/document.rs:845`), which the same BEP44 signature covers.

*Derivation:* for a `did:dht` identity, the DID document's BEP44 signature authenticates that **the DID owner published these bytes**. It does not authenticate the bytes' internal claim, and it binds nothing inside F3's opaque token to the DID under which it sits. §27.3.3 and §27.3.4 state the consequences.

**The BEP44 argument covers one of the two production DID methods.** §9.7.1 of the security spec names both as in scope (`.docs/specs/09-security-model.md:648`): "This applies to **every** production DID method without exception — `did:dht:z*` **and** `did:web:*` (§9.6.2) are both in scope". §9.6.2 of the security spec removes the argument for the second (`.docs/specs/09-security-model.md:578`): "**did:web is NOT self-certifying.** A compromised server, DNS hijack, or CA compromise can serve a fraudulent DID document indistinguishable from a legitimate one." *Derivation:* F3 and F4 carry no signature of their own and take their whole integrity from the enclosing document, so on a `did:web` identity an F3 device token and an F4 custody declaration carry no cryptographic authentication at all, and a reader cannot distinguish an inserted entry from one the DID owner published. Open question OQ-48 asks what a relying party checks on an F3 token or an F4 custody entry read from a `did:web` document.

**A named signer is not universal.** F8 identifies its signer only by a raw 32-byte Ed25519 public key, deliberately: the field's doc comment gives the reason (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/participation.rs:739`) — "Context-specific Ed25519 public key used to sign this profile. Derived with domain separation to prevent cross-context correlation" — and §7.3.2.1 of the trust spec makes the omission the privacy guarantee in a struct comment: "// NOTE: no context_id — this is the privacy guarantee" (`.docs/specs/07-trust-validation-and-capabilities.md:255`). The type's own documentation states it as prose (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/participation.rs:690`): "**Privacy guarantee:** The profile intentionally omits `context_id`." F6 names only `member_did`, which is simultaneously signer and subject. F3 and F4 name no signer.

**A revocation-status field appears only in F1 and F2; an expiry appears in F1, F2, and F5.** F5's `expires_at` is mandatory and pinned to the leaf `Lifetime.not_after` (§27.3.5). Three families withdraw a published record by a mechanism other than a revocation field: F5 by rotating the signing key (§9.7.1 of the security spec), F8 by replacing the statement in place (§7.3.2.1 of the trust spec), and F7 never, by design. F3 and F4 are withdrawn by rewriting the whole DID document — `DidDocument::remove_device_attestation` deletes F3's service entry (`crates/scp-did/src/document.rs:632`) and a republished document carries a different or absent F4 entry — and neither family carries a revocation record a consumer can read. F6 has neither a rewrite path nor a revocation record. §27.5 gives each family's path, and open question OQ-24 records the three gaps.

**Only F1 and F2 share the envelope of §7.4.1 of the trust spec.** F5, F6, F7, and F8 carry a flat, purpose-built field set. F3 and F4 carry no envelope at all.

*Derivation:* the eight families share no field-level property, so the matrix supplies no evidence that they form one category. Whether they form one category is open question OQ-1.

## 27.3 Canonical signing construction

The security spec defines one construction for every signed structure (§9.5.1 of `.docs/specs/09-security-model.md`, Canonical Hash Construction):

> **Construction:** `SHA-256(domain_separator || field_1 || field_2 || ... || field_N)`

with nine encoding rules. The seven the families below exercise, quoted from the same section:

> - **Domain separator:** UTF-8 string, no length prefix (the separator itself is fixed per struct version).
> - **Variable-length bytes** (strings, byte arrays of unknown length): 4-byte big-endian length prefix followed by the raw bytes. `len(field) as u32` in network byte order.
> - **Fixed-length bytes** (`[u8; 32]`, `[u8; 64]`): raw bytes, no length prefix. The length is known from the schema.
> - **u64 integers:** 8 bytes, big-endian (network byte order).
> - **u16 integers:** 2 bytes, big-endian.
> - **u8 integers:** 1 byte (no endianness — a single octet).
> - **Optional fields:** if present, encoded as above. If absent, encoded as `SHA-256(0x00)` (32-byte sentinel).

F2's `attestation_type` exercises the u16 rule (§27.3.2) and F8's `outlet_invocation_count_anchored` exercises the u8 rule (§27.3.8). §9.5.1 states two further rules no family below exercises: "**u32 integers:** 4 bytes, big-endian." and "**Fixed-length bytes of other sizes** (`[u8; 16]`): raw bytes, no length prefix." None of the nine rules names an enum or a discriminator, which §27.3.6 records as the gap under F6's `method` field.

The shared implementation lives in `crates/scp-protocol/src/crypto/canonical.rs`: `canonical_hash` returns the 32-byte digest, and `canonical_hash_bytes` returns the preimage without hashing it. Three of the eight families use it. Two hand-roll a concatenation. Three define no construction at all.

### 27.3.1 F1 — Identity-link attestation

**Domain separator:** `"SCP-IDENTITY-LINK-ATTESTATION-V1:"`, passed to `canonical_hash` at `crates/scp-protocol/src/identity/attestation.rs:578`.

**Consolidated** from §3.5.2 of the identity spec, Signature scope:

> The signature covers the §9.5.1 canonical hash of `(id, attestation_type, issuer, subject, issued_at, expires_at, claim, evidence, revocation_status)` using domain separator `"SCP-IDENTITY-LINK-ATTESTATION-V1:"`. String and DID fields use 4-byte BE length-prefixed encoding, `issued_at` uses 8-byte BE u64, `expires_at` uses the absent sentinel when not set, and sub-structures (`claim`, `evidence`, `revocation_status`) are individually serialized as MessagePack (sorted-key encoding) and included as variable-length byte fields.

The shipped `canonical_signing_bytes` matches that field order exactly, calling `canonical_hash` with the nine fields in the order the spec lists and returning the 32-byte digest (`crates/scp-protocol/src/identity/attestation.rs:557`). `verify_signature` then hands that digest to `verify_ed25519_signature`, so **Ed25519 signs the 32-byte hash, not the preimage** (`crates/scp-protocol/src/identity/attestation.rs:536`). Contradiction C29 records where the same function's sub-structure encoding departs from the same quoted sentence.

**Contradiction C28 — two doc comments on the shipped type name a different preimage.** The struct's own documentation states (`crates/scp-protocol/src/identity/attestation.rs:399`): "The `signature` covers the canonical `MessagePack` serialization of all other fields (sorted-key encoding per §17.1), signed by the issuer's `#active` or `#agent` key." The `signature` field repeats it (`:443`): "Ed25519 signature over the canonical `MessagePack` of all other fields." *Derivation from shipped code:* `canonical_signing_bytes` reaches MessagePack only for the three sub-structures `claim`, `evidence`, and `revocation_status`, and produces the §9.5.1 canonical hash over nine enumerated fields, so the two doc comments describe bytes the code does not produce. A binding author who follows them signs a different preimage. This is the same class as contradiction C26, a code comment describing a state that does not hold. Open question OQ-45.

**Contradiction C29 — §3.5.2 of the identity spec requires sorted-key MessagePack for the three sub-structures; the shipped encoder emits declaration order.** The sentence quoted above states the requirement: sub-structures "are individually serialized as MessagePack (sorted-key encoding)". `canonical_signing_bytes` serializes all three through `rmp_serde::to_vec_named` (`crates/scp-protocol/src/identity/attestation.rs:560`, `:565`, `:571`), and that encoder emits struct fields in declaration order — the property another module in the same crate relies on and states (`crates/scp-protocol/src/context/outlets/message_catalog.rs:287`): "`rmp-serde` emits struct fields in declaration order, so the encoded map keys are alphabetical without an explicit sort. Any future field addition to [`MessageTemplate`] MUST preserve alphabetical declaration order". `AttestationClaim` does not preserve it: it declares `platform`, `platform_handle`, `platform_id`, `link_type` (`crates/scp-protocol/src/identity/attestation.rs:311`–`:324`), while sorted key order is `link_type`, `platform`, `platform_handle`, `platform_id`. The function's own doc comment asserts the opposite of what the encoder does (`:550`): "This method is deterministic: identical attestation data always produces identical bytes, regardless of serde field ordering." *Derivation from shipped code:* the two orders produce different MessagePack bytes for the same claim, so they produce a different `claim` field in the preimage and a different SHA-256, and a signer written from §3.5.2 produces signatures `verify_signature` rejects. §25.13 Vector 26 of the test-vector spec cannot catch the divergence, because it publishes no expected hash — it closes "Compute the expected SHA-256 hash using the Rust reference implementation." — and it equates the two encodings in one phrase, "MessagePack (`rmp_serde::to_vec_named`, sorted-key encoding)". Open question OQ-45.

**Inside the signed scope:** `id`, `attestation_type`, `issuer`, `subject`, `issued_at`, `expires_at`, `claim`, `evidence`, `revocation_status`.

**Outside the signed scope:** `signature` itself, and nothing else — the struct has exactly ten fields.

**A second, separate construction computes the record id.** *Consolidated* from §3.5.2 of the identity spec:

```
id = hex(SHA-256(
  "SCP-ATTESTATION-ID-V1:"                          (22 bytes, no length prefix)
  || BE32(len(issuer_did))  || issuer_did            (4 + N bytes)
  || BE32(len(platform))    || platform              (4 + N bytes)
  || BE32(len(platform_handle)) || platform_handle   (4 + N bytes)
  || BE64(issued_at)                                 (8 bytes)
))
```

`compute_id` hand-rolls that same byte sequence with a `Sha256` hasher rather than calling `canonical_hash` (`crates/scp-protocol/src/identity/attestation.rs:493`). *Derivation:* the two produce identical bytes, because `canonical_hash` writes the domain separator unprefixed, each `VarBytes` field as a 4-byte big-endian length followed by the bytes, and each `U64` as 8 big-endian bytes — the exact sequence `compute_id` writes by hand.

**Contradiction C1 — the domain-separator registry omits both separators.** The domain-separator table (§9.18.2 of the security spec) states the purpose a reader uses it for: "Each separator identifies the struct or derivation being hashed to prevent cross-protocol hash confusion." Neither `"SCP-IDENTITY-LINK-ATTESTATION-V1:"` nor `"SCP-ATTESTATION-ID-V1:"` appears in its rows, although §3.5.2 of the identity spec defines both and the shipped code uses both. Open question OQ-20.

**Contradiction C2 — the ADR incremented the separator; the spec and the code did not.** ADR-044, the attestation two-class model and provider registry (`.docs/adrs/phase-6.md:3453`), closes with a migration note:

> Changing the canonical signing bytes (replacing `revocation` with `revocation_status`) requires incrementing the domain separator from `SCP-IDENTITY-LINK-ATTESTATION-V1:` to `V2` per §9.5.1. No production attestations exist, so no backward compatibility is needed. The V2 separator is adopted in this spec change. The implementation PR uses V2 from the start.

§3.5.2 of the identity spec uses `V1`. The shipped constant uses `V1`. The known-answer vector (§25.13 Vector 26 of the test-vector spec) uses `V1`. The artifact flow in `CLAUDE.md` runs "plans → specs → ADRs → stories → source code," so §3.5.2 of the identity spec governs ADR-044 rather than the reverse, and the ADR's migration note contradicts its own upstream spec section. Open question OQ-11.

### 27.3.2 F2 — Trust attestation

**Domain separator:** `"SCP-ATTESTATION-V1:"`, passed to `canonical_hash` at `crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/attestation.rs:1220`.

**Consolidated** from the signed-structure table (§9.5.2 of the security spec):

| Order | Field | Encoding |
|-------|-------|----------|
| 1 | `id` | 4-byte BE length + UTF-8 bytes |
| 2 | `attestation_type` | 2-byte BE u16 (attestation type tag per `attestation_type_tag()`) |
| 3 | `issuer` | 4-byte BE length + UTF-8 bytes (DID) |
| 4 | `subject` | 4-byte BE length + UTF-8 bytes (DID) |
| 5 | `claim` | 4-byte BE length + UTF-8 bytes (RFC 8785 canonical JSON — see note) |
| 6 | `evidence` | 4-byte BE length + raw bytes if present, or `SHA-256(0x00)` sentinel if absent |
| 7 | `issued_at` | 8-byte BE u64 |
| 8 | `expires_at` | 8-byte BE u64 if present, or `SHA-256(0x00)` sentinel if absent |
| 9 | `revocation_status` | 4-byte BE length + MessagePack bytes of `RevocationStatus` enum |

`canonical_attestation_bytes` reproduces that table field for field (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/attestation.rs:1183`), serializing `claim` through the RFC 8785 JSON canonicalization helper `crate::jcs::to_vec`, serializing `evidence` and `revocation_status` through `rmp_serde::to_vec_named`, and returning the `canonical_hash` digest. The type tag comes from `attestation_type_tag` (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/mod.rs:416`), which pins eight stable values: `IdentityLink` 0, `CapabilityDelegation` 1, `OutletIntegrity` 2, `AgentCapability` 3, `Endorsement` 4, `RoleAssignment` 5, `ContextEndorsement` 6, `ParticipationWitness` 7.

**A ninth tag is already governed.** *Consolidated* from §13.5.3 of the versioning spec, Unknown Attestation Types (`.docs/specs/13-versioning-and-protocol-evolution.md:202`): "Unknown `attestation_type` tags (§9.5.2) MUST be preserved in storage and forwarding but ignored during trust evaluation. An SCP/1.0 implementation encountering an attestation with type tag `0x0010` (defined in SCP/1.2) stores it faithfully and passes it through, but does not factor it into trust scores. This ensures attestations are not lost during version transitions." Open question OQ-37, which asks whether F6 and F7 become new `AttestationType` variants, is bounded by that rule: §13.5.3 already fixes how an implementation of an earlier version treats a tag a later version adds.

**Two of the nine signed fields enter the preimage as MessagePack whose key order no artifact fixes.** §9.5.2 of the security spec fixes the container encoding for both — row 9 gives `revocation_status` as "4-byte BE length + MessagePack bytes of `RevocationStatus` enum" (`.docs/specs/09-security-model.md:441`), and its Attestation note gives `evidence` as "The `evidence` field, when present, is serialized as MessagePack bytes of the `AttestationEvidence` struct." (`.docs/specs/09-security-model.md:443`). Neither states a key order. The same §9.5.2 states, in its GovernanceProposal note, why that gap matters (`.docs/specs/09-security-model.md:528`): "MessagePack has no canonical form standard and field ordering varies by library; JSON serialization is more predictable across languages and has RFC 8785 (JCS) as a formal canonicalization standard." §9.5.1 states the property the gap defeats (`.docs/specs/09-security-model.md:341`): "two implementations that serialize the same logical data MUST produce identical bytes." *Derivation from shipped code:* `rmp_serde::to_vec_named` emits declaration order, and `AttestationEvidence` declares `evidence_type` then `data` (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/attestation.rs:130`–`:135`), which is not sorted order — the same divergence C29 records for F1, with no spec sentence to measure it against. §25.20 Vector 34 of the test-vector spec does not close the gap either: its input line reads "evidence: absent (use absent sentinel)", so the vector never exercises the evidence encoding. Open question OQ-46.

**Inside the signed scope:** the nine fields above.

**Outside the signed scope:** `signature`, `renewal_interval`, and `renewed_at`. **Consolidated** from §25.20 of the test-vector spec, which is the only artifact that states the exclusion (`.docs/specs/25-test-vectors.md:856`):

> The renewal fields (`renewal_interval`, `renewed_at`) are excluded from the signed preimage (§9.5.2) and do not appear below.

§9.5.2 of the security spec, the row that defines the signed field set, does not state the exclusion: its Attestation table lists nine fields and neither the table nor any other sentence of `.docs/specs/09-security-model.md` mentions `renewal_interval` or `renewed_at`. The purpose of the exclusion is stated only in shipped-code documentation, under the heading "# Authenticated field set (and what is intentionally excluded)" (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/attestation.rs:1131`): "The renewal fields [`Attestation::renewal_interval`] and [`Attestation::renewed_at`] are **deliberately excluded** so an attestation can be renewed without re-signing (`renewal.rs`)." The same doc comment states the consequence (`:1138`): those two fields are "**UNAUTHENTICATED / ADVISORY**," and the field's own documentation adds (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/attestation.rs:108`): "A holder/relay can bump it to make a stale attestation read as freshly renewed, and no verifier can detect the change from the signature."

**Contradiction C3 — §7.4.1 of the trust spec signs every field; §9.5.2 of the security spec signs nine.** §7.4.1 writes the envelope's signature as "Ed25519Signature (issuer's cryptographic signature over all fields except itself)" (`.docs/specs/07-trust-validation-and-capabilities.md:955`) and lists `renewed_at` among the envelope's fields (`:953`). §9.5.2 of the security spec tabulates nine fields and omits both renewal fields, and `canonical_attestation_bytes` reproduces the nine (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/attestation.rs:1183`). A signer built from §7.4.1 and a verifier built from §9.5.2 never agree on a byte. Under §9.5.2 a holder or relay rewrites `renewed_at` on a signed attestation undetectably, and `check_attestation_freshness` reads that field (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/attestation.rs:1009`). `.docs/audits/crypto-constructions-audit.md` carries the standing CRITICAL finding "[CRYPTO-12] Attestation Signature Input Not Canonicalized" (`:96`) against the same §7.4.1 envelope. Open question OQ-30.

**Contradiction C4 — §7.4.1's envelope names one field differently and omits another the code carries.** §7.4.1 of the trust spec writes the expiry field as `expires: u64?` (`.docs/specs/07-trust-validation-and-capabilities.md:952`); §9.5.2 of the security spec tabulates `expires_at`, and the shipped struct declares `pub expires_at: Option<u64>` with no serde rename (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/attestation.rs:78`). §7.4.1's envelope lists no `renewal_interval`; the shipped struct declares `pub renewal_interval: Option<Duration>` (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/attestation.rs:96`). §7.4.1 fixes the wire encoding — "Serialization: MessagePack, matching the SCP standard serialization format" (`:973`) — so a field-name divergence changes the bytes on the wire. Open question OQ-30.

**A numeric constraint binds the claim.** *Consolidated* from §9.5.2 of the security spec: "**Numeric constraint (I-JSON, RFC 7493):** numeric values within `claim` MUST be within the IEEE-754 double-precision exactly-representable integer range (|n| ≤ 2^53); larger identifiers (e.g. 64-bit snowflake IDs) MUST be string-encoded." The implementation does not reject an out-of-range integer, and its documentation says so and says why (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/attestation.rs:1183`, the function's doc comment): "This function does not reject out-of-range integers — the coercion is inherent to RFC 8785 and is pinned by test."

**Contradiction C5 — the spec types the id as a UUID; the code does not.** §7.4.1 of the trust spec writes the envelope field as `id: unique identifier (UUID v4)`. The shipped field is `pub id: String` with no format check in construction, canonicalization, or verification (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/attestation.rs:52`). The known-answer vector uses `"att-trust-001"` (§25.20 Vector 34 of the test-vector spec), which is not a UUID. Open question OQ-14.

**Contradiction C6 — the ADR names a type variant the code does not have.** ADR-017, the four-layer trust engine, lists the eighth `AttestationType` variant as `BehavioralWitness` (`.docs/adrs/phase-4.md:102`). §7.4.1 of the trust spec and the shipped enum both name it `ParticipationWitness`. Open question OQ-18.

### 27.3.3 F3 — Device attestation: no domain separator, no SCP construction

**There is no SCP signing construction for F3.** *Derivation from the shipped trait:* the type is `pub struct DeviceAttestationToken(Vec<u8>)` (`crates/scp-platform/src/traits.rs:239`), and the trait that produces it declares two methods and no preimage (`crates/scp-platform/src/traits.rs:795`):

**What the absent input costs.** *Derivation from a cited artifact:* §7.3.5 of the trust spec states "The primary Sybil resistance mechanism is the DeviceAttestation (§9.3), which binds DIDs to hardware-attested devices" (`.docs/specs/07-trust-validation-and-capabilities.md:737`), and the shipped `attest()` takes no DID, so a token it mints binds to no DID and an attacker who obtains any valid token files it under any DID. 2. The Android adapter's `attest(challenge, deviceId)` (`AndroidDeviceAttestation.kt:69`) does carry a `deviceId` into `clientDataJSON`, and a device id is not a DID. Its separate per-request assertion method, `assertRequest(requestHash)` (`:126`), passes an empty device id: `return attest(challenge = requestHash, deviceId = ByteArray(0))` (`:130`).
3. The only Rust code that files a token into a DID document calls `attest()` with no arguments and stores the result verbatim (`DhtDidMethod::attach_device_attestation`, `crates/scp-identity/src/dht.rs:1387`):

```rust

updated_doc.set_device_attestation_token(token.as_bytes());
```

The DID document's own BEP44 signature therefore proves that this DID's owner published these bytes; it does not prove that the platform issued them for this DID. Open questions OQ-2 and OQ-22.

**All three bridges fail closed rather than reaching the test adapter.** The only `scp_platform::DeviceAttestation` implementation in the workspace is `InMemoryDeviceAttestation`, whose tokens are the literal prefix `b"scp-test-attestation-v1:"` followed by a counter and whose `verify` returns true for any token carrying that prefix (`crates/scp-platform/src/testing/attestation.rs:13`). PyO3: `crates/scp-ffi/src/identity.rs:924` and `:2241` against `:971` and `:2299`. UniFFI: `identity_verify_device_attestation_impl` at `crates/scp-ffi/uniffi/src/bridge.rs:3986` against `:4023`. ### 27.3.4 F4 — Key-custody attestation: no domain separator, no construction

**There is no signing construction for F4, and the struct has no signature field.** *Derivation from the shipped struct* (`crates/scp-did/src/attestation.rs:41`): its five fields are `active_key_custody`, `agent_key_custody`, `platform`, `platform_attestation`, and `created_at`. There is no `signature`, no `issuer`, and no `subject`.

**What the artifact says.** ADR-039, the shared-DID human-agent identity model, is the only artifact in `.docs/specs/` or `.docs/adrs/` covering F4, and it covers it twice: in Enforcement Stack layer 4, quoted below (`.docs/adrs/phase-1.md:1302`), and in acceptance criterion 16, "`ScpKeyCustodyAttestation` type published in DID document service entries." (`:1385`). §27.1.3 names the three artifacts outside those two directories that also cover it:

> **Custody attestation.** At identity creation, the DID document includes a `ScpKeyCustodyAttestation` service entry declaring key custody model (`hardware-biometric` vs `software`) with optional platform attestation proof (Apple App Attest / Android Key Attestation). Unambiguous violations (Category A attempts with `#agent`, attestation mismatches with hardware proof) are permanently logged as `ScpCustodyViolationAttestation` records. DID owners can publish counter-attestations for reputation restoration. Absence of attestation is itself a signal.

**Serialization, not signing.** `to_service_entry` JSON-encodes the whole struct into a service entry's `serviceEndpoint` under type `"ScpKeyCustodyAttestation"` with fragment `#custody-attestation` (`crates/scp-did/src/attestation.rs:203`). `from_service_entry` reverses it. Neither hashes anything, and `serde_json::to_string` gives no key-ordering guarantee across implementations.

*Derivation:* F4's integrity therefore rests entirely on the DID document's BEP44 signature (§9.6.1 of the security spec). That signature covers the whole document, so it authenticates the custody declaration as the DID owner's. It cannot be presented apart from the document, and a second party cannot counter-sign a custody claim. Whether that is the intended design or an omission is open question OQ-3.

**The `platform_attestation` proof is opaque to SCP.** The type's documentation states the boundary (`crates/scp-did/src/attestation.rs:105`): "Contains opaque proof bytes from a platform attestation service. The proof format and verification procedure are platform-specific and outside the scope of the SCP protocol itself."

### 27.3.5 F5 — KeyPackage attestation

**Domain separator:** `"SCP-KEYPACKAGE-ATTESTATION-V1:"`, 30 ASCII bytes, at `crates/scp-mls/src/keypackage_attestation.rs:70`.

**Consolidated** from the signed-structure table (§9.5.2 of the security spec), abbreviating the long field notes:

| Order | Field | Encoding |
|-------|-------|----------|
| 1 | `did` | 4-byte BE length + UTF-8 bytes (the attested DID; MUST equal the leaf's `ScpCredential.did`) |
| 2 | `leaf_signature_key` | 32 bytes (raw Ed25519 public key — the MLS leaf `signature_key`) |
| 3 | `leaf_encryption_key` | 32 bytes (raw X25519 public key — the LeafNode ratchet-tree `encryption_key`) |
| 4 | `init_key` | 32 bytes (raw X25519 public key — the KeyPackage `init_key`). §9.5.2 fixes the value on a leaf that has no KeyPackage: "a bare LeafNode created without a KeyPackage — the group creator's leaf and every PCS-Update leaf (§9.7.1) — has exactly one HPKE key, its `encryption_key`, and no separate `init_key`; on such a leaf this field therefore carries `leaf_encryption_key`" |
| 5 | `wrapping_key` | 32 bytes (raw X25519 public key — the `scp_wrapping_key` (`0xFF01`) LeafNode extension value) |
| 6 | `signing_key_id` | 4-byte BE length + UTF-8 bytes (`#active` or `#agent`) |
| 7 | `issued_at` | 8-byte BE u64 (equals the leaf's `Lifetime.not_before`) |
| 8 | `expires_at` | 8-byte BE u64 (equals the leaf's `Lifetime.not_after`) |

`canonical_fields` builds exactly that eight-element array (`crates/scp-mls/src/keypackage_attestation.rs:205`), `signing_preimage` prepends the domain separator through `canonical_hash_bytes`, and `signing_hash` returns `SHA-256(signing_preimage())` (`crates/scp-mls/src/keypackage_attestation.rs:246`). The module states the signer's obligation without ambiguity (`crates/scp-mls/src/keypackage_attestation.rs:27`):

> The **only** signable output is [`signing_hash`]: the 32-byte `SHA-256(signing_preimage())` prehash. A later CRYPTO-22 slice's signer MUST compute the Ed25519 signature over that 32-byte hash — and over **nothing else**. It MUST NOT sign the raw `signing_preimage()` bytes, and it MUST NOT sign the [`to_extension_body`] output.

**Inside the signed scope:** all eight fields.

**Outside the signed scope:** the 64-byte `signature` itself, and — deliberately — any context identifier. §9.5.2 of the security spec states the reason: "The attestation is deliberately **context-agnostic**: it carries no `context_id`. A KeyPackage is a per-identity, pre-published pre-key bundle that must be mintable offline — before any group it will be added to is known — so a context scope is unknowable at mint time; group-scope binding is provided separately and redundantly by the `scp_context_params` GroupContext extension (`0xFF02`, §5.13.3)."

**The wire body is not the signed preimage.** *Consolidated* from §9.5.2 of the security spec: the `scp_keypackage_attestation` (`0xFF03`) LeafNode extension body is "the eight attestation fields, **in the same order as the signed preimage above**, followed by the raw 64-byte Ed25519 signature," and "the domain separator appears only in the signed preimage, never in the extension body." `to_extension_body` implements that by passing an empty domain to `canonical_hash_bytes` and appending the signature (`crates/scp-mls/src/keypackage_attestation.rs:264`).

**F5 is the only family with a byte-exact known-answer vector for both the preimage and the wire body.** §25.23 Vector 37 of the test-vector spec pins a 211-byte preimage, the hash `50cf61db5a97e0ddbd762de07e107684dfd0f00cfe53bad2750a70103ac38957`, a 64-byte signature, and a 245-byte extension body. The module reproduces the vector in its own tests (`crates/scp-mls/src/keypackage_attestation.rs:950`).

**Three mint sites.** *Consolidated* from §9.7.1 of the security spec (`.docs/specs/09-security-model.md:628`): "An attestation is minted at **every leaf-creation site — all three of them**: (1) group creation (`create_group`, where the creator's leaf is built directly with **no** published KeyPackage — the creator leaf still has a LeafNode `Lifetime`, just no `KeyPackage` wrapper), (2) add-time KeyPackage generation (the published pre-key bundle), and (3) every PCS Update, which generates a fresh ephemeral leaf key and therefore requires a fresh attestation over it (§9.7.3)."

**Contradiction C10 — ADR-057 names two mint sites; §9.7.1 of the security spec requires three.** ADR-057's 2026-08-01 amendment assigns the production signer for two of them: "Produced **once at KeyPackage generation**, and re-issued on leaf rotation (PCS Update, §9.7.3), via the identity custody `KeyCustody::sign`." Group creation is absent from that sentence. *Derivation:* an implementer who wires only the two sites ADR-057 names ships creator leaves carrying no attestation, and §9.7.1 states that "Attestation verification is MANDATORY and fail-closed for **all DIDs**: a leaf whose attestation is absent, malformed, expired, or failing any check above MUST be rejected." Open question OQ-17.

**F5 has no signer in production.** *Derivation:* every site in the workspace that produces an F5 signature sits inside a `#[cfg(test)]` module. There are four, each writing `att.signature = signer.sign(&att.signing_hash()).to_bytes()`: the `sign_in_place` helper (`crates/scp-mls/src/keypackage_attestation.rs:1299`), whose doc comment reads "The real signer is a later slice; this test helper stands in for it" (`:1296`); two further fixture sites in the same file (`:1749`, `:1789`); and one in the runtime verifier's tests (`crates/scp-runtime/src/crypto/mls/attestation_verification.rs:430`). The `#[cfg(test)]` markers that enclose them sit at `crates/scp-mls/src/keypackage_attestation.rs:931` and `:1654` and at `crates/scp-runtime/src/crypto/mls/attestation_verification.rs:255`. No production call site exists at any of the three mint sites §9.7.1 of the security spec requires. §27.4.5 gives the matching gap on the verify side, and open question OQ-17 records both.

**Outside the signed scope:** `signature` and **`platform_attestation`**. **No rule governs the `method` encoding.** §9.5.1's nine encoding rules name no enum and no discriminator, and §9.5.2 of the security spec fixes field encoding per struct in tables that §9.15 has no row in. §9.5.2 sanctions four different enum encodings inside a canonical preimage: `InnerEnvelope.message_type` as a "1-byte U8 discriminator (0x00=Content, 0x01=Signaling, 0x02=KeyDistribution)" (`.docs/specs/09-security-model.md:372`), `Attestation.attestation_type` as "2-byte BE u16 (attestation type tag per `attestation_type_tag()`)" (`:434`), `Attestation.revocation_status` as "4-byte BE length + MessagePack bytes of `RevocationStatus` enum" (`:441`), and `GovernanceProposal`'s `action_bytes` as "4-byte BE length + canonical JSON serialization of `GovernanceAction` (compact, no whitespace)" (`:525`). *Derivation from shipped code:* `signing_payload` writes a 4-byte big-endian length prefix followed by the `Display` output's UTF-8 bytes (`crates/scp-protocol/src/context/memory_scope.rs:356`), which is §9.5.1's variable-length-bytes rule applied literally and is a fifth shape. ## 27.4 Verification

### 27.4.1 F1 — Identity-link attestation

**What the spec requires.** §3.5.4 of the identity spec splits verification by class. For Class 1 (Cryptographic — the `Oauth` and `ChallengeResponse` methods):

> 1. Resolve the issuer's DID document. Extract the `#active` or `#agent` public key.
> 2. Verify the Ed25519 signature on the attestation envelope against the issuer's public key.
> 3. Check `revocation_status` is `Active`. If `Revoked`, reject.
> 4. Check `expires_at` (if present). If expired, reject.
> 5. Check freshness: if `evidence.verified_at` is older than the renewal interval for the verification method (§3.5.1), the attestation is stale. Stale attestations are degraded (reduced trust weight), not rejected outright.
> 6. **Trust the self-attestation.**

For Class 2 (Reference — the `SignedPost` and `DnsRecord` methods), the same section adds a proof fetch, a rule that a failed fetch leaves the attestation unverified, and a one-hour cache on success. §3.5.2 of the identity spec adds a further required check: "Verifiers check revocation by resolving the issuer's DID document and looking for an `AttestationRevocations` service endpoint (§18.2.2)."

**What the code checks.** `IdentityLinkAttestation::verify_signature(&self, public_key: &[u8])` performs **step 2 only** (`crates/scp-protocol/src/identity/attestation.rs:536`). It rebuilds the canonical bytes and calls `verify_ed25519_signature`. It checks no revocation state, no expiry, and no freshness.

**The verifier takes the public key from the caller.** *Derivation:* `verify_signature` accepts the key as a parameter and never resolves a DID. Every bridge propagates that shape to the SDK boundary. The PyO3 entry point documents the reason (`crates/scp-ffi/src/identity.rs:989`):

> The issuer's public key cannot be reliably extracted from the DID string because attestations are signed with `#active` or `#agent` keys (spec §3.5.2), not the `#0` identity key embedded in the DID.

and takes `issuer_public_key_hex: &str` from its caller (`crates/scp-ffi/src/identity.rs:1011`). The UniFFI bridge (`crates/scp-ffi/uniffi/src/bridge.rs:4335`) and the NAPI bridge (`crates/scp-ffi/napi/src/identity.rs:1387`) do the same. So step 1 of §3.5.4 — resolve the issuer's DID document and extract the key — has no implementation anywhere; the SDK caller supplies whatever key it likes, and a caller that supplies an attacker's key gets `true`. Open question OQ-12.

**The structural check runs at mint time and at no verifier.** `validate_structure` implements six checks — the type string is `"identity_link"`, `issuer` equals `subject`, `id` matches `compute_id`, `claim.link_type` is `"self_attestation"`, a `Revoked` status names the issuer as `revoked_by`, and `expires_at` exceeds `issued_at` (`crates/scp-protocol/src/identity/attestation.rs:611`). The shared F1 construction helper calls it before signing and rejects a non-empty error list with `AttestationBuildError::StructureValidation` (`crates/scp-ffi/common/src/attestation.rs:140`), and all three bridges route their mint path through that helper: `crates/scp-ffi/src/identity.rs:2391`, `crates/scp-ffi/uniffi/src/bridge.rs:4182`, and `crates/scp-ffi/napi/src/scp.rs:953`. *Derivation:* a workspace-wide search for `validate_structure` finds no other caller outside its own unit tests and the unrelated app-declaration type in `crates/scp-runtime/src/context/app_sandbox.rs`, so the six checks bind what a shipped SDK mints and bind nothing a shipped SDK verifies. A verifier that receives an F1 attestation from a third party runs none of them.

**Step 6 of §3.5.4 of the identity spec depends on one of those six checks.** That step reads: "**Trust the self-attestation.** Because issuer == subject, the DID key signature is sufficient." §3.5.2 of the identity spec states the same invariant only as a struct comment (`.docs/specs/03-identity.md:204`): "subject: DID, // Same as issuer (self-attestation)". `subject` sits inside the signed scope (§27.3.1). *Derivation:* an issuer DID A can therefore mint an attestation whose `subject` names a victim DID B, sign it with A's `#active`, and pass `verify_signature`, because the shipped verifier checks the signature alone; step 6's justification does not hold on that record, and a consumer reading `subject` attributes the platform handle to B. Open question OQ-39.

**The revocation endpoint has no implementation.** *Derivation:* the string `AttestationRevocations` appears in two files and four lines — `.docs/specs/03-identity.md:246` (§3.5.2) and `:373` (§3.5.6), and `.docs/specs/18-addressability-and-deployment.md:62` (the §18.2.2 service-endpoint table) and `:132` (that section's example document) — and in no Rust, Python, TypeScript, Swift, or Kotlin file. It appears in no line of `.docs/specs/07-trust-validation-and-capabilities.md`; the §18.2.2 table row points its Spec Reference column at §7.4.4 of the trust spec, which states the revocation duty without naming the endpoint. Open question OQ-16.

**The freshness inputs exist.** `is_time_expired`, `needs_renewal`, and `renewal_deadline_secs` compute the §3.5.4 step-5 judgment from the four renewal constants — 30 days for `Oauth`, 90 for `SignedPost`, 180 for `DnsRecord`, 60 for `ChallengeResponse` (`crates/scp-protocol/src/identity/attestation.rs:40`–`:57`). No verifier calls them.

### 27.4.2 F2 — Trust attestation

F2 has the most complete verifier of the eight families. `verify_attestation_with_revocation` runs five steps in order (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/attestation.rs:933`), which the function's own documentation numbers:

1. **Signature** — resolve the issuer's key through an injected `DidPublicKeyResolver`, rebuild `canonical_attestation_bytes`, and verify. A failure yields `TrustError::AttestationSignatureInvalid`.
2. **Evidence** — `validate_evidence` requires evidence for exactly two types, `OutletIntegrity` and `ParticipationWitness`, and requires a non-empty `evidence_type` string when evidence is present (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/attestation.rs:1250`).
3. **Expiry** — reject when `expires_at` is earlier than `clock.now_secs()`.
4. **Revocation field** — reject a `Revoked` status; reject first with `AttestationRevocationInvalid` when `revoked_by` differs from `issuer`, which enforces §7.4.1 of the trust spec's rule that "The `revoked_by` field MUST equal the attestation's `issuer`."
5. **External revocation** — query an optional injected `AttestationRevocationChecker`.

**Step 2 tests presence and a type string; it does not verify evidence.** *Derivation from shipped code:* `validate_evidence` rejects two conditions and no others — `attestation.evidence.is_none()` on the two types that require evidence, and `evidence.evidence_type.is_empty()` — and never reads `evidence.data`, which is declared `pub data: serde_json::Value` (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/attestation.rs:134`). So an `OutletIntegrity` attestation carrying `evidence_type: "x"` and `data: null` passes all five steps. §7.4.2 of the trust spec carries seven type bullets, and three of them state a `Verification:` procedure: capability delegation, "Verification: cryptographic chain validation" (`.docs/specs/07-trust-validation-and-capabilities.md:986`); outlet integrity, "Verification: deterministic testing (Layer 2)" (`:988`); and role assignment, "Verification: validate against governance model and UCAN chain" (`:994`). A fourth, identity link, states a verification sentence without a procedure — "Verification of the evidence is automated where possible" (`:984`). The remaining three — agent capability (`:990`), endorsement (`:992`), and context endorsement (`:996`) — state none; the endorsement bullet says why: "No objective evidence — the value comes from the issuer's own participation record and the attestation's accuracy history." No shipped component runs any of the three stated procedures. Open question OQ-40.

**The production resolver resolves a key, and it resolves the wrong one.** The production resolver, `IdentityDidPublicKeyResolver` (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/attestation.rs:682`), extracts the Ed25519 key embedded in a `did:dht:z...` string and states its own limit (`:673`): "This resolver does NOT perform full DID document resolution (DHT lookup, relay query, cache check)." *Derivation:* because it returns the `#0` identity key, an F2 attestation signed by `#active` or `#agent` fails step 1 under the production resolver — the same key-selection problem §27.4.1 records for F1, reached from the other side.

**Contradiction C17 — the resolver attributes a `#0`-signs-attestations rule to ADR-017, which does not state it, while two artifacts require `#active` or `#agent`.** The sentence after the one quoted above reads (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/attestation.rs:675`): "It extracts the identity key directly from the DID string, which is sufficient for attestation signature verification because the identity key (`#0`) is the canonical signing key for attestations per ADR-017." ADR-017, the four-layer trust engine, runs from `.docs/adrs/phase-4.md:26` to `:226` and contains none of the strings `#0`, `#active`, or `#agent`; its only signature sentence reads "Verifies Ed25519 signature against issuer's public key (resolved via DID)" (`:166`). §3.5.2 of the identity spec requires the other keys for an identity-link attestation (`.docs/specs/03-identity.md:220`): "signature: Ed25519Signature, // Signs §9.5.1 canonical hash (see Signature scope below), using issuer's #active or #agent key", and `AttestationType::IdentityLink` is tag 0 of the F2 enum (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/mod.rs:416`). ADR-039 places attestation issuance outside `#0`'s scope, and §9.7.1 of the security spec states it directly (`.docs/specs/09-security-model.md:628`): "The Identity Key (`#0`) is NOT used — `#0` is reserved exclusively for DID document operations and pre-rotation commitments (ADR-003, ADR-039); attestation issuance is a Category-B operational action, not a Category-A DID-document action." Open question OQ-31.

**Three `AttestationRevocationChecker` implementations ship, and two of them read a real list.** *Derivation from shipped code:*

1. `NoOpRevocationChecker` "always returns `None` (not revoked)" (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/attestation.rs:722`). Step 5 is inert on any path that supplies it.
2. `RevocationMapChecker` (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/aggregate.rs:151`) reads the context's persisted revocation list. `AttestationCache::get_verified_attestations` constructs it on every call (`:288`), and its doc comment states the purpose (`:142`): "Used by [`AttestationCache::get_verified_attestations`] so the READ path enforces context revocation, not only the ingest path: a cached attestation that is later context-revoked is dropped on read (even while still inside its cache TTL)."
3. `RevocationStateChecker` (`crates/scp-ffi/common/src/trust_store.rs:146`) reads the same list on the FFI ingest path. `verify_and_cache_attestations` constructs it (`:221`), and its doc comment states the checks it adds (`:206`): each entry is verified "against the RESOLVER-resolved issuer key, checks expiry, the issuer-bound `revocation_status` field, AND the context's external revocation list BEFORE caching."

Neither implementation 2 nor implementation 3 carries a `#[cfg(test)]` or a feature gate: the `#[cfg(test)]` markers in their files sit at `crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/aggregate.rs:618` and `crates/scp-ffi/common/src/trust_store.rs:370`, after both declarations and both construction sites. Both read the list through `TrustProtocolRepository::get_revocation_state`. *Derivation:* step 5 therefore bites on the two paths that build a checker from that list, and no artifact states that the context revocation list is the transport §7.4.4 of the trust spec means by "revocation endpoint," nor which component writes to it. §3.5.6 of the identity spec names what an unwritten list leaves open: "this does NOT prevent replay of the original `Active`-signed version — the original attestation remains valid until consumers check the revocation endpoint." Open question OQ-15.

**Freshness is a separate, advisory call.** `check_attestation_freshness` returns `Fresh`, `Stale { since }`, or `Expired` (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/attestation.rs:1009`), measuring from `renewed_at` when present and from `issued_at` otherwise. Both inputs sit outside the signed scope (§27.3.2), so the result is advisory. §7.3.6 of the trust spec sets the policy the function implements: "Attestations that lapse (exceed their renewal interval without re-verification) are not revoked — they are marked as stale."

**Threshold checking is a fourth entry point, and two spec sections disagree about whether the protocol enforces its result.** `check_threshold_attestation` counts attestations of one type across an attestor set and scores independence by penalizing shared context memberships and mutual endorsements (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/attestation.rs:1063`). §7.3.5 of the trust spec states that the protocol does not enforce the result (`.docs/specs/07-trust-validation-and-capabilities.md:735`): "Independence is verified by the consumer, not enforced by the protocol." §22.13.3 of the human-readable-addressing spec states the opposite for one named context (`.docs/specs/22-human-readable-addressing.md:1247`): "The Verified bootstrap context requires endorsement independence checking as part of its `ContextSybilPolicy::high_trust()` admission policy. This is a normative requirement. … Implementations MUST add this wiring." It goes on (`:1251`): "the admission flow MUST additionally invoke `check_threshold_attestation` on the Endorsements signal category when the policy requires endorsements." *Derivation from shipped code:* the code follows §22.13.3 — `RequiredSignal` carries the `threshold_requirement: Option<ThresholdRequirement>` field §22.13.3 mandates (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/sybil.rs:576`), and `evaluate_sybil_resistance` calls `check_endorsement_independence` (`:843`), which calls `check_threshold_attestation` (`:896`). So the protocol does enforce the independence result on that path, and §7.3.5's sentence does not hold over it. Open question OQ-43.

**`check_threshold_attestation` binds neither subject nor issuer, and the caller supplies both bindings.** *Derivation from shipped code:* the function's only filter over its `attestors` argument is the type test `a.attestation.as_ref().is_some_and(|att| &att.attestation_type == attestation_type)` (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/attestation.rs:1076`–`:1082`). It never compares an attestation's `subject` to the DID under assessment, never compares an attestation's `issuer` to the attestor presenting it, never deduplicates attestors, and never verifies a signature. `check_endorsement_independence` supplies all three preconditions before calling it: it filters on `att.subject == assessment.subject_did && att.issuer == a.did` and deduplicates by attestor DID (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/sybil.rs:886`–`:895`), and its comment names the two attacks the filters close (`:876`): "Without the subject check, an attacker could submit endorsement attestations for a different DID. Without the issuer check, an attacker could claim attestations issued by someone else." §22.13.3 of the human-readable-addressing spec states one of the three: its step 1a reads "Collect the endorsing DIDs from the candidate's endorsement attestations" (`.docs/specs/22-human-readable-addressing.md:1254`), which names the subject binding, and its steps 1b through 1d name neither the issuer binding nor a per-attestor deduplication nor a signature check (`:1255`–`:1257`). So an implementer wiring §22.13.3's `MUST` from those four steps clears an endorsement threshold with endorsements issued by a party other than the attestor presenting them, or with one endorsement submitted repeatedly. Open question OQ-47.

**Verification is cacheable by class, and one of the two TTLs matches no artifact.** `AttestationVerificationCache` keys on attestation id and expires entries by the class constants `REFERENCE_TTL_SECS` (3600) and `CRYPTOGRAPHIC_TTL_SECS` (86400) (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/attestation.rs:738`). §3.5.4 of the identity spec defines one cache and scopes it to Reference attestations (`.docs/specs/03-identity.md:296`): "Consumer-side. Each consumer maintains its own cache of Reference attestation verification results. - TTL: 1 hour. … - Cache key: attestation ID." The same list closes "Class 1 attestations do not require caching — DID signature verification is deterministic and fast." *Derivation:* the 3600-second Reference constant matches that TTL and the 86400-second Cryptographic constant matches no artifact.

**The cache stores a verification result under a key revocation does not change.** The entry type is `Option<&Result<(), TrustError>>`, returned by `get(&self, attestation_id: &str, now: u64)` (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/attestation.rs:808`), so a cached positive outcome survives for its TTL. For F1 the id stays stable across revocation: `compute_id` takes `issuer`, `claim.platform`, `claim.platform_handle`, and `issued_at` (`crates/scp-protocol/src/identity/attestation.rs:493`), none of which the issuer changes when republishing with `revocation_status: Revoked`. §7.4.4 of the trust spec states the duty that bears on this (`.docs/specs/07-trust-validation-and-capabilities.md:1010`): "Agents that cached a previous verification SHOULD re-check on a defined interval (RECOMMENDED: at least once per hour for security-critical attestations, once per day for others). A revoked attestation MUST NOT be accepted by validators for any purpose." No component invalidates a cache entry on revocation, and a workspace search for `AttestationVerificationCache` finds the type only in its own declaration (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/attestation.rs:765`) and its own unit tests, so no shipped path caches a verification result today. Open question OQ-41.

### 27.4.3 F3 — Device attestation

**The verifier checks a prefix, and the exported verifier does not reach the trait the platform adapters implement.** `scp_platform::DeviceAttestation::verify` returns `bool` and receives only the token (§27.3.3). Every SDK consumer reaches that method through a free function rather than through a provider object: the UniFFI bridge exports `identity_verify_device_attestation(did, token_base64)` (`crates/scp-ffi/uniffi/src/bridge.rs:4303`), the Swift SDK wraps it as `identityVerifyDeviceAttestation(did:tokenBase64:)` (`bindings/swift/Sources/SCP/Internal/ScpBindings.swift:16654`), and the Kotlin SDK wraps it at `bindings/kotlin/scp-kt/src/main/kotlin/works/limn/scp/Identity.kt:120`. `DeviceAttestationProvider`, the trait both platform adapters implement, declares `attest` and `assert_request` and no verification method (`crates/scp-ffi/uniffi/src/lib.rs:556`), and the exported free function never consults it. The `#[cfg(feature = "testing")]` arm of that free function constructs `InMemoryDeviceAttestation` instead (`crates/scp-ffi/uniffi/src/bridge.rs:3986`), and its shipped arm returns `IDENT_1016` (`:4023`). *Derivation from shipped code:* a Swift or Kotlin consumer therefore calls a verifier that, on a shipped build, verifies nothing and says so, and that on a `testing` build tests a prefix against a token no platform adapter produced. The single `DeviceAttestation` implementation compares against a literal (`crates/scp-platform/src/testing/attestation.rs:64`):

```rust
let has_prefix = token.as_bytes().starts_with(SYNTHETIC_ATTESTATION_PREFIX);
```

where the prefix is `b"scp-test-attestation-v1:"`. *Derivation:* the whole verifier is that prefix test, every FFI call path that reaches it is `#[cfg(feature = "testing")]` on all three bridges, and the shipped variants return the typed errors `IDENT_1015` and `IDENT_1016` instead (§27.3.3), so a shipped build performs no device-attestation verification and says so to its caller.

**The conformance macro asserts a contract the trait cannot express.** §16.12.4 of the test-infrastructure spec generates `attest_verify_roundtrip` and `invalid_token_rejected` (`crates/scp-testing/src/conformance/attestation.rs:41`). Neither asserts a binding to a DID, because the trait signature admits none.

**A second shipped consumer gates on the signal without reading a token.** `evaluate_sybil_resistance(assessment, policy, current_time, attestors)` enforces a per-context policy flag by testing for the presence of a map key (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/sybil.rs:772`):

```rust
// 1. Check device attestation requirement.
if policy.require_device_attestation
    && !assessment
        .signals
        .contains_key(&TrustSignalCategory::DeviceAttestation)
{
    return Err(SybilResistanceError::DeviceAttestationRequired);

```

The signal it tests is caller-supplied and is documented as a count, not a token (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/sybil.rs:118`): "- `DeviceAttestation`: number of distinct device attestation tokens", under a struct doc that reads "Each signal is a verifiable fact about a DID — the protocol provides the data, agents evaluate the weight" (`:105`). *Derivation:* the gate does not fail closed. It admits a DID on a caller-asserted map key, with no token, no signature, and no DID binding — so `require_device_attestation` gates on the caller's own assertion rather than on a platform attestation. Open question OQ-2.

**The same function does enforce the other §22.13.3 requirement.** §22.13.3 of the human-readable-addressing spec requires `evaluate_sybil_resistance` to invoke `check_threshold_attestation` on the Endorsements signal category, and the shipped function does so through `check_endorsement_independence` (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/sybil.rs:843`). *Derivation:* the two policy flags on one function therefore behave differently — the endorsement-independence flag runs a real independence computation over attestor data, and `require_device_attestation` runs a map-key presence test. §27.4.2 records the spec-versus-spec disagreement §22.13.3 opens with §7.3.5 of the trust spec, and open question OQ-43 carries it.

**No artifact states what a production verifier checks.** §9.3 of the security spec repositions the family rather than specifying it (`.docs/specs/09-security-model.md:187`): "Device attestation (Apple App Attest, Google Play Integrity) is an optional SDK-level trust signal, not a protocol-level uniqueness gate." The same paragraph states what an unsupported device produces: "Its absence is expected — desktop users, non-native clients, protocol-only implementations — and is not penalizing." The next paragraph states the platform scope (`:189`): "**Desktop gap acknowledged.** macOS, Linux, and Windows have no App Attest or Play Integrity equivalent." ADR-025 and ADR-027 describe the platform-side mint. Neither states the checks a relying party runs on a token it reads out of someone else's DID document. Open question OQ-2.

### 27.4.5 F5 — KeyPackage attestation

**Twelve of the thirteen checks are implemented in a form that can reject; check 2 is not.** §9.7.1 of the security spec enumerates thirteen checks. Checks 1 and 3 through 13 are implemented across two layers, each returning a typed error on failure. Check 2 is implemented against the clock at the resolve call rather than against the age of the document the resolver served.

**The checks run on leaf introduction or change, and on nothing else.** *Consolidated* from §9.7.1 of the security spec, Verification (MUST) — when it runs (`.docs/specs/09-security-model.md:630`):

> Attestation verification is triggered by **leaf introduction or change**, never by mere message arrival. A member verifies a leaf's KeyPackage attestation when — and only when — that leaf **enters or is replaced in** the group: (a) at an **Add**, the KeyPackage introducing a new member's leaf, and (b) at an **Update / Commit-with-UpdatePath**, an existing member replacing their own leaf with a fresh ephemeral key (§9.7.3). A **Commit or Proposal that does not introduce or change the committer's leaf carries no new attestation to check** — the leaf and its embedded attestation are byte-for-byte the ones already fully verified when that leaf was introduced — so the verifier **MUST NOT** re-run attestation verification, and in particular **MUST NOT** re-resolve the committer's DID, for it.

The same paragraph states what re-running costs: "gating every steady-state Commit on a fresh DID resolution would let an attacker who degrades one member's DID resolution — a DHT eclipse of their `did:dht` record, or a `did:web` DoS — get that member's Commits rejected group-wide, forking the epoch or censoring the member." The thirteen checks below are the checks that run on a triggering event, not the whole verifier contract.

*Checks 3 through 13 — the pure core.* `verify_attestation(attestation, ctx)` (`crates/scp-mls/src/keypackage_attestation.rs:567`) is deterministic, performs no input or output, and returns one typed variant per failing check. In evaluation order — the What-it-verifies column consolidates §9.7.1 of the security spec's numbered checks, and the Error-variant column names the shipped `AttestationVerifyError` variants (`crates/scp-mls/src/keypackage_attestation.rs:393`):

| Check | What it verifies | Error variant |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | Ed25519 signature over `signing_hash()` against the resolved current verification method | `SignatureInvalid` |
| 4 | `leaf_signature_key` equals the leaf's actual `signature_key` | `LeafSignatureKeyMismatch` |
| 5 | `leaf_encryption_key` equals the leaf's ratchet-tree `encryption_key` | `LeafEncryptionKeyMismatch` |
| 6 | `wrapping_key` equals the leaf's `scp_wrapping_key` (`0xFF01`) extension value | `WrappingKeyMismatch` |
| 7 (Add only) | `init_key` equals the KeyPackage's `init_key` | `InitKeyMismatch` |
| 8 (Add only) | the KeyPackage's `init_key` differs from its `encryption_key` | `InitKeyEqualsEncryptionKey` |
| 9 | `did` equals the leaf credential's DID | `DidMismatch` |
| 10 | `signing_key_id` equals the leaf credential's `signing_key_id` | `SigningKeyIdMismatch` |
| 11 | `[issued_at, expires_at]` equals the leaf `Lifetime` `[not_before, not_after]` | `LifetimeWindowMismatch` |
| 13a | `expires_at` strictly exceeds `issued_at` | `ExpiresNotAfterIssued` |
| 12 | `expires_at - issued_at` does not exceed 7,261,200 seconds | `LifetimeTooLong` |
| 13b | `now` does not exceed `expires_at` | `Expired` |
| 13c | `issued_at` leads `now` by at most 300 seconds | `IssuedInFuture` |

Check 13a runs before check 12 because it is what keeps check 12's subtraction from underflowing on an attacker-supplied attestation; the code states the dependency in both comments (`crates/scp-mls/src/keypackage_attestation.rs:638`): "Check 13a: strictly-positive window (also makes the check-12 subtraction underflow-safe)" and "Check 12: lifetime cap. `expires_at > issued_at` above ⇒ no underflow." An implementer who runs 12 first returns `LifetimeTooLong` where the shipped core returns `ExpiresNotAfterIssued`, and computes `expires_at - issued_at` on an unchecked window.

*Checks 1 and 2 — the resolution seam.* `verify_attestation_with_resolution` (`crates/scp-mls/src/keypackage_attestation.rs:782`) enforces check 2 first — the resolving DID document is no older than `MAX_ATTESTATION_KEY_RESOLUTION_STALENESS`, 300 seconds — then check 1, that the credential's `signing_key_id` names a current `#active` or `#agent` verification method, and only then delegates checks 3 through 13. The ordering is deliberate; the module states why: check 2 runs "**before** check 1 and before the pure checks 3–13, so a stale document can never mask a later failure."

*The Add-only gate is structural.* Checks 7 and 8 run when and only when `ctx.trigger` is `AttestationTrigger::Add { kp_init_key }`. §9.7.1 of the security spec requires exactly that: "A verifier MUST NOT decide whether to run checks 7–8 by comparing the attestation's `init_key` to its `leaf_encryption_key`." The `kp_init_key` rides inside the `Add` variant, so an `Add` without an `init_key` cannot be constructed.

**Check 2 cannot reject a cache-served document.** *Derivation from the runtime seam:* the layer that supplies check 2's input stamps `let resolved_at = clock.now_secs();` immediately before `resolver.resolve(signer_did).await` (`crates/scp-runtime/src/crypto/mls/attestation_verification.rs:217`), so `now - resolved_at` is approximately zero on every successful resolution, including one the resolver satisfies from the §9.10.7 privacy cache. The module states the gap (`:46`): "Resolver-side privacy-cache freshness enforcement — that a document served from the §9.10.7 24h/7d cache and older than `MAX_ATTESTATION_KEY_RESOLUTION_STALENESS` MUST force a fresh re-resolution — is the boundary-stable **SCP-CRYPTO22-005** follow-on". The upstream story states the consequence (`.docs/prds/keypackage-attestation.json`, story SCP-CRYPTO22-004): "Layer A's §9.7.1 check-2 300s gate is vacuous in production and cannot reject a cache-served document up to 24h stale. Wiring this live before 005 would let a rotated-away/#retired-* key still present in a ≤24h privacy-cache-served document pass check 1, silently weakening §9.12 rotation-is-revocation on the Add branch — and a cache HIT is a resolution SUCCESS, so the AC6 Add fail-closed policy does NOT cover it." §9.7.1 states the requirement the seam does not meet (`.docs/specs/09-security-model.md:635`): "A resolver-cache entry older than this bound MUST NOT be used for the current-key check — it MUST trigger a **fresh** resolution". Open question OQ-17.

*The runtime layer resolves the DID and fails closed on Add.* `verify_add_attestation` (`crates/scp-runtime/src/crypto/mls/attestation_verification.rs:191`) reads the injected clock immediately before the resolve call, resolves through the injected `DidResolver`, and rejects on both `Err` and `Ok(None)` with no cache fallback — §9.7.1 of the security spec's Add policy: "A resolution failure on an Add is a **REJECT** (fail-closed), never accept-if-uncertain, and the verifier **MUST NOT fall back to a stale or pre-rotation cached DID document**." The `did:test:` and `did:key:` carve-out is `#[cfg(any(test, feature = "testing"))]`, so a shipped build resolves every DID.

**The complete verifier has no production caller, and no leaf carries the extension.** *Derivation from four searches of the workspace:*

1. `verify_add_attestation` appears only in its own module and that module's tests. No `ContextManager`, `Supervisor`, or pipeline code calls it. The module says so itself (`crates/scp-runtime/src/crypto/mls/attestation_verification.rs:55`): "This function is NOT yet wired into the `ContextManager` / `Supervisor` / pipeline — that live Add-path wiring is S6/S7."
2. `make_attestation_extension` — the only function that builds an `Extension::Unknown(0xFF03, ...)` — has no caller outside its own module's tests (`crates/scp-mls/src/keypackage_attestation.rs:1215`, inside the `#[cfg(test)]` block opened at `:931`).
3. `scp_capabilities_with_keypackage_attestation` is re-exported from `crates/scp-mls/src/lib.rs:73` and has no caller outside its own module's tests (`crates/scp-mls/src/keypackage_attestation.rs:1250`). Group and leaf construction in `crates/scp-mls/src/group.rs` uses `scp_capabilities_with_context_params` (`:510`) and `scp_capabilities_with_wrapping_key` (`:451`, `:1080`) instead, so no leaf declares support for extension type `0xFF03`.
4. No production code signs an F5 attestation (§27.3.5).

So §9.7.1 of the security spec's "Attestation verification is MANDATORY and fail-closed for **all DIDs**" holds vacuously: no leaf in the shipped tree carries an attestation to verify. The pure layer implements checks 3 through 13 and the byte-exact known-answer vector pins its bytes; check 2 cannot reject a cache-served document; and nothing calls either layer, so no check rejects anything a shipped build processes. Open question OQ-17.

**The Update grace path is absent by construction.** §9.7.1 of the security spec grants an already-admitted member's Update "a bounded last-known-good grace on a transient resolution failure." The runtime seam accepts only `AttestationAddGroundTruth`, which carries no trigger field, so an Update cannot be routed through it. The module states the reasoning (`crates/scp-runtime/src/crypto/mls/attestation_verification.rs:162`): routing Update through a fail-closed-only seam "would let a transient resolver outage hard-reject a legitimate member's Update — a censorship/liveness regression." The same module records the branch as deferred (`crates/scp-runtime/src/crypto/mls/attestation_verification.rs:160`): the Update last-known-good grace "is deferred to **S7**", and (`:50`) "until it lands, an Update whose resolution fails also fails closed here". *Derivation:* §9.7.1 states the grace as a requirement — an Update on a transient resolution failure "uses the member's **last-known-good** DID document … **instead of** hard-rejecting, so a transient DID-resolution outage cannot fork the epoch or censor an existing member" — and no component implements it, so a §9.7.1 requirement has no owner. Open question OQ-36.

### 27.4.6 F6 — Key-destruction attestation

**Ruling (2026-08-25) — a destruction attestation declaring a hardware-backed method binds a consumer only when a verified platform attestation proof accompanies it.** Alec ruled that a key-destruction attestation declaring a hardware-backed method reads as software-backed unless a verified platform attestation proof accompanies it. This section states his ruling and does not quote him, because the corpus holds no record of the words he used. He reached it in two steps, and both steps bind the clauses below. First he established that a key sits in hardware or in software, and that either is a legitimate choice. Then, asked whether declaring the hardware method earns a publisher more than declaring the software method earns it, he answered that it earns more. *Derivation:* a claim that earns its publisher more requires backing, which is the step that carries his answer into the four clauses below.

Four clauses state that ruling and add nothing to it. They govern every claim that a member destroyed context key material by a hardware-backed method, wherever a participant publishes that claim for another participant to read. Today the corpus carries that claim in `method` of `PublishableKeyDestructionAttestation`, and the clauses reach any later field carrying the same claim.

1. A consumer reads `method` as `HardwareBacked` only when a verification of a `platform_attestation` the same record carries returns a pass.
2. In every case clause 1 does not admit, the consumer reads the method as `SoftwareOnly`, and the declared enum value changes nothing about that reading. Four cases a reader meets, given as instances and not as the test: the record carries no `platform_attestation`; no verifier runs for the proof's platform; a verification returns a reject; a verification reaches no verdict.
3. A declaration of `SoftwareOnly` requires no proof, and a consumer reads it as `SoftwareOnly`.
4. Every consumer of a destruction method reads the method clauses 1 through 3 produce, and no consumer reads the declared enum value in place of it. The trust level §9.15 of the security spec grants, an admission or governance decision, a diagnostic report, and a value displayed to a user are instances of a consumer, not the test for one.

*Derivation:* clause 1 conditions a hardware reading on a verified proof, and §27.3.6 shows that the signing preimage does not cover `platform_attestation`. Contradiction C34 in that subsection states the consequence: the signature binds the declaration and leaves the proof detachable, so a verification of the proof establishes nothing about the declaration it arrived beside, and clause 1 cannot admit a hardware reading on the shipped record however that verification turns out. Open question OQ-8 asks a human whether `platform_attestation` enters the signed scope. Clause 1 waits on that answer as well as on the verifier questions OQ-2 and OQ-29 carry, and this section designs neither the scope nor the verifier.

*Derivation:* clause 1 says a verification returns a pass and no artifact states who runs that verification. The encryption-as-access-control tenet of `CLAUDE.md` states the protocol's answer for records generally — "clients verify every record independently, so correctness never depends on a relay" — from which a consumer that accepts a relay's or another party's assertion of a pass reads a method it did not verify. §9.15 of the security spec publishes F6 records to relays, so the relay that serves a record is the party a consumer would most readily take a pass from. Open question OQ-2 asks a human what a relying party checks on a platform proof, and it owns the question of who checks it.

*Derivation:* clause 1 also says nothing about what a passing verification establishes, and three properties decide whether a pass means anything for the declaration it accompanies. Whether the proof was issued to the DID that published the record: §9.15 of the security spec publishes F6 records to relays outside the closed context, so a proof carrying no DID is readable out of one published record and writable into another. Whether the proof covers the key material whose destruction the record claims: §27.3.6 records that no key identifier enters F6's payload, and the audit file `.docs/audits/adr-audit-phase-4-6.md` carries the same gap as the standing finding "[ADR-018] M5: Key Destruction Attestation Not Linked to Specific Keys" (`:184`). Whether the proof reports a destruction rather than some other platform fact: §9.15 asks the SDK to request "a destruction attestation from the hardware after deleting key material," while §9.3 of the security spec introduces Apple App Attest and Google Play Integrity for a device claim and ADR-039's Enforcement Stack layer 4 introduces Apple App Attest and Android Key Attestation for a custody claim, and no artifact states which platform call produces F6's proof. §27.6 records that F3, F4, and F6 model those platform artifacts as three separate types. No artifact states any of the three as a requirement. Open questions OQ-2 and OQ-29 own the verification procedure itself.

*Derivation:* clause 4 addresses every consumer, and a rule addressed to every future caller holds only while every caller remembers it. The mechanical-enforcement tenet of `CLAUDE.md` — "Enforce mechanically. Linters, structural tests, and the type system — not documentation" — points at a read path that returns what clauses 1 through 3 produce. *Derivation from the shipped type:* `PublishableKeyDestructionAttestation::verified_method` is that read path, the declared `method` field carries `pub(crate)` visibility, and `PublishableKeyDestructionAttestation::new` takes the declaration from its caller and exposes no public accessor for it (`crates/scp-protocol/src/context/memory_scope.rs`). Which further read paths a later bridge or SDK exposes is an API decision no artifact makes.

**The ruling decides what a written declaration is worth, and it decides nothing about an absent record.** §9.15 of the security spec states what an absent record means: "**No attestation** (member went offline before close): No confidence. The member may still have the key." The four clauses decide what a written hardware declaration is worth. They decide nothing about a member who published no destruction attestation, and they leave §9.15's third trust level with the meaning it already had.

**Two destruction-method values fall outside the clauses, and each falls outside because a member observes it rather than declaring it to a second party.**

- `KeyDestructionAttestation.level`, the internal close-event record ADR-018 acceptance criterion 7 describes. `finalize_close` sets it from the observed `DisposalOutcome`, and the comment beside the assignment states the reason (`crates/scp-runtime/src/context/ttl_close_helpers.rs:947`): "`dispose_secrets` zeroizes the sender key (`ZeroizeOnDrop`) and frees the MLS group in software; no hardware attestation is wired on this path, so `SoftwareOnly` is the honest level." *Derivation:* `finalize_close` returns the record to `handle_finalize_close` for logging, and the same module states that recording it into the event log or the `ContextClosed` leaf is a separate wire-format decision that path does not take, so no second party reads it today. The clauses reach it on the day a member publishes it, because clause 1 governs the claim and not the struct that carries it. ADR-018's Rationale already states that the level travels — "higher assurance levels are visible to other participants" — and the Amendment (2026-08-25) at the end of ADR-018 records what the ruling costs that sentence.
- The platform adapters' own `DestructionAttestation`, carrying a `method` and a `confirmed` flag, declared at `bindings/swift/Sources/SCP/Platform/AppleKeyCustody.swift:116` and `bindings/kotlin/scp-kt-android/src/main/kotlin/works/limn/scp/android/platform/Types.kt:84`. Each reports what the adapter itself did to an identity key on the device it runs on: the Apple adapter returns `.softwareOnly` because Keychain holds the key material, and the Android adapter returns `HARDWARE` for a key the Keystore TEE held and `SOFTWARE_ONLY` for a key it did not. *Derivation:* a search of `bindings/` and `crates/` for the type name finds the two declarations, their `destroyKey` producers, and the Kotlin unit tests, and no bridge export and no serialization carries either value off the device. Each is a local observation rather than a claim a second party reads, and the clauses reach either one on the day something publishes it.

**The four clauses read the same today and after a platform verifier lands.** No SCP implementation verifies an F6 `platform_attestation` today, and no artifact states the checks such a verifier would run (open questions OQ-2 and OQ-29), so clause 2 covers every hardware declaration and every consumer reads `SoftwareOnly`. Clause 1 conditions on a verification returning a pass, not on a verifier existing, so a verifier landing changes which declarations clause 1 admits and rewrites no clause. Two answers decide whether clause 1 ever admits one: what a verifier checks, which open questions OQ-2 and OQ-29 and the three properties above own, and whether the proof enters the signed scope, which open question OQ-8 owns.

**What the opposite reading would ship.** A consumer that read the declared enum value would give §9.15's High confidence to a claim nothing checks, so a member would raise how much others trust its destruction by writing `HardwareBacked` into a field no code reads back against a proof, and §9.15 grants that rating over key material the member may still hold. The no-dev-stand-in tenet of `CLAUDE.md` states what such a reading costs: "Masking a missing production backend with a dev construct ships a *false guarantee*, which is strictly worse than the capability being honestly absent (absence is detectable; a nullifier lies)." Reading an unproven hardware declaration as `SoftwareOnly` keeps the missing verifier detectable, because the declaration then buys its writer nothing.

**There is no verifier.** *Derivation:* `PublishableKeyDestructionAttestation` exposes four methods: `new`, `verified_method`, `signing_payload` (`crates/scp-protocol/src/context/memory_scope.rs:328`), and `has_valid_signature_length`, which tests `self.signature.len() == 64`. `verified_method` applies clauses 1 through 3 above to the declared value, and no function verifies the signature. A workspace-wide search finds the type name only inside `crates/scp-protocol/src/context/memory_scope.rs` and its unit tests: nothing constructs one outside those tests, nothing signs one, nothing publishes one, and nothing reads one. Under clause 2 above, a record that did reach a consumer would still give no hardware reading: the consumer reads `SoftwareOnly`.

**What the spec expects.** §9.15 of the security spec states the flow: "Attestations are published to relays (outside the now-destroyed context). They are signed by the identity key so they remain verifiable after context keys are destroyed." That section also states that its three trust levels rate the method a consumer verifies rather than the method a record declares, which is the ruling above carried into the section that owns F6. No artifact states the verifier's checks. ADR-018, context TTL and memory-scope enforcement (`.docs/adrs/phase-4.md:227`), covers key destruction in acceptance criteria 5 through 7 and never mentions signing or publishing an attestation; its criterion 7 says only that "Verification level is metadata recorded in the close event — not a gate."

*Derivation:* the record that survives close is `KeyDestructionAttestation` — the internal, unsigned type ADR-018 criterion 7 describes (`crates/scp-protocol/src/context/memory_scope.rs:144`), built inline from the observed disposal outcome at the actor's finalize seam per the deletion note at `crates/scp-protocol/src/context/memory_scope.rs:393`. **Contradiction C19 — §9.15 gives its signed record the name the code gives an unsigned one.** §9.15 of the security spec specifies the record as `KeyDestructionAttestation { contextID, memberDID, destroyedAt, platformAttestation, method, signature }` (`.docs/specs/09-security-model.md:1380`). ### 27.4.8 F8 — Participation profile

**The verifier runs in three steps and is wired to production.** `verify_participation_requirements(current_time, expected_subject, requirements, statements)` (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/participation.rs:1000`):

- **Step 0 — subject binding.** Discard every statement whose signed `subject_did` differs from `expected_subject`. An empty `expected_subject` is rejected up front as invalid input, not treated as a wildcard. §7.4.1 of the trust spec states the property this closes: "**Subject binding IS enforced by the protocol, not delegated to the consumer:** both siblings take the DID of the agent being admitted (`expected_subject` / `subject_did`) and discard any profile or challenge result whose signed subject does not match, so a victim's genuine high-standing profiles cannot be replayed to admit a different agent."
- **Step 1 — signature.** Verify every surviving statement's Ed25519 signature over `signable_bytes` against its own `signer_public_key`, using `verify_strict`. Any failure aborts the whole call.
- **Step 2 — per-requirement evaluation.** For each `RequireParticipation`, drop statements whose `updated_at` leads the verifier's clock by more than `MAX_PARTICIPATION_FUTURE_SKEW_SECS` (300 seconds) or trails it by more than `max_age_secs`, extract the requested fact, test the threshold, and count distinct `signer_public_key` values against `min_contexts`.

**The sibling §7.4.1 names is `ChallengeVerification`, and it binds a context id where F8 omits one.** §7.3.4 of the trust spec defines it (`.docs/specs/07-trust-validation-and-capabilities.md:437`): "**ChallengeVerification record format.** A signed record proving that a specific verifier tested a capability and the agent passed:" — a record carrying `verifier_did`, `subject_did`, `capability_uri`, `expires_at`, an optional `context_id`, and a `verifier_signature`. §9.18.2 of the security spec registers its separator (`.docs/specs/09-security-model.md:1787`): "| `"SCP-CHALLENGE-VERIFY-V1:"` | Trust challenge verification signing | §7.4 |". The shipped type is `ChallengeVerification` (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/challenge.rs:305`) with `DOMAIN_CHALLENGE_VERIFY_V1` (`:391`), `canonical_challenge_verification_bytes` (`:509`), and `verify_challenge_verification` (`:874`). §7.3.4.4 of the trust spec, Context Admission via Capability URIs, requires the admitting context to check both bindings (`.docs/specs/07-trust-validation-and-capabilities.md:716`): "every candidate `ChallengeVerification`'s signed `subject_did` equals the DID of the agent being admitted AND its signed `context_id` equals the context being admitted to". *Derivation:* F8 binds only the subject, because §7.3.2.1 of the trust spec makes the absent `context_id` the privacy guarantee (§27.2.2), so the two siblings differ on the second binding. `ChallengeVerification` exhibits all three of §27.1.2's indicators, and §27 does not gather it; open question OQ-1 asks a human whether it belongs to this surface.

**A verified signature is not an authorization.** §7.4.1 of the trust spec states the limit plainly: "the `signer_public_key` is self-certifying: a subject can mint genuinely-signed profiles from signers it controls (the colluding-contexts forgery of §7.3.2.1) … a consumer MUST establish it SEPARATELY — e.g. a context-membership proof, a trusted-signer set, or the threshold/independence path (§7.3.5) — and MUST NOT treat a passing signature check as an authorization decision."

**Two facts in the profile cannot be verified against `event_log_root`.** §7.3.2.1 of the trust spec says so in the struct's own field comments: `attestation_count` is a "credential-layer count (§7.4) … NOT a context-event count, NOT verifiable against `event_log_root`," and `outlet_invocation_count_anchored` is "false until ADR-051: derived from local ContextEvents, not the Merkle log — consumers MUST NOT treat as Merkle-proven." The shipped struct states the same two limits in different words (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/participation.rs:714`–`:719` and `:728`–`:730`).

**Contradiction C22 — `min_contexts` counts signer keys, and §7.3.2.1 of the trust spec mandates a window in which one context presents two.** Step 2 counts raw keys: `distinct_signers.insert(statement.signer_public_key);` over caller-supplied statements (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/participation.rs:1071`, over the `statements: &[ParticipationProfile]` parameter at `:1004`). §7.3.2.1 of the trust spec defines a governance action that changes a context's signer key and requires verifiers to honour both keys for thirty days (`.docs/specs/07-trust-validation-and-capabilities.md:280`): "The `participation_signing_seed` itself can be rotated independently of admin rotation via the `RotateParticipationKey` governance action. … Verifiers who cached the old public key MUST accept both old and new keys for a 30-day overlap period (matching the statement `max_age_secs` default). … Historical statements signed with the old key remain valid if their `updated_at` predates the rotation event." *Derivation:* the shipped verifier knows nothing of rotation, so during the mandated overlap a subject who keeps the pre-rotation profile and adds the post-rotation one presents two distinct signer keys from one context and clears `min_contexts = 2`. Open question OQ-32.

## 27.6 Cross-family dependencies

*Every edge below is a derivation from a cited artifact or from shipped code, and each states its own basis.*

**F1 → F2: the identity-link claim has two incompatible wire formats.** F1 is a standalone struct with a MessagePack `claim` and its own domain separator. F2 carries `AttestationType::IdentityLink` (tag 0) with a JSON `claim`, and a typed helper, `IdentityLinkClaim`, converts a four-field struct — `platform`, `platform_handle`, `verification_method`, `proof_url` — to and from `serde_json::Value` (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/attestation.rs:154`). §3.5.2 of the identity spec says F1 "use[s] the attestation envelope defined in §7.4.1, with identity-link-specific fields," and §9.5.2 of the security spec relates them without ruling on the wire format: "This structure is the direct analog of `IdentityLinkAttestation` (§3.5.2): a `#active`/`#agent`-signed statement binding an identity to an out-of-band fact, verified against the DID document" (`.docs/specs/09-security-model.md:465`). The statement that the two canonicalization schemes are independent appears only in shipped-code documentation (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/attestation.rs:1161`): "Note: `IdentityLinkAttestation::canonical_signing_bytes` is governed by a different spec row (§3 identity, domain `SCP-IDENTITY-LINK-ATTESTATION-V1:`) which mandates `MessagePack` for its `claim` as well — its scheme is independent of this function." No section of the security spec rules on whether the two claims share a wire format. *Derivation:* the two are separate wire formats for one logical claim, with different field sets (F1's claim carries `platform_id` and `link_type`, F2's carries `verification_method` and `proof_url`) and different canonicalizations. Open question OQ-28.

**F2 → F8: `attestation_count` counts F2 records and cannot be Merkle-proven.** §7.3.2 of the trust spec states the exception: "**`attestation_count` is the explicit exception: it is NOT a context-event fact and NOT Merkle-anchored.** Attestations are credential-layer artifacts (§7.4), not context-log leaves." So an F8 profile's ninth field depends on how many F2 records the *signing context* could reach, and §7.3.2 says the number is "verifier-relative: two agents may compute different counts because they can access different subsets of the subject's attestations."

**F2 → F8, and back: an unbounded count is not a Sybil bound.** §7.4.1 of the trust spec constrains how that count may be used: "A raw count of authentic attestations — notably `attestation_count` (§7.3.2) — is therefore a credential-layer **claim count**, NOT a standalone trust score, and MUST NOT be treated as one … an admission gate that needs Sybil resistance MUST express it through the independence-scored threshold path (§7.3.5; the per-context `sybil_policy`), NOT through an `AttestationCount` participation requirement (§7.3.2.1)."

**F3 → F2: threshold independence rests on device attestation.** §7.3.5 of the trust spec routes Sybil resistance for F2 threshold checks through F3: "The primary Sybil resistance mechanism is the DeviceAttestation (§9.3), which binds DIDs to hardware-attested devices. Consumers requiring strong Sybil resistance SHOULD require attestors to have valid DeviceAttestations." *Derivation:* §27.3.3 shows nothing binds an F3 token to a DID, and §27.4.3 shows that the one shipped gate on the per-context `sybil_policy` — `evaluate_sybil_resistance` — admits a DID on a caller-asserted map key with no token, no signature, and no DID binding, while the PyO3 verification path returns `IDENT_1016`. So the recommended Sybil control has a gate that reads the consumer's own assertion rather than a platform attestation.

**F3 → F4: F4 carries an F3-class proof as an opaque field, and neither verifies it.** F4's `platform_attestation` holds Apple App Attest or Android Key Attestation bytes — the same platform artifacts F3 wraps — under a separate type, `PlatformAttestation`, with its own `AttestationPlatform` enum (`crates/scp-did/src/attestation.rs:109`). *Derivation:* two families independently wrap the same platform proof, one as an opaque `Vec<u8>` newtype and one as a two-field struct, and neither defines a verification procedure. Open question OQ-29.

**F4 → F7: an F7 violation cites an F4 claim.** The `AttestationMismatch` variant fires when "Platform attestation contradicts observed behavior … an Apple App Attest or Android Key Attestation claims `#active` is hardware-backed, but operations are observed that would require software access to that key material" (`crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/custody_violation.rs:172`), and its `claimed_custody` field carries an F4 custody-model string. *Derivation:* F7's `AttestationMismatch` cannot be raised soundly while F4 carries no verifiable proof (§27.3.4) and F4's `platform_attestation` has no verifier (§27.4.4). The variant's own doc comment states the precondition it depends on: "Without hardware proof, the mismatch is a behavioral signal (Layer 5), not a violation."

**F5 → the DID document, not F3 or F4.** §9.7.1 of the security spec check 1 depends only on resolving the signer's current `#active`/`#agent` verification method. *Derivation from the shipped verifier:* `verify_attestation_with_resolution` reads `ScpCredential::resolve_signing_key(resolved_document)` and nothing else from the document (`crates/scp-mls/src/keypackage_attestation.rs:782`), so **F5's verification does not depend on F3**. It depends on the same DID-document authenticity — the BEP44 self-certification of §9.6.1 of the security spec — that F3 and F4 lean on, and on the rollback resistance §9.7.1 names as an assumption: "This is out of scope for the attestation verifier; it is mitigated at the **resolution layer** — did:dht BEP44 `seq` monotonicity (§9.6.1 …) and did:web fetch integrity (§9.6.2) — which the attestation verifier **assumes**, rather than re-checking."

**F5 → F1, by construction and by precedent.** §9.5.2 of the security spec names the relationship: "This structure is the direct analog of `IdentityLinkAttestation` (§3.5.2): a `#active`/`#agent`-signed statement binding an identity to an out-of-band fact, verified against the DID document." ADR-057's 2026-08-01 amendment repeats it: "the direct in-protocol analog is `IdentityLinkAttestation` (§3.5.1–3.5.2)." *Derivation:* the two families are the same construction applied to two subjects, and they diverge on one point that matters — F5 resolves the signer's key itself and F1 accepts it from the caller (§27.4.1).

**F6 → F4 and F3, through the shared platform proof.** F6 carries a third `PlatformAttestation` type — `{ attestation_data: Vec<u8>, platform: String }` (`crates/scp-protocol/src/context/memory_scope.rs:203`) — distinct from F4's two-field struct with an enum platform and from F3's newtype. *Derivation:* three families now model the same hardware proof three ways, with the platform identified as an enum in F4 and as a free-form string in F6. Open question OQ-29 covers all three.

**F7 → F2: a violation record is not an F2 attestation.** ADR-039's Enforcement Stack layer 4 names F7 records and counter-attestations in one sentence, and neither uses the §7.4.1 envelope. *Derivation:* F7 defines no `AttestationType` tag, so an F7 record cannot travel through the F2 verifier, cannot be revoked through §7.4.4 of the trust spec's mechanism, and does not contribute to `attestation_count`. F7's five fields map onto the §7.4.1 envelope — `subject_did` onto `subject`, `timestamp` onto `issued_at`, `violation` onto `claim` and `evidence`, `verifier_signature` onto `signature`, and `verifier_did` onto `issuer` — and F6 carries a comparable set, so whether either belongs in the envelope as a ninth and tenth `AttestationType` variant is open question OQ-37.

**F8 → the event log, for four of its eight facts.** §7.3.2 of the trust spec pins which facts are convergent: "Facts derived from convergent events — participation duration (`MemberJoined`/`MemberLeft`), governance actions (`GovernanceActionExecuted`), role progression (`RoleAssigned`), and context-creation (`ChildContextCreated`) — are convergent-by-construction," and adds that they are "committer-local rather than independently Merkle-verifiable until ADR-051." `outlet_invocation_count` joins them under ADR-051, which is why `outlet_invocation_count_anchored` is a signed field and `attestation_count` gets no such flag: §7.3.2.1 of the trust spec explains that `attestation_count` "is PERMANENTLY non-anchored … so a per-profile boolean would be a tamperable constant on a signed struct."

## 27.7 Open questions

Each entry states which artifact should define the answer and what breaks while the entry stays open. Entries come in two shapes. An entry marked *Undefined* names a question no artifact answers, or one that two artifacts the artifact flow does not order answer differently. An entry marked *Settled upstream; the downstream artifact diverges* names a question an upstream artifact already answers while a downstream artifact — an ADR, a story, or the shipped code — does something else; under the artifact flow in `CLAUDE.md` the upstream artifact governs and the downstream one changes, and the only decision left to a human is whether to amend the upstream artifact instead. Each such entry names the diverging artifact in its own label. This section answers neither shape.

**OQ-1 — What criterion decides membership in the attestation category, and which records does it admit?** *Undefined:* §7.4 of the trust spec's "signed claims by identities about something" admits ten of the eleven structures §9.5.2 of the security spec tabulates and states no test that separates the ten from each other (§27.1.2), and no artifact defines the set of eight this section gathers. The rule must also decide ten records the section leaves out. Six carry the word: `CounterAttestation`, `KeyDestructionAttestation`, the two `PlatformAttestation` structs, `UcanAttestation`, and `StoredAttestation` (`crates/scp-node/src/bridge_handlers.rs:34`), the bridge-operator record §7.6 of the trust spec covers in its Bridges bullet and §12 of the platform-bridge-connectors spec specifies (see OQ-28). Three do not: the UCAN capability delegation that §7.4.2 of the trust spec names as an attestation type with its own format; `ChallengeVerification`, which §7.3.4 of the trust spec specifies with a record format (`.docs/specs/07-trust-validation-and-capabilities.md:437`), §9.18.2 of the security spec gives its own domain separator, §7.4.1 of the trust spec calls F8's sibling, and `crates/scp-protocol/src/trust/challenge.rs:305` ships with a verifier; and the shadow identity claim, which §7.6's Bridges bullet names — "Shadow identity claims are bridge operator attestations" — and which carries the registered separator `"SCP-CLAIM-V1:"` (§9.18.2 of the security spec), a byte-exact vector (§25.10 of the test-vector spec), and an implementation at `crates/scp-protocol/src/bridge/claiming.rs:189`. The tenth is `DataProvenance` (`crates/scp-protocol/src/provenance/mod.rs:202`), specified by §7.7.1 of the trust spec: §7.6's Security bullet calls provenance chains attestations — "Provenance chains are sequences of attestations about where data came from" (`.docs/specs/07-trust-validation-and-capabilities.md:1036`) — while the shipped struct declares twelve fields and no signature, so §7.4's "signed claims" wording excludes it and §7.6's bullet admits it. *Derivation:* `ChallengeVerification` exhibits all three of §27.1.2's indicators, which F3, F4, F6, and F7 do not, so a criterion that admits the eight and excludes it is not a criterion the indicators produce. *Should define it:* §7.4 of the trust spec, which carries the corpus's only membership sentence, amended to state a test. §27.1.2 shows that the corpus states one criterion and no second one: the paragraph at `.docs/planning-sessions/planning-session-02.md:637` enumerates seven constructs and reports two properties they share, and §7.6 of the trust spec carries that same paragraph downstream, so the amendment states a test the corpus does not yet carry rather than reconciling two artifacts. §27.1 then enumerates against the test. *Breaks meanwhile:* a reviewer cannot tell whether a ninth construct belongs to this surface, so the ninth family drifts the way the first eight did.

**OQ-2 — What is F3's canonical construction, and what binds a device token to the DID it is filed under?** *Undefined:* no domain separator, no field order, no DID input; the platform SDKs compute a `clientDataJSON` binding that the Rust trait's `attest()` discards (§27.3.3). *Should define it:* a new subsection of §9.3 of the security spec, plus a domain-separator row in §9.18.2. *Breaks meanwhile:* an attacker who obtains any valid platform token can file it under any DID, so §9.3's device-attestation trust signal and §7.3.5 of the trust spec's Sybil recommendation both rest on nothing. The same decision fixes what `evaluate_sybil_resistance` must be given before `policy.require_device_attestation` means anything, because that gate tests only for the presence of a caller-supplied map key (§27.4.3, contradiction C9). It also settles `.docs/specs/00-open-questions.md:9`, which marks the device-attestation wiring resolved and lists four launch-blocking responsibilities the shipped build does not perform; that entry needs to point here rather than report the question closed.

**OQ-3 — Does F4 carry its own signature, or does it rely on the DID document's?** *Undefined:* the struct has no signature field, and ADR-039's Enforcement Stack layer 4 says only that the document "includes a `ScpKeyCustodyAttestation` service entry" (§27.3.4). *Should define it:* an F4 spec section, whose place in the corpus a human names — §3.5 of the identity spec already owns the DID document service entries F4 rides in. ADR-039 is the only artifact in `.docs/specs/` or `.docs/adrs/` that mentions F4, and it does not decide the question; `.docs/spec-extraction-plan.md` classes the `ScpKeyCustodyAttestation` service entry format as a P0 gap and its test vector as P1, and story SCP-AB-018 in `.docs/prds/agent-binding.json` — marked done — already requires an attestation "signed by #0 (Identity Key)" that the shipped struct cannot carry (§27.4.4, contradiction C18). *Breaks meanwhile:* no second party can counter-sign a custody claim, no consumer can present a custody attestation apart from the whole document, the JSON serialization has no cross-implementation canonical form, and a done story's acceptance criteria are unmet.

**OQ-4 — What does F7's `verifier_signature` cover?** *Undefined:* no domain separator, no construction, no verifier; every field sits outside the signed scope, so nothing is signed (§27.3.7). *Should define it:* an F7 spec section, whose place in the corpus a human names. The decision has two shapes and OQ-37 asks which: F7 signs under its own domain separator, which then needs a row in §9.18.2 of the security spec, or F7 becomes an `AttestationType` variant and signs under `"SCP-ATTESTATION-V1:"`, which §9.18.2 already registers. `.docs/spec-extraction-plan.md` classes the `ScpCustodyViolationAttestation` format as part of the same P0 identity gap. *Breaks meanwhile:* ADR-039 calls these records permanent and §9.9.3 of the security spec grounds a relay operator's cross-context trust score on them, and any party can forge one because there is no signature to check.

**OQ-5 — Which of F8's two domain separators is normative?** *Undefined:* the code signs under `"SCP-PARTICIPATION-V1:"`, §9.5.2 of the security spec tabulates `"SCP-PARTICIPATION-PROFILE-V1:"`, and §9.18.2 registers both for one struct (§27.3.8). *Should define it:* §9.5.2 and §9.18.2 of the security spec, together. This entry is one of four that together fix one preimage — OQ-5 the separator, OQ-7 the field set, OQ-9 the hash-versus-raw construction, and OQ-34 the field order — so answering any one of the four in isolation leaves F8 without a construction. *Breaks meanwhile:* two conforming implementations produce signatures that do not verify against each other.

**OQ-6 — Which HKDF salt and info derive F8's context signing key?** *Undefined:* three candidates stand. §7.3.2.1 of the trust spec specifies `salt = SHA-256("SCP-PARTICIPATION-SIGNER-V1")` with `info = "scp-participation-signer:" || context_id`. The code uses the context id as salt and `"scp-participation-statement-v1"` as info (§27.3.8), which §9.18.3 of the security spec registers. `.docs/audits/crypto-constructions-audit.md` recommendation 7 proposes a third (`:356`): "`signing_seed = HKDF-Expand(HKDF-Extract(salt="scp-participation-signer-v1", ikm=context_secret_key), info=context_id, L=32)`", which matches neither of the other two in salt or info. Answering this closes the standing CRITICAL audit finding "[CRYPTO-14] `ParticipationProfile` Signing Key Derivation Not Specified" (`.docs/audits/crypto-constructions-audit.md:110`). *Should define it:* §7.3.2.1 of the trust spec and §9.18.3 of the security spec, together — §9.18.3 registers the code's info string as normative, so the two spec sections contradict each other and a human amending only one leaves the conflict standing. The salt needs a home in either section; it is registered nowhere. *Breaks meanwhile:* the two derive different keypairs from the same seed, so a verifier conforming to §7.3.2.1 rejects every profile a shipped SDK signs.

**OQ-7 — Is `outlet_invocation_count_anchored` inside F8's signed scope?** *Undefined:* the code signs it and §7.3.2.1 of the trust spec requires it signed; §9.5.2 of the security spec's table omits it (§27.3.8). *Should define it:* §9.5.2 of the security spec. It is one of the four entries — OQ-5, OQ-7, OQ-9, and OQ-34 — that together fix F8's one preimage. *Breaks meanwhile:* the anchoring flag can be stripped from a profile signed by a spec-conforming implementation, which is exactly what §7.3.2.1 says the signed field prevents.

**OQ-8 — Does `platform_attestation` enter F6's signed scope, what else does that scope contain, how is `method` encoded, and what binds the signing key?** *Undefined:* §9.15 of the security spec states neither a field order nor an encoding for F6, so four questions stand together and no spec sentence rules on any of them (§27.3.6). (a) **Does `platform_attestation` enter the signed scope?** It sits outside the preimage today while the `method` field it substantiates sits inside, so a holder detaches the proof from the signature that binds the declaration (contradiction C34). The 2026-08-25 ruling in §27.4.6 makes this answer load-bearing rather than cosmetic: clause 1 of that ruling admits a `HardwareBacked` reading only when a verification of the accompanying proof returns a pass, and a proof the signature does not cover establishes nothing about the declaration it arrived beside — so clause 1 cannot admit a hardware reading on the shipped record whatever a future verifier decides, and answering OQ-2 and OQ-29 alone leaves clause 1 with no carrier. A human decides the scope; §27 does not design it. (b) `method` enters the preimage as its `Display` output behind a 4-byte length prefix, which is a fifth enum encoding alongside the four §9.5.2 of the security spec sanctions, and §9.5.1's nine rules name no enum or discriminator. (c) The preimage binds no `signing_key_id`, so a verifier reading only the signed bytes cannot enforce §9.15's rule that a destruction attestation is "signed by #0 (Identity Key) or #active (Active Signing Key); NOT #agent" (`.docs/specs/09-security-model.md:1386`) — F5 carries its `signing_key_id` inside the preimage (§27.3.5 field 6) and F6 carries the constraint nowhere.

# ADR-061: Outlet Invocation Modes — Delivery × Envelope Taxonomy

**Status:** Accepted (2026-07-12). Revised 2026-07-13 after a four-lens review (architecture, premises, cryptography, spec-alignment) — the revision corrects a fabricated citation, defines the envelope axis by guarantee rather than by mechanism, and specifies the streaming-saga seal phase + receipt separator that the first draft left implicit.

**Context sources:** spec §5.4 (outlet registration/invocation) and §5.4.5 (Progressive Output / streaming delivery); §6.2 (cross-context outlet interfaces) and §6.2.4 (cross-context outlet invocation saga — its Prepare/Commit FSM, exactly-once durable `SagaId`-keyed capture, dual event-log, receipt, §17.16.4 crash recovery); §7.3.8 (invocation caveats, Class-S caveat counters); §9.18.2 (domain-separator registry); ADR-049 §3 (Commit *triggers* execution — the generic executor cannot cross the actor mailbox), §3a (bounded block-until-terminal saga wait; 30 s per-phase timeout), §3b (a saga is justified ONLY by a harmful partial commit across 2+ distinct contexts), §9 (Class-S crash-safety, `commit_class_s_keep`); PRD SCP-OUT-036 (streaming outlet delivery; AC "bridge does not buffer"). **Anchor-integration note:** §5.4.5 (Progressive Output) and the outlet PRD (incl. SCP-OUT-036) originated on `feat/outlet-redesign` and have been integrated onto this branch (§5.4.5 in commit `38592c876`; `outlet.json` in `04c552b06`) so every citation resolves on-branch — an ADR must not cite artifacts an agent cannot find.

**Supersedes:** the informal "every outlet call is a stream" unification (a *planning-note* framing, never an ADR). ADR-061 deliberately makes **unary and streaming genuinely distinct modes** with *different* committed integrity artifacts (`output_hash` vs `stream_manifest_hash`); a unary call is NOT modeled as a 1-chunk stream. (An earlier draft mis-cited this framing as "ADR-049 §5"; ADR-049 §5 is `OwnedIdentityDid` and contains no streaming concept. The mis-citation is removed.)

## Decision

Classify an outlet invocation by **two axes**, never by *where* it runs:

- **Delivery** — the shape of the response (a §5.4/§5.4.5 property; applies even same-context):
  - **unary** — a single response; committed integrity artifact `output_hash`.
  - **streaming** — a sequence of operator-signed chunks (`SCP-OUTLET-CHUNK-SIG-V1`) with per-chunk credit/escrow; committed integrity artifact `stream_manifest_hash` (RFC-6962 Merkle root over the sealed chunk sequence; a fixed 32 bytes regardless of stream length).
- **Envelope** — defined by its **guarantee**, NOT by a mechanism: **best-effort** (no exactly-once, receipt, or crash recovery) vs **transactional** (exactly-once execution, a signed receipt, durable `SagaId`-keyed capture, dual event-log recording, crash recovery). The transactional guarantee is *realized* by a mechanism chosen by locality: **cross-context by the §6.2.4 saga** (the only justification for a saga per ADR-049 §3b), and — in principle — same-context by a single-actor journal. Defining the axis by the guarantee (not "the saga") is what keeps it orthogonal to delivery and to location.

**On the envelope↔location correlation (a review finding, stated honestly).** For the modes SCP builds *today*, the transactional guarantee is realized only by the cross-context saga (§3b forbids a same-context saga), so best-effort⟺same-context and transactional⟺cross-context are presently a bijection. This ADR still names modes by **delivery + guarantee, never by location**, because (a) the guarantee, not the deployment, is the property a caller reasons about; (b) it is forward-compatible with a future same-context journaled transactional mode; and (c) naming by location is precisely what caused the recurring confusion. The bijection is a *current realization fact*, not a definitional identity.

The cross product yields **four canonical modes**, all supported:

| | **best-effort** | **transactional** (exactly-once + receipt + recovery) |
|---|---|---|
| **unary** (→ `output_hash`) | **plain outlet invocation** — the base same-context call | **outlet invocation saga** (§6.2.4; realized cross-context) |
| **streaming** (→ `stream_manifest_hash`) | **outlet stream** | **streaming saga** (realized cross-context) |

- **plain outlet invocation** — unary, best-effort: base `invoke_outlet`. On `main`.
- **outlet invocation saga** — unary, transactional: §6.2.4; commits `output_hash`. On `main`.
- **outlet stream** — streaming, best-effort: signed chunk stream + Class-S credit, no transaction envelope. (Same-context streaming runtime.)
- **streaming saga** — streaming, transactional: the §6.2.4 envelope extended with a **seal phase** (below), committing `stream_manifest_hash`.

**Naming rule (normative).** Discriminate by delivery (**unary**/**streaming**) and envelope guarantee (**best-effort**/**transactional**). Never use "cross-context" as a mode discriminator. "unary"/"streaming" are the industry matched pair (cf. gRPC); "unary" names response cardinality, not an implementation ("buffered").

## Streaming-saga mechanism (the seal phase)

The first draft's "the saga commits once over the Merkle root" was imprecise: the root is a *close-time* artifact (it does not exist until the stream ends), so it cannot be committed at the Commit *transition*. The §6.2.4 FSM is extended with a distinct **seal/finalize phase**:

1. **Commit-transition** (unchanged shape, prompt): confirms the reservation and *triggers* the pump (ADR-049 §3 — Commit triggers execution; the executor cannot cross the mailbox). It does **not** block for the stream and does **not** yet sign a receipt.
2. **Streaming** (off-mailbox pump): chunks are (a) forwarded to the caller as produced (`mpsc::Receiver`, no buffering — SCP-OUT-036 AC preserved) and (b) captured durably and *incrementally*, keyed by `SagaId`: an **O(log n) RFC-6962 Merkle frontier** plus the per-chunk credit ledger, persisted as chunks arrive. This durable capture is a **replay snapshot, not a bridge buffer** — it never gates caller-forward latency and never accumulates the full payload set in memory (the batch `compute_chunk_manifest_root(&[…])` is a convenience for bounded inputs; the pump MUST use the incremental frontier).
3. **Seal-phase / stream-close**: finalize the manifest root from the frontier, sign the streaming receipt, settle escrow (`settle_at_close` → refund = reserved − billed), record both event logs (`OutletInvoked` target-side, `CrossContextOutletInvoked` caller-side), then FSM → `Committed`. The atomic `Committed` terminal is reached **at close**, not at the Commit-transition.

**Duration / ADR-049 §3a reconciliation (two clauses, one of which needs a §3a amendment).** §3a has two load-bearing clauses; the streaming saga reconciles with the first and *extends* the second:

1. **30 s per-phase timeout — reconciled.** A long stream must not sit inside the Commit-B phase and blow the phase timeout. The stream executes in the **seal phase**, whose duration is bounded by the invoker-controlled credit/escrow envelope (`effective_max_billable_chunks` + `timeout_ms` + the credit-stall timer), **not** the saga phase timeout. The Commit-transition itself completes promptly.

2. **≤~95 s block-until-terminal FFI wait — EXTENDED, not merely reconciled.** §3a states the FFI worker blocks one bounded saga (≤~95 s worst case) and returns *after* `Committed`, explicitly rejecting an async/poll model. That premise is **unary-specific**: it was written when the only cross-context saga was unary and its result was bounded. A streaming saga reaches `Committed` at **seal-close**, which for a slow LLM stream can exceed 95 s (the credit ceiling bounds chunk *count*, not wall-clock). Therefore the streaming-saga FFI surface does NOT block-until-`Committed`: it returns the `mpsc::Receiver` **promptly** at the Commit-transition (the caller consumes chunks as produced) and the saga reaches `Committed` **asynchronously at seal-close**. This is a genuine extension of §3a's wait model, not a reuse of it. **Consequence: ADR-049 §3a requires a companion amendment** stating that its block-until-terminal / no-async-model rule governs the *unary* saga, and the *streaming* saga returns a receiver promptly + commits asynchronously at seal-close. (Deferrable with the streaming-saga FFI surface, which is planned — but the §3a wait model must be amended before that surface ships, not silently broken.)

**Mid-stream crash: seal-prefix-and-close, never resume (normative).** Re-invoking a non-deterministic or side-effecting outlet (an LLM produces different tokens) would break §6.2.4's "replayed Commit re-emits the stored output, never re-invokes." On crash mid-stream the recovery MUST **seal the durable chunk prefix and close the stream truncated**: compute the manifest root over the sealed prefix, sign the receipt over that truncated manifest, settle escrow at the prefix's `billed_count`. A replayed Commit re-emits the sealed prefix + terminal close and does **not** resume the outlet. The `CancelAckTracker` billing-ceiling (`accrue only at-or-below cancel-ack-seq`) is exactly the primitive that makes a truncated close well-defined.

## Receipt (streaming): a distinct separator, a different reproducibility mechanism

The §6.2.4 `SCP-XCTX-RECEIPT-V1:` preimage is closed with `Fixed32(output_hash)` and a self-verifiability obligation that carries the **JCS output bytes inline** so a verifier recomputes `SHA-256(output)`. A manifest root cannot occupy that slot under the same separator (a verifier cannot tell whether to recompute `SHA-256(bytes)` or a Merkle root — an ambiguous, non-domain-separated receipt). Therefore:

- **New separator `SCP-XCTX-STREAM-RECEIPT-V1:`** for streaming-saga receipts (registered in §9.18.2 alongside `SCP-OUTLET-CHUNK-SIG-V1` and the `SCP-OUTLET-CHUNK-V1` manifest-leaf domain). The unary `SCP-XCTX-RECEIPT-V1` stays exactly as specified.
- **Different reproducibility mechanism (normative).** The streaming receipt carries the **32-byte manifest root directly** (it cannot carry the chunk sequence without defeating boundedness). Its reproducibility on replay comes from the **`SagaId`-keyed durable capture of the root at stream-close**, never from re-executing the stream — replacing the unary "output canonicalization obligation" (carry-bytes-and-recompute) with a "durable root capture at close" obligation. A third-party auditor holding only the receipt gets **root-binding** verification (the root commits the ordered, counted, per-chunk-signed sequence), not inline-output recomputation.

## Rationale

- **Why streaming.** The dominant outlet is an LLM; unary-only breaks token-by-token delivery, caps output, and prevents incremental metering. SCP-OUT-036 mandates non-buffering delivery.
- **Why keep the unary saga.** Atomic one-shot calls needing a receipt + recovery (not streaming) are fully served by §6.2.4.
- **Why the synthesis is sound (and per-chunk 2PC is not).** Per-chunk 2PC is unsound (an unbounded stream is not a single atomic prepare/commit, and it destroys streaming). The synthesis instead commits a **bounded** `stream_manifest_hash` **once, at the seal phase** — the Merkle root summarizes an arbitrarily long sequence in 32 bytes, so a single seal-commit suffices. Boundedness is the enabling lemma; the seal phase is the mechanism.
- **Class-S composition is clean (affirmative).** The saga Prepare reservation is *reversible* (RAII drop-on-not-commit, pre-Commit); the per-chunk stream credit is *Class-S monotonic* (`commit_class_s_keep`, post-Commit, never un-consumed on a coalesce crash). They never collide: aborts occur pre-Commit (pre-stream); `settle_at_close` refunds only the *unused reservation ceiling* (reserved − billed), never a rollback of already-spent credit. On `NeedsRepair`, §6.2.4 already declines to auto-void the economic reservation (settled by the signed divergence marker + operator repair).
- **No-buffering + exactly-once is not a contradiction (affirmative).** Forward-as-produced to the caller and durable-incremental-seal are two sinks (write-through), not buffering; the durable sink is an O(log n) frontier, not the payload set.
- **What the transactional envelope uniquely adds (fourth-mode rationale, corrected).** Per-chunk exactly-once *billing* comes from the Class-S credit ledger — present in **best-effort outlet stream too**, so it is NOT what distinguishes the streaming saga. The saga uniquely adds **cross-context atomic dual-log recording, a signed receipt, and caller-side escrow settlement**. The streaming saga is the intersection of two first-class features (cross-context transactionality × streaming delivery) — the required mode for a paid, cross-context, metered LLM outlet.
- **Equivocation resistance (affirmative gain over the unary saga).** Because each Merkle leaf hashes `jcs(chunk)` including the operator's per-chunk signature and sequence, the manifest root binds the ordered, counted, individually-signed sequence — an operator cannot stream one content to member X and another to Y and commit a root covering only one.

## Alternatives considered and rejected

1. **Unary saga only (no streaming).** Breaks LLM outlets; violates SCP-OUT-036.
2. **Streaming bridge only; retire the §6.2.4 saga.** Loses exactly-once/receipt/recovery for transactional calls.
3. **A "streaming saga" that 2PCs each chunk.** Unsound (see Rationale). The accepted synthesis is single-seal-commit over a bounded Merkle root.
4. **Keep "cross-context" as the mode discriminator.** It is a deployment location, not a call kind — the source of the recurring confusion.
5. **Reuse `SCP-XCTX-RECEIPT-V1` for the streaming receipt.** Rejected: ambiguous verification algorithm in a closed preimage; requires the distinct `SCP-XCTX-STREAM-RECEIPT-V1` separator.

## Consequences

- **§6.2.4 gains a seal-phase** FSM extension for the streaming saga; §6.2.5 carries the normative mode summary; §6.2.4 is tagged as the unary/transactional corner.
- **§9.18.2 registry rows** must be added: `SCP-OUTLET-CHUNK-SIG-V1`, the `SCP-OUTLET-CHUNK-V1` manifest-leaf/interior domain, and `SCP-XCTX-STREAM-RECEIPT-V1`.
- **Upstream anchors must be integrated onto the implementing branch**: spec §5.4.5 (Progressive Output / streaming delivery) and PRD SCP-OUT-036, currently on `feat/outlet-redesign`. The streaming-saga slice must not proceed until they are present (artifact-flow invariant).
- **Wiring obligations** (streaming slices): compute `stream_manifest_hash` and `chunks_billed` from **one** retained canonical chunk sequence; invoke `verify_chunks_billed` → `EventLogError::ChunksBilledMismatch` at event-log append; reconcile `StreamEscrow` settled `billed_count` against the manifest-derived count. Production sites currently hardcode `stream_manifest_hash: [0u8;32]` (e.g. `outlets/invoke.rs`, the uniffi bridge, the MCP surface) — these are the wiring targets.
- **Defense-in-depth (streaming slice):** replace the free-form `StreamSignerError::Custody { detail: String }` with a bounded category enum so a custody adapter cannot leak key material / preimage into structured logs.
- **Chunk-sig replay resistance** rests on `request_id` (UUIDv7) uniqueness + `caveats_binding` pinning (`stream_epoch` is not in the chunk preimage) — to be noted in §5.4.5.
- Implementation status: *plain outlet invocation* and *outlet invocation saga* on `main`; *outlet stream* in progress (same-context streaming runtime; primitives ported); *streaming saga* planned (this ADR defines its target mechanism).

## Addendum (2026-08-02): saga-open idempotency + bounded self-refund of an abandoned streaming-saga reserve (#2197)

**Status:** Accepted (2026-08-02), cryptographer-vetted SOUND conditional on the six refinements folded in below. Spec text landed with this addendum: §6.2.4 *Saga-open idempotency* and §5.4.5 *Universal seal-phase reclamation*. The runtime implementation is a separate follow-up.

**Context.** Implementing the streaming saga surfaced a money-safety defect: the caller's **only** handle to an in-flight saga's escrow reserve is the `SagaId`. If the caller loses it — a dropped FFI handle, a caller crash, or (verified) an FFI open on a **detached** runtime task (UniFFI `runtime().spawn().await`, mirrored on the Kotlin streaming surface) whose cancellation cannot drop the Rust future — the reserve **orphans and never refunds**. A conditional **double-reserve** also exists when a fast retry re-opens after the nonce-dedup window has lapsed or on a new handle. Root cause: saga-open is not idempotent on a caller-held key, and the seal phase can block indefinitely (`outer pump blocks on inner_rx.recv()` with no terminal chunk and `timeout_ms == 0`). The fix is **server-side** — a caller-side cancel cannot drop a detached future. Two permanent decisions:

**(i) Saga-open idempotency.** The §6.2.4 `nonce` is the saga-open idempotency key. A **same-caller-principal** duplicate open — the **channel-/bridge-authenticated** `(caller_context_id, caller_did)` identity, **never a caller-supplied `caller_did`** — plus `(target_context_id, outlet_registration_id, nonce)` **resumes** the existing saga (same `SagaId`, same single caller-side escrow reservation, reattached live stream), resolved at the **initiator before Prepare-A**, never re-invoking the outlet. A **different-principal** duplicate remains a target-side anti-replay `Rejected` (§6.2.4 nonce-dedup, unchanged). The `SagaOpenIdempotencyKey → { saga_id, index_state }` mapping is durable and registered **atomically with the `SagaId` mint, in a single lookup-or-create critical section, before the Phase-1 escrow debit**, so (a) no escrow reserve can exist without a durable key to reach it and (b) two concurrent same-nonce opens cannot both mint-and-reserve. Normative in §6.2.4.

**(ii) Bounded self-refund of an abandoned streaming-saga reserve.** The seal phase (this ADR, *Streaming-saga mechanism*) MUST terminate within a bounded envelope — an **idle deadline** (`stream_seal_idle_secs`, `SCP-OUTLET-6134`) OR an **absolute max-duration cap** (`stream_max_duration_secs`, `SCP-OUTLET-6136`; the context default backing `OutletStreamOpen.timeout_ms == 0`) — and force-settle over the durable prefix exactly as the *mid-stream crash: seal-prefix-and-close* mechanism does, refunding `reserved − billed`. The seal-phase pump owns these timers **server-side** and force-settles with **no** caller-side drain/cancel, so seal-close (and thus `settle_at_close`) is **always reached** and escrow always refunds without the client-held `SagaId`. This **generalizes the crash-only seal-prefix-and-close to also cover an abandoned/wedged live stream**, and closes the "outer pump blocks on `inner_rx.recv()` forever" gap. Normative in §5.4.5.

**Cryptographer refinements folded into the spec text (conditions of the SOUND verdict).**
- **R1 (resume authentication).** The resume principal is the **channel-/bridge-authenticated** identity, never a caller-supplied `caller_did` — a co-tenant asserting a victim's `caller_did` MUST NOT reattach to the victim's saga (the §5.4.5 *FFI bridge caller authentication, CRITICAL #1* class, applied to resume). §6.2.4 *Idempotent resume*.
- **R2 (replay-deterministic cutoff).** Seal-reclaim MUST **durably record the seal cutoff (last-sealed `sequence` / `billed_count`) BEFORE signing the receipt**, so `stream_manifest_hash` is reproducible on replay. §5.4.5 force-settle step 1.
- **R3 (log-insert consistency).** The reclaimed `OutletInvokedEvent` MUST populate `cancel_ack_seq` / the ceiling so the §5.4.5 `chunks_billed_ref` log-insert check passes over the truncated manifest. §5.4.5 force-settle.
- **R4 (never reattach-to-a-corpse; index-keyed resume).** The resume path dispatches on the durable **index** state, NEVER on journal presence (the journal legitimately disappears under compaction): **index-absent ⇒ fresh** (the only mint trigger); **index `Live` + journal live ⇒ re-drive the open (`PreparingA`/`PreparingB`) or reattach the live stream (seal-phase)**; **index `Live` + journal absent ⇒ the benign pre-`PreparingA` open-crash window (no reserve) ⇒ re-drive the open**; **index `Terminal(outcome)` ⇒ return the recorded outcome regardless of journal presence** (post-compaction case), never re-invoke. **Journal absence alone is NEVER "fresh"** — that would re-mint and re-invoke an already-terminal saga (double-reserve). §6.2.4 *Resume dispatches on the INDEX state*.
- **R5 (atomic lookup-or-create).** The resume lookup-or-create is a **single critical section**, so two concurrent same-nonce opens cannot both mint-and-reserve. §6.2.4 *Durable saga-open idempotency index*.
- **R6 (do not purge B's dedup; tombstone A-side instead).** A saga terminal — **including `Aborted`** — MUST NOT purge B's nonce-dedup (that would reopen the replay window for the forward-declared cross-node transport and would need a new authenticated purge message). Instead the **A-side index entry is tombstoned on all terminals** (grace ≥ `max(B nonce-dedup TTL, seal max-duration)`); a same-nonce retry hits the tombstone and short-circuits to the recorded terminal, budget-neutral. §6.2.4 *Anti-replay reject* + *Index lifecycle*.
- **Minor (deterministic reclamation code, closed by construction).** The effective idle deadline is **derived/clamped** — `effective_idle = max(configured stream_seal_idle_secs, stream_credit_stall_secs + 1s)` — so the credit-stall (`SCP-OUTLET-6133`) always fires strictly before the idle-reclaim (`SCP-OUTLET-6134`) **by construction**, with **no config-rejection MUST and no standing deployer obligation** (per the project's "closed by construction, not a denylist" tenet). The two reclamation codes (`6134` idle, `6136` max-duration) are allocated in the Execution `6130..6139` band, coordinated with the #2209 taxonomy split (which **allocated** `SCP-OUTLET-6132` = `execution.stream-cap-exhausted` as a live registry row; `6134`/`6136` were left free for #2197). `stream_seal_idle_secs` keeps default `45`. The only residual coincidence — the absolute max-duration cap firing in the same tick as the idle or credit-stall deadline — resolves to `6136` (the harder absolute cap wins); the money outcome is identical.

**Open product/design notes (deliberately non-blocking, recorded as spec notes).** The exact `stream_max_duration_secs` default (proposed `900s`) is a product call (§5.4.5). Cross-process resume carries **no payload backfill** — the durable capture is a Merkle frontier of hashes, not a payload buffer — so a cross-process resume delivers at most post-reattach chunks plus the terminal receipt; money-safety does not depend on reattach (§5.4.5 *Live-saga reattach*).

**Reservation / journal lifecycle (composition with ADR-049).** The durable idempotency index is an **adjunct to the ADR-049 saga journal** and shares its §17.16.4 crash-recovery sweep (which reverses an orphaned reserve on the Prepare-in-progress path per §6.2.4 *Durably-staged caller deduction*). There is **no cross-store transaction** between the index and the journal (the production `SagaJournal` exposes none, and it compacts a resolved saga to zero entries), so two strict **happens-before** orderings — not shared critical sections — govern consistency:
- **Open side:** `index {saga_id, Live}` → journal `PreparingA` → escrow reserve + `CallerReservationRecord` → journal `PreparingB`. The `PreparingA` append precedes the reserve, so **no live reserve is ever journal-absent** — the journal-driven §17.16.4 sweep always covers and reverses it (matching the pre-existing *Durably-staged caller deduction* clause). The index precedes the reserve, so no reserve exists without a durable key.
- **Terminal side:** the `Terminal(outcome)` tombstone (carrying the durable terminal record) is made durable **BEFORE** `mark_resolved`/compaction, so a journal-compacted terminal always reads `Terminal` in the index (never `Live`).

The resume FSM keys on the **index state**, never on journal presence: **absent ⇒ fresh**; **`Terminal` ⇒ recorded outcome** (post-compaction case); **`Live`+journal-live ⇒ re-drive/reattach**; **`Live`+journal-absent ⇒ the benign pre-`PreparingA` open-crash window (no reserve taken) ⇒ re-drive the open**, NOT `SagaNeedsRepair`. `SagaNeedsRepair` is reserved for **durably-decidable contradictions** — states decidable from durable state alone that cannot arise under any legal happens-before, e.g. a `Terminal(outcome)` tombstone whose recorded settlement cutoff / `billed_count` contradicts the sealed chunk sequence or the journal's recorded facts. A purely structural `Terminal`-index-over-a-non-terminal-journal state at rest is **NOT** such a contradiction: it is the benign tombstone-before-`mark_resolved` crash transient (recovered by idempotently re-running `mark_resolved`/compaction; `settle_at_close` is `SagaId`-idempotent), and "still-advancing" is not decidable at crash-restart so it MUST NOT be the discriminator. The tombstone is deleted only after the grace window by a bounded durable-store TTL; journal-less `Live` entries (pre-`PreparingA` crash, reserveless) are reaped by the same index-store reaper. The escrow reservation continues to settle at seal-close via `settle_at_close` (ADR-049 §3a *streaming-saga slot release*), which decision (ii) now guarantees is **always reached**. Decision (ii) also **strengthens the ADR-049 §3a streaming-wait-model companion amendment already flagged in this ADR** (§Duration/§3a reconciliation, clause 2): the streaming saga returns a receiver promptly and commits asynchronously at seal-close — this addendum makes that asynchronous seal-close a **bounded, always-reached** terminal, not an open-ended one.

**Standalone ADR vs. addendum — decision: ADDENDUM (this document), not a standalone ADR.** Neither decision introduces a new architectural axis — (i) refines the existing §6.2.4 `nonce` semantics and this ADR's seal-phase reservation lifecycle; (ii) generalizes the seal-prefix-and-close mechanism this ADR already owns. The durable idempotency index, though a new durable structure, is best understood as an adjunct to the ADR-049 journal + reservation lifecycle (cross-referenced there), not a free-standing decision; a standalone ADR would fragment the streaming-saga decision record across two documents. Promote to a standalone ADR only if implementation generalizes the index beyond the streaming saga (e.g. to the unary saga or a future same-context journaled transactional mode).

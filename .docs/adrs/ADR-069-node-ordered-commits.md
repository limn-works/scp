# ADR-069: Node-Ordered Commits

**Status:** Accepted 2026-11-02. Alec settled the design on 2026-10-25 (decisions D1 to D3 and question Q4 of the context-operation plan), and ruled "Rewrite now" on 2026-11-02 for the artifacts that ADR-067 had shaped. **Amended 2026-11-02:** decision 10 records Alec's ruling that the node's order must stop starvation and enforces it by a queue of requests that every member verifies: every action that needs a Commit enters the chain as a self-contained request, and a Commit is valid only when it executes a non-empty prefix of the queue; decision 2 records that D2's priority order is withdrawn, because the queue bounds every request without it; decision 8 makes the re-seat a request that X submits to the node itself, and records that two of Alec's rulings expired with their premise; decision 9 records the design he accepted for checking a KeyPackage's lifetime against the time the node signs into the chain, adds margins to that check, and sizes them from the message lifetime.

**Supersedes:** ADR-067, Concurrent Commits and fork merge, in `phase-6.md`. ADR-067's status flips to `Superseded by ADR-069`, and its body stays as the historical record.

**Amends:** ADR-066 decisions 5, 10, 32 and 40 and its acceptance-criteria paragraph; ADR-031 §7 and its 2026-10-17 amendment; ADR-029 §5b, §5c and §6 step 4, and its rationale paragraph on conflict resolution; ADR-001 criterion 6; ADR-051 §3, "No trusted sequencer". Each amended record carries a dated amendment that names this record.

**Spec:** `07-trust-validation-and-capabilities.md` §7.3.1 and §7.3.1.1 ("Competing Commits", "The re-seat", "Commit order"), `05-contexts.md` §5.9.1, §5.9.1.1, §5.12.3.4, §5.12.3.5 and §5.13.3 ("Governance state", rules 11, 13, 15 and 16), `09-security-model.md` §9.5.2, §9.7.1, §9.7.2, §9.9.3, §9.10.2, §9.14 and §9.18, `10-infrastructure-and-self-hosting.md` §10.2, §10.3 and §10.8.1, `23-sync-and-offline-strategy.md` §23.4, §23.5 and §23.7, and `25-test-vectors.md` §25.33.

## Context

`admit_and_merge_own_commit` (`crates/scp-mls/src/group.rs`) merges the committer's own Commit before anyone orders it. When two members commit at one epoch N, each merges its own Commit, and each member's openmls then rejects the other Commit as `WrongEpoch`, so the group splits.

ADR-066 decision 5 had answered the race with "Epoch order decides conflicts": a Commit that loses the race for an epoch never merges, and its executor re-checks it and sends it again. That rule needs one order of Commits that every member reads. On 2026-10-24 no party could give that order, because Alec had ruled "We can’t rely on daily presence" and the relays were untrusted store-and-forward services. ADR-067 therefore let every member keep both children of an epoch, choose one by the least (rank, cth), and re-apply the losing child's changes through fork records, imports and a merge function G. It needed sixteen mechanisms (M1 to M16), a fifth body item `merge` in the governance block, a journal, a key ledger, an epoch table, branch retention for up to seven days, a PPRF over child init secrets, two signed notice records, and ten accepted residuals.

On 2026-10-25 Alec settled how a context runs (`~/.claude/plans/context-operation-plan.md`, rulings 1 to 10):

- "Contexts most certainly have operators, and in fact, require them." "Nodes are pieces of infrastructure that operate many things. One of those things could absolutely be a specific context."
- "if a context runs on a single node and that node is off then the context is unreachable. Se la vie."
- "The context is the engine for finalization." "Why would governance be involved? It's an immutable part of the protocol".
- D1: "One node runs a context and is its MLS Delivery Service: it totally orders submitted Commits, one per epoch. Members verify every Commit."
- D2: members submit Commits directly to the context's node, with the priority class (Remove, re-join, other) as a field of the submission outside the MLS message, ordered "Remove first, then re-join, then arrival." Decision 2 withdraws that order: the node appends in arrival order, the submission carries no class field, and the request queue of decision 10 bounds the wait of every action, a Remove and a re-join among them.
- D3: "The node holds no MLS group secrets."
- Q4: the node signs each chain entry (position, item hash, previous hash) and returns a signed receipt that assigns a position to each accepted submission, under a key the operator binds to its identifier when the context is instantiated.

A node that runs the context is online whenever the context is reachable, so the ordering party that ADR-067 lacked exists. The question this record answers is how members turn the node's order into one Commit per epoch when the node cannot read a Commit.

## Options considered

1. **Keep ADR-067 and add the node as a relay.** Every mechanism of ADR-067 stays, and the node only delivers. This lost because the node already gives a total order, so every mechanism that exists to merge two orders protects against a case the node removes. ADR-067's own necessity table justifies each mechanism by a fork, and a fork needs two Commits that two members both merge.
2. **The node accepts one Commit per epoch and refuses the rest.** The node reads the epoch from the `PrivateMessage` framing (RFC 9420 §6.3, which leaves `epoch` in the clear), appends the first Commit it receives for the current epoch, refuses the rest, and then expects the next epoch. This lost because of D3: the node holds no group secret, so it cannot verify a Commit. A member that sends a malformed Commit for epoch N would take epoch N's place, every member would reject it, and the node would refuse every honest Commit for epoch N as stale, so the context would stop. The node also cannot prove a refusal (Q4: "Refusal to accept is provable to no one").
3. **The node orders every submission, and members take the first valid Commit for each epoch.** The node appends every submission it accepts to one signed chain. Every member processes the chain in position order, and the canonical Commit for epoch N is the first Commit in the chain that names epoch N and passes the member's verification at epoch N. This option is chosen. It meets D1 ("one per epoch", "Members verify every Commit") and D3, because the choice reads only the chain order and each member's own verdict, and ADR-066 decision 10 makes that verdict a function of the Commit's bytes and the state every member holds identically.
4. **A member quorum or a witness set signs the order.** Members or witness nodes would sign which Commit comes first. This lost because Alec ruled on 2026-10-25 that "The context is the engine for finalization" and asked of governance "Why would governance be involved? It's an immutable part of the protocol", so no member, witness or governance action takes part in ordering.

### Options considered for starvation (decision 10, 2026-11-02)

A Commit built for epoch N loses to another member's Commit for N, is rebuilt for N + 1, and arrives after that member's Commit for N + 1, because the other member builds its next Commit while the first is still processing the chain. Under any rule that lets a member's own Commit carry an action that the member alone rebuilds after each loss, a member that commits nonstop wins every epoch, and every other member's action waits for ever.

1. **Append on arrival, with no rule.** The node holds at most one submission at a time, so D2's priority order never acts, and a member that commits nonstop starves every other action. This lost to Alec's ruling of 2026-11-02.
2. **A fixed batching window on the node's clock.** The node collects submissions for a fixed time and appends each batch in D2's order. This lost because the window bounds nothing: a member whose rebuild takes longer than the window loses every epoch to a committer whose next Commit arrives inside the window.
3. **A first-in, first-out queue of requests, and Commits that only execute it.** Every action that needs a Commit enters the chain as a request: a sealed chain item that carries everything its execution needs and authenticates its own author. Every member treats a Commit for epoch N as valid only when it executes a non-empty prefix of the queue, the oldest live requests in chain order, and nothing else. The committer chooses only how many. This option is chosen. The node reads nothing it cannot read (D3) and orders on arrival. A member that commits nonstop can only advance the queue, so it starves nothing. A request waits for at most the live requests ahead of it when it entered, whatever rate any member commits at, and the bound reads no clock (decision 10). A re-filed request enters at the back, so re-filing jumps no queue.
4. **Class-ranked pending requests.** Only a Remove-class or re-join-class action entered the chain as a request, and a Commit had to execute the first executable request in D2's order, Remove first, then re-join, then position; while no request was executable, any Commit was valid. The first fix round of 2026-11-02 chose it, and the check of that round replaced it with option 3 for two reasons. Every other action, an Add, a key-history advance and the record of a recovery that a re-join needs among them, stayed a Commit its author rebuilt, so a member that committed nonstop starved it. While a request was executable, a Commit executed nothing else, and a member that filed a fresh self-reset each time its previous one executed kept every other action out of the chain for as long as it chose. The rank gave the front of the line to whoever could keep a Remove-class or re-join-class request pending.
5. **A hold that any later priority submission ends.** The node cannot verify a submission's class, so a committer that wants to starve a Remove sends one malformed Remove-class submission, the hold ends, and the committer's Commit is appended first. This lost for that reason.
6. **A hold that only the lost submission's own submitter ends, capped by the node's clock.** The node would hold lower-class submissions after a Remove-class or re-join-class submission lost its epoch, until the submitter revealed the secret behind a commitment the lost submission carried, or until `PRIORITY_HOLD_SECS` (120 seconds) passed on the node's clock. The first draft of this amendment chose it, and the review of that draft on 2026-11-02 replaced it for four reasons. The node cannot verify a class, so each malformed Remove-class submission delays every lower-class submission by 120 seconds, and nothing bounds how many one sender submits. The class field tells the node which submissions remove or re-join members. The bound held only for a remover or re-seater whose rebuilt Commit arrived within 120 seconds. The hold secret was a bearer secret, so whoever learned it could open the hold.

## Decision

1. **The node orders.** The node that runs a context is its MLS Delivery Service, the role RFC 9750 (the MLS architecture) gives the service that orders and delivers a group's messages. A member sends each Commit it makes to that node, at the address the context's `ContextNodeAddress` names. The node appends every submission it accepts to the context's chain at the next position, signs the chain entry, and returns a signed receipt naming the position (Q4). The node holds no MLS group secret (D3), so it never decides whether a Commit is valid.
2. **No priority class.** A submission carries the sealed bytes and nothing else, so the node learns no class and appends every submission in arrival order. D2's order, Remove first, then re-join, then arrival, is withdrawn: the queue of decision 10 runs in chain order, and its bound holds for every request, a Remove and a re-join among them, with no rank. D2 placed the class in a field outside the MLS message; a node that holds no group secret cannot verify that field (option 5 of the starvation options), the field tells the node which submissions remove or re-join members, and a rank among requests lets whoever keeps a high-ranked request pending hold every other request back (option 4).
3. **One Commit per epoch.** Every member processes the chain in position order. At epoch N, the canonical Commit is the first chain item that is a Commit naming epoch N and that passes steps 1 and 2 of `07-trust-validation-and-capabilities.md` §7.3.1.1 "Receiver processing" in the member's state at epoch N, with the request items the chain holds before it. A member skips every other Commit: a Commit naming an epoch below its current one, and a Commit naming its current epoch that fails verification. A skipped Commit changes no member's state. Because every member's verdict reads only the Commit, the chain items before it, the `append_time` values the node signed into the chain (decision 9), and state that every member holds identically (ADR-066 decision 10), every honest member merges the same Commit at every epoch.
4. **The committer merges its own Commit at its position.** A committer keeps its own Commit staged and unmerged after it submits it. It merges the staged Commit when, processing the chain in order, it reaches the position its receipt names, its group is still at the epoch the Commit names, and the Commit passes steps 1 and 2 at that position. Otherwise it discards the staged Commit, which changed no member's state. A Commit executes only requests from the queue (decision 10), so a Commit that is not first loses nothing: each request it carried stays in the queue until a canonical Commit executes it or it becomes void, and the protocol obliges no rebuild and no resubmit. The committer never merges a Commit that the chain does not hold.
5. **Conflicts are serial.** "Committed first" in ADR-031 §7 means earlier in the chain. The conflict rule of §7.3.1.1 "Conflicts" and the executed-proposal set settle every pair of actions in chain order, and no merge function runs. The `member:X` conflict key stays: the governance state keeps `change_seq["member:X"]` after X leaves, so an Add of X anchored before a Remove of X loses to that Remove, and an invitation anchored after the Remove passes.
6. **The governance block has four body items.** The body loses item 5, `merge`, and with it the fork record, the import leaves, the re-applications and the carried re-seats. The body bound returns to 1 + 168 + 3 + 16,224 + 15,680 + 1 = 32,077 bytes, 687 bytes below the 32,764-byte body (`07-trust-validation-and-capabilities.md` §7.3.1.1, "Why 32,768 bytes and 96 approvals").
7. **No merge state.** The journal, the key ledger, the epoch table, the fork records, the imports, the pending joiners and the re-seat windows leave the governance state, and `merge_state_hash` leaves the `0xFF02` extension (`05-contexts.md` §5.13.3 rule 16 is withdrawn). A Commit with an empty block again carries the GroupContextExtensions proposal with its extensions unchanged (ADR-066 decision 32).
8. **The re-seat is a request that X submits.** A member X every one of whose leaves its latest recorded `RootRecovery` cut off returns by a re-seat request (`05-contexts.md` §5.12.3.5; `09-security-model.md` §9.7.1, "The re-seat"). X signs a `ReseatRequest` that carries one KeyPackage of X, seals it to the context's request key (decision 10), and submits it to the context's node itself; no member forwards it. X reads the request key from the `scp_request_keys` extension of the latest GroupInfo chain item and checks it against the `request_keys_hash` of the group context that GroupInfo carries, which every member confirmed. The first canonical Commit whose prefix reaches the request removes every leaf of X the recovery cut off, Adds the request's KeyPackage, and sends X the Welcome, whoever commits it. X learns the outcome from that Welcome, so it reads no receipt and no chain item other than the GroupInfo before it joins. The recovery that the re-seat needs is itself a key-history request in the queue, which X or any member submits, so neither the record of the recovery nor the re-seat waits on a member's choice to act. Every external Commit is rejected, and the external re-seat, the re-seat wrap, `ReseatGroupInfo`, the GroupInfo with `external_pub`, rank −1, the re-seat window, the `D_MAX` send rule, the carried re-seat and the unused carried seat are removed. The GroupInfo chain item carries no `external_pub` and no ratchet tree.
   - **Two rulings that expired with their premise.** On 2026-10-21 Alec ruled that the re-joiner "rebuilds its Commit against the new epoch and sends it again". Its premise was a re-joiner that commits its own re-seat as an external Commit and loses an epoch to another Commit. X now commits nothing, and the members execute its request, so no re-seat of X loses an epoch and nothing is rebuilt. On 2026-11-02 Alec answered "Ok" to the recommendation: "the node's signed receipt tells [the external re-seater] 'your submission lost; the epoch is now N+1', and the re-seater retries. Every member already trusts the node for ordering, and a lying node can only delay the re-seater, because the re-seater's next attempt is verified in full." Its premise was an external re-seater that holds no secret of the epoch it joins and so cannot see in the chain that it lost. X now learns the outcome from the Welcome, so the receipt carries no `lost` flag. Neither ruling is reversed: each governs the external re-seat, and that construct no longer exists.
   - **Residual: a forged request key.** X verifies the GroupInfo's signature under a leaf of the group state X last held. A party that holds the private key of such a leaf, a member removed since or a thief of a device that member since replaced, can sign a GroupInfo that names a request key it controls. X's request then never reaches the members, and X re-files after fetching the GroupInfo again. The forger learns the request, which carries nothing X does not publish to the group, and gains no seat.
9. **A KeyPackage's lifetime is checked against the node's append time, with margins, and SCP carries no openmls patch.** The choice of decision 3 requires every member that processes a Commit to reach one verdict on it, and a lifetime check against each member's own clock gives two members two verdicts. Alec accepted the design below on 2026-11-02 ("Accepted."), except the margins and their sizes, which the reviews of 2026-11-02 added after his acceptance to close the case that the "Closed" bullet below names. It replaces the bound that followed his 2026-10-04 answer "no patch, bound, leave us a note for the happy future path", under which a member whose clock ran more than one hour ahead of the adder's, or a member that processed a Commit more than 7 days after it was sent, refused a Commit that the other members merged. Earlier on 2026-11-02 he had rejected an openmls patch: "you offered me a solution that did not rely on a patch. What has changed since then that makes you feel like we need one?"
   - **Append time.** The chain entry the node signs for each position carries `append_time`, the node's clock in Unix seconds when it appended the item (`09-security-model.md` §9.9.3). This is a requirement on the chain entry's format, whose bytes no spec defines yet. Each entry's `append_time` MUST be at least the previous entry's. A member that finds a lower one holds two signed entries that prove the node signed a bad time, processes nothing past the lower entry, and the members re-found the context (plan D9).
   - **Three times.** t_e is the `append_time` of the entry at which a request entered the queue, t_N is the `append_time` of the canonical Commit that opened epoch N, and t_p is the `append_time` of the Commit at position p. A committer at epoch N holds t_e of every request and t_N. It does not know t_p when it builds its Commit.
   - **The numbers.** W, `REQUEST_LIFETIME`, is 604,800 seconds, the time a request stays executable after t_e. P is 604,800 seconds, the processing window: the time after t_p within which a member processes the Commit at p. L is 3,300 seconds, the clock lead a member may hold over the node (55 minutes). σ is 300 seconds, the clock-skew tolerance of `09-security-model.md` §9.14. δ is 300 seconds, the submission delay an honest submitter allows between its own check and the node's `append_time`.
   - **The check at entry.** A request that carries a KeyPackage enters the queue only when the KeyPackage passes, against t_e, `not_after - t_e >= W + P + L` (1,212,900 seconds, 14 days + 55 minutes), `t_e - not_before >= 3,000` seconds (50 minutes), `not_before < not_after`, and `not_after - not_before` no greater than `MAX_KEYPACKAGE_ATTESTATION_LIFETIME` (`09-security-model.md` §9.7.1, KeyPackage `Lifetime` checks), and openmls's public `KeyPackageIn` validation. Every member reads the same t_e, so every member reaches one entry verdict.
   - **The check at execution.** Every member checks each KeyPackage a Commit at p adds against t_p: `not_after - t_p >= P + L` (608,100 seconds, 7 days + 55 minutes) and `t_p - not_before >= 3,000`. A request that executes is live, so t_N - t_e < W (decision 10), and t_N <= t_p. When t_p - t_e < W the first margin holds by the entry check, because `not_after - t_p > not_after - t_e - W >= P + L`, and the second holds because t_p >= t_e. The execution check can fail only when the Commit lands more than W after t_e while epoch N opened less than W after it. A request whose KeyPackage fails it is void at p, so no Commit at p executes it, and a Commit that executes it is invalid. This is the one place where the Commit's own t_p decides what a Commit at p must execute. An honest committer avoids the boundary: it builds no Commit while its clock c satisfies `b - σ - δ < c <= b + σ` for the boundary `b = not_after - 608,100` of any KeyPackage its prefix would reach, a band of 900 seconds, and outside that band it knows on which side of b t_p falls.
   - **The processable window.** openmls's own check passes at a member whose clock reads c when `not_before <= c < not_after`. A Commit that passes the execution check therefore passes openmls at every member that processes it within P of t_p with a clock at most L ahead of the node's and at most 3,000 seconds behind it: c < t_p + P + L <= `not_after`, and c >= t_p - 3,000 >= `not_before`.
   - **The requester.** A member that submits a request carrying a KeyPackage requires, under its own clock c, `KEY_PACKAGE_MIN_REMAINING_LIFETIME` (1,213,500 seconds, 14 days + 65 minutes) and `KEY_PACKAGE_MIN_NOT_BEFORE_AGE` (3,300 seconds). The first is the entry margin 1,212,900 plus σ plus δ: the node's clock may run σ ahead of c and the submission may take δ, so t_e <= c + σ + δ and `not_after - t_e >= 1,212,900`. The second is the entry margin 3,000 plus σ: the node's clock may run σ behind c, so t_e >= c - σ, and a submission delay only ages the KeyPackage. A request whose submission takes longer than δ can fail entry; it changes no state, and the requester re-files it with a fresh check.
   - **No caller time.** No SCP API takes a time from its caller for this check. The node-signed `append_time` is its only time input.
   - **Order of the checks.** The added KeyPackages travel inside the Commit's encrypted content, and openmls 0.9.0 decrypts and validates a Commit in one public call, `process_message`; its decrypt-only step, `unprotect_message`, is crate-private. SCP therefore runs the execution check on the staged Commit's Adds after `process_message` returns and before the merge. When `process_message` refuses the Commit with a lifetime error, the error names the bound that failed, `not_after` for `Expired` and `not_before` for `NotValidYet`, and the member applies the check to that bound: if the bound fails at t_p, the Commit is invalid, as it is at every member.
   - **The committer.** A committer that reaches its own Commit's position (decision 4) runs the same check against t_p before it merges its staged Commit, and discards the staged Commit when a KeyPackage fails, as every other member skips it.
   - **openmls stays unpatched.** openmls keeps its check against the system clock, at entry in `KeyPackageIn` validation and at execution in `process_message`. Outside the processable window that check can refuse a KeyPackage or a Commit that passes at t_e or t_p. Such a refusal is not a verdict. The member never skips that Commit, never takes a later Commit for that epoch and never drops that request from its queue. It stays at the state before and recovers into the group's state through Welcome-based fast-forward or a group state reset (`23-sync-and-offline-strategy.md` §23.4.1 source 3, §23.5), so it forks from no member.
   - **Closed: a Commit no honest member can process.** The open question of that name asked what happens when every member's openmls refuses a Commit that passed at t, as when a member adds a KeyPackage that expires seconds after t. The margins make such a Commit invalid at every member. A valid Commit is processable by every member inside the window, and a member outside it recovers through a member inside it. When no member at the Commit's state is reachable inside the window, the members re-found the context from the last state they verified (plan D9).
   - **Cost.** A device outside the window recovers through fast-forward or a reset. Two rulings of Alec's cover the two causes, each its own. For a clock that diverges from the node's, Alec on 2026-11-02, on item 6 of the design as presented to him: "6 is extremely normal. When system clocks diverge from remote ones, users almost always expect to see issues, both at the operating system and application level. This is a normal thing; it's not a failure mode on our part. If anything, it's desirable." For state more than 7 days stale, Alec chose on 2026-10-24 the 7-day message lifetime, whose option text read in part: "a device offline more than 7 days resets on reconnect under spec 23 §23.5.1" (`09-security-model.md` §9.10.2, Rulings).
   - **Trust in the node's time.** Members trust the node's signed `append_time`, as they already trust the node for order. Alec on 2026-11-02: "Generally trust less but if we are doing trust it's in contexts not clients." A decreasing time is provable: two entries whose times decrease are two signed objects (`09-security-model.md` §9.9.3). A time far in the future is attributable to the node's signature but not provable, because no member's clock is a verdict input. Through the monotone rule it pins every later entry, so every fresh KeyPackage fails the entry check, every request that carries one is refused, and no Add executes. That is a context fault on the same footing as a node that refuses every submission (`09-security-model.md` §9.9.4): a member resubmits under §9.9.4, acts on its own observation that its submissions take no effect, and re-founds the context under §9.9.3. No check reads a member's clock.
   - **Where the 7 days come from.** Alec ruled on 2026-10-24: "Just use the max number the current design supports right now and design for a higher number as well". The message lifetime T is 7 days because the relay maximum `MAX_BLOB_TTL` is 7 days: no relay keeps a message longer, and a device offline longer than T cannot read what it missed, so it resets under `23-sync-and-offline-strategy.md` §23.5.1 trigger 1, whose 7 days are set to T. W and P are set to T: a request outlives a member's absence up to T, and a member processes a Commit within T of t_p or resets. The margins follow from T, `2T + L` at entry and `T + L` at execution, and nothing sets T from the margins. A higher T needs relays that keep blobs longer (`MAX_BLOB_TTL`), a longer trigger 1, and a W and a P equal to the new T. The entry margin `2T + L` plus the 3,000-second age must fit inside `MAX_KEYPACKAGE_ATTESTATION_LIFETIME` (84 days + 1 hour), so T can grow to 3,627,450 seconds (just under 42 days) before that maximum must grow with it (`09-security-model.md` §9.10.2, Rulings).
10. **The request queue.** Alec ruled on 2026-11-02, when the starvation case was presented to him as an open question: "the whole point of context driven commit ordering and processing is to STOP starvation. No." Members enforce that ruling in their verdict, and the node enforces nothing (`07-trust-validation-and-capabilities.md` §7.3.1.1, "Commit order").
   - **Every action is a request.** Every action that needs a Commit enters the chain as a request: a governance request (an `AddMember` or `ResetMember` names its KeyPackage), a departure request, a reset request (the subject's `ResetRequest` with its KeyPackage and the leaf it replaces), a re-seat request, a key-history request, an own-device request, a checkpoint request, a revocation request, an outlet request, a leaf-removal request and an update request (`05-contexts.md` §5.9.1.1). Each carries everything its execution needs and authenticates its own author: a key-history request by the key event it carries, and every other kind by its author's `SCP-REQUEST-V1:` signature under the `active_key` of the author's recorded key position (`09-security-model.md` §9.5.2). That preimage binds the author's `member_since` and a `request_counter` above the author's `request_floor`, which every executed request of the author raises to its counter, so a signed request executes at most once and never in a later membership; a replayed departure, own-device Add or outlet registration is void. A governance request also carries the signed proposal and the signed votes that authorize its action, and the member that files it is its author. Governance proposals and votes are notices, not requests: they enter the chain sealed so that members read them across epochs, and a governance request carries them when the requirement is met.
   - **Sealing.** Every request and notice is sealed by HPKE (RFC 9180) to the context's request key. Every member derives the key pair of a period from the MLS exporter of the epoch that starts the period, so a member that the starting Commit removes cannot derive it. That epoch's group context cannot hold a key derived from its own secrets, so the `request_keys_hash` of `0xFF02` commits each period's public half from the next epoch on, and the signed GroupInfo chain item carries the public halves it commits (`05-contexts.md` §5.9.1.1, §5.13.3). Entry accepts an item sealed to the period current at its position or to the period before it, so a sender that holds only a GroupInfo whose group context commits the previous period still reaches the members. Whoever holds the public half submits directly to the node, including a member whose leaves a recovery cut off, so no request waits on a member that forwards it. The node sees opaque bytes and learns no kind. Members keep the private half of every period that ended less than W ago, so they open every request throughout W, and a Welcome carries those halves to a joiner.
   - **Entry.** A request enters the queue at its position q when, against the state at q that every member holds: it opens under the request key of the period current at q or of the period before it; it decodes as one kind; its author authenticates under the key current at the author's position, or at the position a key-history request ahead of it in the queue advances it to; its KeyPackage, when it carries one, passes decision 9's entry check and openmls's `KeyPackageIn` validation at t_e; no request with the same digest is in the queue; and its author has fewer than `MAX_PENDING_REQUESTS_PER_AUTHOR` (8) requests in the queue. A key-history request counts against no author's cap, because only the holder of an identity's keys can sign the event it carries and the event either advances the position or is void at execution. Members skip an item that fails entry.
   - **Execution.** Members evaluate the queue in chain order from its head, each request in the state that the live requests ahead of it produce. A request is void at epoch N when `t_N - t_e >= W`, when it fails any check that a Commit executing it applies in that state, or when it fails decision 9's execution check at t_p. Every state-dependent check runs at execution, so a request whose subject or proposer left, whose proposer lost the capability, whose author's key a later rotation retired, whose sending leaf is no longer current, whose proposal already executed, or whose KeyPackage collides with a key already in the tree is void when it reaches the head, and leaves. The execution checks include every leaf validity condition of RFC 9420 §7.3 against the tree, so no KeyPackage whose `signature_key` or `encryption_key` another leaf already holds reaches openmls.
   - **The rule.** A Commit for epoch N at position p is valid only when it executes the first k live requests of the queue, k >= 1, and nothing else. It drops every void request ahead of the k-th, and the queue's cursor moves to the k-th executed request. A Commit that executes no request is invalid. A run of k > 1 is admissible only for key-history requests within `MAX_KEY_ADVANCES_PER_COMMIT` and its byte cap, or for departure, reset, re-seat and update requests for distinct identities within the leaf limits; every other kind executes alone. Each request expands to one fixed proposal list, and members find k by matching the Commit's proposals to the expansion of the first k live requests.
   - **Authority is the author's.** A Commit that executes a request reads the request's author for every authorization: the proposer for a governance action, the token's issuer or the context's creator for a revocation, the holder of `outlet:register` for an outlet. The committer needs no capability. The committer stays the recorder: the log leaf carries its `actor_did` and its leaf signature, and the leaf's `timestamp` is the request's t_e, so every member derives the same leaf whoever commits. The UpdatePath keeps the committer's `signature_key` and credential and changes its HPKE `encryption_key`, so the committer re-issues its `0xFF03` attestation over the new `encryption_key` under the same `active_key` (`09-security-model.md` §9.7.3). A member replaces its own leaf, to rotate its `signature_key` or install a later key, only by an own-device request. A checkpoint request names its creator as its author and carries the full quorum, so any member records it; `CheckpointPayload` gains `creator_did`, and `MAX_GOVERNANCE_LEAF_BYTES` grows from 15,616 to 15,680 bytes to hold it.
   - **Group state.** `request_cursor`, the position of the last executed request, and `pending`, the SHA-256 digests in chain order of the requests that entered at positions after the cursor and before the position of the Commit that opened epoch N, are members of the governance state that `governance_state_hash` commits (`05-contexts.md` §5.13.3). A request that entered after that Commit carries forward to the next epoch's commitment.
   - **The joiner.** A Welcome joiner reads the bodies of the pending requests from the node's chain, opens them with the retained private halves the Welcome carries, and checks each against the digests under `governance_state_hash`. It evaluates the entries between the opening of epoch N and the Commit that added it itself, against the state at N that it rebuilds from the log (`23-sync-and-offline-strategy.md` §23.7). It needs no byte from its adder, and a body the node does not serve leaves the joiner unable to verify the next Commit, which is the node withholding chain items (`09-security-model.md` §9.9.2).
   - **The bound.** A request that enters at q with k live requests ahead of it executes or is void by the (k + 1)-th canonical Commit after q. Every canonical Commit executes at least the head's live request, and a request that enters after q stands behind it. The bound counts requests and Commits, whatever rate any member commits at, and reads no clock. The cap bounds k by eight times the number of authors with requests in the queue, plus key-history requests, each of which carries a key event its identity's key holder signed. The bound needs some member to commit, and any member can, because executing a request needs no capability.

## Consequences

- Two members that commit at one epoch no longer split the group, and no member keeps parent states, branch stores, a PPRF over child init secrets, a journal, a key ledger or an epoch table.
- The governance block body has four items, and the body bound is 32,077 bytes. Spec 25's fork-merge vectors (§25.33, Vectors 71 to 79) are removed, and the `ForkNotice` and `DepartureRequest` preimages and the fork record leave `09-security-model.md` §9.5.2.
- A context with its node off makes no progress: no member can commit until the node returns (ruling 4).
- A malformed or stale Commit costs a chain position and changes no member's state.
- A member trusts the node for liveness, for order, and for the `append_time` it signs, never for validity, because every member verifies every Commit and every request. A node that equivocates, drops a receipted submission, or signs a decreasing `append_time` is proven by its own signatures (Q4) and answered by re-founding (plan D9). A node that signs a time far in the future is attributable by the signature on that time, and members answer it as they answer a node that refuses submissions (decision 9).
- The node appends every submission in arrival order and learns no kind. A malformed request or Commit costs a chain position, and members skip it. Every Commit executes requests in queue order and nothing else, so no member's action waits on another member's choice to commit it, a re-filed request enters at the back, and the cap bounds the requests one author holds in the queue. An empty Commit is invalid; a member that wants an epoch advanced for post-compromise security submits an update request.
- Every chain entry carries the node-signed `append_time`, every member checks a request's KeyPackage at entry and each added KeyPackage at execution against it with decision 9's margins, and a device outside the processable window recovers through fast-forward or a reset (decision 9).
- A receipt names a position and carries no other verdict. No submission carries a field outside the MLS message, and no external Commit is valid.
- Withdrawal and re-signing by key rotation (`05-contexts.md` §5.9.1, ADR-066 decision 10) rest on a change of the member's `signature_key`, which is now an own-device request that removes the member's leaf and adds its new KeyPackage, because an UpdatePath keeps the committer's `signature_key` (decision 10). `propose_update` (`crates/scp-mls/src/ratchet.rs`) passes `LeafNodeParameters::default()` and rotates no `signature_key` either.
- This record takes in the part of plan decision D6 it needs: governance proposals, governance votes and requests are sealed chain items that members read throughout W (decision 10). D6's ordering of the other application messages in place of ADR-051's causal DAG is not changed by this record, and the artifacts that describe the DAG stay as they are.

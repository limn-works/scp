# ADR-069: Node-Ordered Commits

**Status:** Accepted 2026-11-02. Alec settled the design on 2026-10-25 (decisions D1 to D3 and question Q4 of the context-operation plan), and ruled "Rewrite now" on 2026-11-02 for the artifacts that ADR-067 had shaped. **Amended 2026-11-02:** decision 10 records Alec's ruling that the node's order must stop starvation. Members enforce it by a queue of requests that every member verifies. Every action that needs a Commit enters the chain as a sealed, self-authenticating request. A Commit names a cover position and executes the longest run of live requests that entered before it and fits, and a request with k live requests ahead of it executes or is void within k + 2 Commits. Two earlier drafts of this amendment, priority holds and then a queue with a non-empty prefix, per-period request keys, a fixed request lifetime W and an exempt key-history request, were discarded after review; options 6, 7 and 8 of the starvation options record why. Decision 2 keeps D2's class order, which Alec reaffirmed on 2026-11-02 after a draft of this amendment dropped it. Decision 8 has a current member submit the re-seat of a member whose devices lost their leaves, from that member's key-event log and published KeyPackage, which Alec accepted on 2026-11-02, so the context has no intake key. Decision 9 records the design Alec accepted for checking a KeyPackage's lifetime against the time the node signs into the chain, with entry and execution margins. Decision 10 seals each item under a symmetric key of its epoch, with the epoch and the class in the clear.

**Supersedes:** ADR-067, Concurrent Commits and fork merge, in `phase-6.md`. ADR-067's status flips to `Superseded by ADR-069`, and its body stays as the historical record.

**Amends:** ADR-066 decisions 5, 10, 32 and 40 and its acceptance-criteria paragraph; ADR-031 §7 and its 2026-10-17 amendment; ADR-029 §5b, §5c and §6 step 4, and its rationale paragraph on conflict resolution; ADR-001 criterion 6; ADR-051 §3, "No trusted sequencer"; ADR-057's KeyPackage lifetime rule. Each amended record carries a dated amendment that names this record.

**Spec:** `07-trust-validation-and-capabilities.md` §7.3.1 and §7.3.1.1 ("Competing Commits", "The re-seat", "Commit order"), `05-contexts.md` §5.9.1, §5.9.1.1, §5.12.3.3, §5.12.3.4 and §5.13.3 ("Governance state", rules 11, 13, 15 and 16), `09-security-model.md` §9.5.2, §9.7.1, §9.7.2, §9.9.1 to §9.9.4, §9.10.2, §9.11, §9.14 and §9.18, `10-infrastructure-and-self-hosting.md` §10.2, §10.3 and §10.8.1, `23-sync-and-offline-strategy.md` §23.4, §23.5 and §23.7, `25-test-vectors.md` §25.23 (Vectors 81 and 82 withdrawn) and §25.33, and `00-open-questions.md` ("The intake key", "Dropping the D2 class order").

## Context

`admit_and_merge_own_commit` (`crates/scp-mls/src/group.rs`) merges the committer's own Commit before anyone orders it. When two members commit at one epoch N, each merges its own Commit, and each member's openmls then rejects the other Commit as `WrongEpoch`, so the group splits.

ADR-066 decision 5 had answered the race with "Epoch order decides conflicts": a Commit that loses the race for an epoch never merges, and its executor re-checks it and sends it again. That rule needs one order of Commits that every member reads. On 2026-10-24 no party could give that order, because Alec had ruled "We can’t rely on daily presence" and the relays were untrusted store-and-forward services. ADR-067 therefore let every member keep both children of an epoch, choose one by the least (rank, cth), and re-apply the losing child's changes through fork records, imports and a merge function G. It needed sixteen mechanisms (M1 to M16), a fifth body item `merge` in the governance block, a journal, a key ledger, an epoch table, branch retention for up to seven days, a PPRF over child init secrets, two signed notice records, and ten accepted residuals.

On 2026-10-25 Alec settled how a context runs (`~/.claude/plans/context-operation-plan.md`, rulings 1 to 10):

- "Contexts most certainly have operators, and in fact, require them." "Nodes are pieces of infrastructure that operate many things. One of those things could absolutely be a specific context."
- "if a context runs on a single node and that node is off then the context is unreachable. Se la vie."
- "The context is the engine for finalization." "Why would governance be involved? It's an immutable part of the protocol".
- D1: "One node runs a context and is its MLS Delivery Service: it totally orders submitted Commits, one per epoch. Members verify every Commit."
- D2: Alec wrote "So we also submit right to nodes and that solves the issue". The plan recorded D2 as members submitting Commits directly to the context's node, with the priority class (Remove, re-join, other) as a field of the submission outside the MLS message, ordered "Remove first, then re-join, then arrival." Decision 2 keeps both. On 2026-11-02 Alec reaffirmed the class order: "But nodes need to process using more than just received ordering that is the entire point of having contexts process… so we can prioritize specific commit types".
- D3: "The node holds no MLS group secrets."
- Q4: the node signs each chain entry (position, item hash, previous hash) and returns a signed receipt that assigns a position to each accepted submission, under a key the operator binds to its identifier when the context is instantiated.

A node that runs the context is online whenever the context is reachable, so the ordering party that ADR-067 lacked exists. The question this record answers is how members turn the node's order into one Commit per epoch when the node cannot read a Commit, and how they keep a member that commits nonstop from starving any other member's action.

## Options considered

1. **Keep ADR-067 and add the node as a relay.** Every mechanism of ADR-067 stays, and the node only delivers. This lost because the node already gives a total order, so every mechanism that exists to merge two orders protects against a case the node removes. ADR-067's own necessity table justifies each mechanism by a fork, and a fork needs two Commits that two members both merge.
2. **The node accepts one Commit per epoch and refuses the rest.** The node reads the epoch from the `PrivateMessage` framing (RFC 9420 §6.3, which leaves `epoch` in the clear), appends the first Commit it receives for the current epoch, refuses the rest, and then expects the next epoch. This lost because of D3: the node holds no group secret, so it cannot verify a Commit. A member that sends a malformed Commit for epoch N would take epoch N's place, every member would reject it, and the node would refuse every honest Commit for epoch N as stale, so the context would stop. The node also cannot prove a refusal (Q4: "Refusal to accept is provable to no one").
3. **The node orders every submission, and members take the first valid Commit for each epoch.** The node appends every submission it accepts to one signed chain. Every member processes the chain in position order, and the canonical Commit for epoch N is the first Commit in the chain that names epoch N and passes the member's verification at epoch N. This option is chosen. It meets D1 ("one per epoch", "Members verify every Commit") and D3, because the choice reads only the chain order and each member's own verdict, and ADR-066 decision 10 makes that verdict a function of the Commit's bytes and the state every member holds identically.
4. **A member quorum or a witness set signs the order.** Members or witness nodes would sign which Commit comes first. This lost because Alec ruled on 2026-10-25 that "The context is the engine for finalization" and asked of governance "Why would governance be involved? It's an immutable part of the protocol", so no member, witness or governance action takes part in ordering.

### Options considered for starvation (decision 10, 2026-11-02)

A Commit built for epoch N loses to another member's Commit for N, is rebuilt for N + 1, and arrives after that member's Commit for N + 1, because the other member builds its next Commit while the first is still processing the chain. Under any rule that lets a member's own Commit carry an action that the member alone rebuilds after each loss, a member that commits nonstop wins every epoch, and every other member's action waits for ever.

1. **Append on arrival, with no rule.** The node holds at most one submission at a time, so D2's priority order never acts, and a member that commits nonstop starves every other action. This lost to Alec's ruling of 2026-11-02.
2. **A fixed batching window on the node's clock.** The node collects submissions for a fixed time and appends each batch in D2's order. This lost because the window bounds nothing: a member whose rebuild takes longer than the window loses every epoch to a committer whose next Commit arrives inside the window.
3. **A first-in, first-out queue of requests that every Commit must execute.** Every action that needs a Commit enters the chain as a request: a sealed chain item that carries everything its execution needs and authenticates its own signer. Each Commit names a cover position b and executes the longest run of live requests at the head of the queue that entered before b and fits, a governance action only alone. The committer chooses only b, and b cannot pass the Commit's own position. This option is chosen. The node reads nothing it cannot read (D3) and orders by D2's class, then arrival (decision 2). A member that commits nonstop can only advance the queue. A request waits for at most the live requests ahead of it at entry, a number the caps fix at entry, plus two Commits, whatever rate any member commits at, and the bound reads no member's clock (decision 10).
4. **Class-ranked pending requests.** Only a Remove-class or re-join-class action entered the chain as a request, and a Commit had to execute the first executable request in D2's order, Remove first, then re-join, then position; while no request was executable, any Commit was valid. The first fix round of 2026-11-02 chose it, and its check replaced it. Every other action, an Add, a key-history advance and the record of a recovery that a re-join needs among them, stayed a Commit its author rebuilt, so a member that committed nonstop starved it. While a request was executable, a Commit executed nothing else, and a member that filed a fresh self-reset each time its previous one executed kept every other action out of the chain for as long as it chose.
5. **A hold that any later priority submission ends.** The node cannot verify a submission's class, so a committer that wants to starve a Remove sends one malformed Remove-class submission, the hold ends, and the committer's Commit is appended first. This lost for that reason.
6. **A hold that only the lost submission's own submitter ends, capped by the node's clock.** The node would hold lower-class submissions after a Remove-class or re-join-class submission lost its epoch, until the submitter revealed the secret behind a commitment the lost submission carried, or until `PRIORITY_HOLD_SECS` (120 seconds) passed on the node's clock. The first draft of this amendment chose it, and the review of that draft on 2026-11-02 replaced it. The node cannot verify a class, so each malformed Remove-class submission delays every lower-class submission by 120 seconds with no bound on their number; the class field tells the node which submissions remove or re-join members; the bound held only for a submitter whose rebuilt Commit arrived within 120 seconds; and the hold secret was a bearer secret.
7. **A queue whose Commits execute any non-empty prefix, with a fixed request lifetime.** The second fix round chose option 3 in this form: a Commit executed any k ≥ 1 of the oldest live requests; a request was void a fixed W (604,800 seconds) after its own entry; a key-history request counted against no cap, because only its identity's key holder signs the event it carries; and an empty Commit was invalid, so an update request existed to advance epochs. Its check found the findings growing, and the design was discarded. A hostile committer could drain one request per Commit. The exemption let one member stage key-history requests for itself and for every identity it staged, so k had no bound at entry, and the fixed W then turned an unbounded wait into a void. The update request healed the committer's leaf, not its author's. The cover position of option 3, the caps with no exemption (decision 10), the signed `not_after` in place of W, and empty Commits replace it.
8. **Per-period request keys that a GroupInfo names.** The second fix round sealed every item to a request key that rotated daily and at every leaf removal, committed by `request_keys_hash` in `0xFF02`, published in a GroupInfo chain item (`0xFF05`), and carried to joiners in `0xFF06`, with entry accepting the current or the previous period, replays stopped by a digest check, and the floor set to the highest executed counter. It lost. A member that removed and re-added leaves rotated periods faster than an honest sender's item reached the chain, so entry by period count could be starved. A stale requester authenticated the GroupInfo under a leaf of the state it last held, which a removed member or a thief also holds. An ECDSA high-s copy or a re-encoded copy escaped the digest check, and a floor set only at execution let copies fill the cap. Decision 10 derives each epoch's key from that epoch's MLS exporter, measures the window in node time, and checks a per-key counter at entry; decision 8 has the stale requester submit nothing.
9. **A node-signed intake key per epoch.** The orchestrator drafted it on 2026-11-02 and withdrew it the same day, because a junk Commit carrying an attacker's key gets the node's signature as the canonical Commit does, so the stale requester could seal to the attacker.
10. **A context intake key that a requester with no group state seals to.** The re-derived queue sealed a re-seat request, and a reset request from a device that held no window key, under one context key, with three candidates: a genesis key pair whose private half every Welcome delivers, the node's key, or a current member that forwards the request. Alec rejected the genesis key on 2026-11-02: "I don't want to accept this privacy residual." Every member removed after genesis holds that private half and reads the identity, the KeyPackage and the timing of every later request sealed under it. The node's key lets the operator read them. A forwarding member may be a removed member that the requester cannot tell from a current one. No key escapes all three: a stale requester holds no secret of an epoch after its last state, so every key it can authenticate is one a removed member also holds or can post, or one a single party vouches for. Decision 8 drops the premise that the requester submits anything.
11. **One HPKE seal shape with no key identifier, opened by trial decryption.** The re-derived queue sealed every item in HPKE to an epoch key pair `DeriveKeyPair(MLS-Exporter("SCP request key"))`, gave every item one shape with no key identifier, and had members try each key they held, so the node learned neither the kind nor which key sealed an item. Its two premises expired on 2026-11-02. A public-key seal helps only a sealer that holds the public half and not the private half, which was the intake requester, and decision 8 removes that requester. Hiding the kind contradicts D2, which puts the class in the clear for the node, and Alec answered on 2026-11-02 "I don't think revealing the commit type would be much of an issue. Do you?". Decision 10 keeps what the queue still needs: a joiner must open the items sealed before it joined and nothing else, and an item must stay openable for a window measured in node time. MLS framing meets neither. openmls keeps past epoch secrets by a count of epochs (`max_past_epochs`), which a flood of epochs defeats, and only the epoch's secret tree would let a joiner open pre-join items, which opens all of that epoch's application traffic.

## Decision

1. **The node orders.** The node that runs a context is its MLS Delivery Service, the role RFC 9750 (the MLS architecture) gives the service that orders and delivers a group's messages. A member sends each Commit and each sealed item it makes to that node, at the address the context's `ContextNodeAddress` names. The node appends every submission it accepts to the context's chain at the next position, in the class order of decision 2, signs the chain entry with its `append_time` (decision 9), and returns a signed receipt naming the position (Q4). The node holds no MLS group secret (D3), so it never decides whether a Commit or a request is valid.
2. **The class order.** Every submission carries a class that the node reads, outside the MLS message and outside the sealed body: Remove, re-join or other (`07-trust-validation-and-capabilities.md` §7.3.1.1, "Submission"). A Commit's submission is the class and the MLS bytes; a sealed item carries its class in its clear header (decision 10). Among the submissions it has accepted and not yet appended, the node appends next one of the highest class it holds, Remove first, then re-join, then other, and within one class the one that arrived first. "Highest" means first in that order, so the highest class is the lowest code: Remove `0`, re-join `1`, other `2`. The order covers sealed requests and the Commits that execute them. A request's class follows from its kind (`05-contexts.md` §5.9.1.1), a notice's class is other, and a Commit's class is the highest class among the requests its executed run holds, other when the run is empty. In that run a governance action that removes a member (`RemoveMember`) counts as Remove, a `ResetMember` action counts as re-join, and every other governance action counts as other.
   - **Admission before the order.** The node authenticates no submitter, so a flood of junk labelled Remove would, under the order alone, keep every re-join and other submission off the chain until each passed the entry window. The node therefore admits a submission before it orders it, under three rules (`07-trust-validation-and-capabilities.md` §7.3.1.1, "Submission"; `09-security-model.md` §9.18.11). It holds at most `NODE_CLASS_BACKLOG` (4,096) unappended submissions per class in one context. When an arrival finds its class full, the node evicts one unappended submission of that class from the connection that holds the most of that class, counting the arrival, ties going against the connection opened latest, and evicts that connection's latest arrival; the arrival itself is refused only when its own connection holds the most. Connections cost an attacker nothing and the node can open no sealed item, so refusing every arrival to a full class let junk from 64 free connections keep every honest submission of that class out, a `SeatConfirmation` among them, until provisional seats expired and members re-founded the context; under eviction a connection loses nothing to capacity while another connection holds more of its class. It holds at most `NODE_CONNECTION_BACKLOG` (64) unappended submissions from one connection in one context, and accepts at most `NODE_CONNECTION_RATE` (16) submissions a second from one connection in one context, refusing the rest. It appends in cycles of four: the first two appends of a cycle take the highest class it holds, the third takes the earliest re-join submission when it holds one, and the fourth takes the earliest other submission when it holds one; a slot whose class is empty takes the highest class it holds. The class order therefore decides which submission goes first and never stalls a class: re-join and other each receive at least one append in four while they hold a submission. A flood delays an honest submission by the share of appends it captures, and takes backlog from an honest connection only when it holds no more per connection than that connection does. A refused submitter resubmits, from a new connection or through another member (`09-security-model.md` §9.9.4). The chain holds each submission as the node received it, class included, so every member checks the class: an item whose class is not its kind's fails entry and takes no place under any cap, and a Commit whose class is not its executed run's is invalid. The node cannot check a class, so a member that labels a submission above its class gains a place at the node ahead of submissions that arrived with it, and then its item or Commit fails at every member and executes nothing. The class order decides the order in which items enter; the queue of decision 10 then runs in entry order with no rank, so its bound holds for every request, and no request kept pending holds other requests back (option 4).
   - **Alec's ruling.** Alec settled the class order in D2 on 2026-10-25. On 2026-11-02, after a draft of this amendment dropped it, he reaffirmed it: "But nodes need to process using more than just received ordering that is the entire point of having contexts process… so we can prioritize specific commit types". The drop is withdrawn.
   - **Disclosure.** D2 places the class where the node reads it, so the node learns which submissions remove a member, which re-join one, and the highest class each Commit executes, with each submission's arrival time and address (`09-security-model.md` §9.9.1). The chain carries the class for members to check, so every party that reads the chain learns the same classes. The class names no member and no context content.
3. **One Commit per epoch.** Every member processes the chain in position order. At epoch N, the canonical Commit is the first chain item that is a Commit naming epoch N and that passes steps 1 and 2 of `07-trust-validation-and-capabilities.md` §7.3.1.1 "Receiver processing" in the member's state at epoch N, with the request queue at the Commit's cover position. A member skips every other Commit: a Commit naming an epoch below its current one, and a Commit naming its current epoch that fails verification. A skipped Commit changes no member's state. Because every member's verdict reads only the Commit, the chain items before it, the `append_time` values the node signed into the chain (decision 9), and state that every member holds identically (ADR-066 decision 10), every honest member merges the same Commit at every epoch.
4. **The committer merges its own Commit at its position.** A committer keeps its own Commit staged and unmerged after it submits it. It merges the staged Commit when, processing the chain in order, it reaches the position its receipt names, its group is still at the epoch the Commit names, and the Commit passes steps 1 and 2 at that position. Otherwise it discards the staged Commit, which changed no member's state. A Commit executes only requests from the queue (decision 10), so a Commit that is not first loses nothing: each request it carried stays in the queue until a canonical Commit executes it or it becomes void, and the protocol obliges no rebuild and no resubmit. The committer never merges a Commit that the chain does not hold.
5. **Conflicts are serial.** "Committed first" in ADR-031 §7 means earlier in the chain. The conflict rule of §7.3.1.1 "Conflicts" and the executed-proposal set settle every pair of actions in queue order, and no merge function runs. The `member:X` conflict key stays: the governance state keeps `change_seq["member:X"]` after X leaves, so an Add of X anchored before a Remove of X loses to that Remove, and an invitation anchored after the Remove passes.
6. **The governance block has four body items.** The body loses item 5, `merge`, and with it the fork record, the import leaves, the re-applications and the carried re-seats. The body bound returns to 1 + 168 + 3 + 16,224 + 15,680 + 1 = 32,077 bytes, 687 bytes below the 32,764-byte body (`07-trust-validation-and-capabilities.md` §7.3.1.1, "Why 32,768 bytes and 96 approvals"). The requests one Commit executes share that body: `MAX_REQUEST_BYTES_PER_COMMIT` is 32,764 bytes.
7. **No merge state.** The journal, the key ledger, the epoch table, the fork records, the imports, the pending joiners and the re-seat windows leave the governance state, and `merge_state_hash` leaves the `0xFF02` extension (`05-contexts.md` §5.13.3 rule 16 is withdrawn). A Commit with an empty block again carries the GroupContextExtensions proposal with its extensions unchanged (ADR-066 decision 32).
8. **Members re-seat a member that holds no current leaf, and that member submits nothing.** A member X that holds at least one leaf and no current leaf returns by a re-seat request that a current member submits for it (`05-contexts.md` §5.9.1.1, kind `0x06`; `09-security-model.md` §9.7.1, "The re-seat"). The trigger is group state: a recorded key event of X, a `RootRecovery` or a rotation of the key that attested X's leaves, leaves X no current leaf. Every member whose leaf is current then holds a slot at which it fetches one of X's published KeyPackages, attested under X's current key, and submits the request sealed under an item key of its current epoch. The request takes X's one re-seat slot, whoever submits it, and no place under the submitter's cap. The first canonical Commit whose executed run holds it removes every leaf of X that is not current, Adds the KeyPackage, and sends X the Welcome, whoever commits it, and X learns the outcome from that Welcome. The seated leaf is provisional until an item it signs enters the chain. Every external Commit is rejected, and the external re-seat, the re-seat wrap, `ReseatGroupInfo`, the GroupInfo with `external_pub`, rank −1, the re-seat window, the `D_MAX` send rule, the carried re-seat, the unused carried seat, the GroupInfo chain item, and the re-derived queue's re-seat request that X signed and sealed itself, with its own signature preimage, are removed.
   - **Alec's ruling.** On 2026-11-02 Alec rejected the context intake key, "I don’t want to accept this privacy residual." (starvation option 10), and answered "Sure..?" to "should a member who lost their state get back in when another member submits the re-seat request for them".
   - **Why X submits nothing.** Any item X appends must open for current members and not for a member removed after X's last state, and X holds no secret of a later epoch, so every key X can authenticate is one a removed member also holds or can post, or one a single party vouches for (option 10). Members need three things from X, and all three are public before any re-seat: X's recovered keys, in X's key-event log; a KeyPackage, in X's published buffer; and X's consent to that device holding a leaf, which the KeyPackage's `0xFF03` attestation under X's current key carries.
   - **Authority is X's.** X's key event removed X's old leaves from currency, and X's current key attested the device the Commit adds. A hostile member can neither start a re-seat, which needs X's recorded key event, nor seat a device X did not attest. The submitter also chooses which of X's attested KeyPackages the request carries. A choice of a KeyPackage whose `init_key` X already consumed costs X one `RESEAT_CONFIRM_WINDOW`, because the leaf never confirms (below). Which party chooses the KeyPackage, so that no submitter can seat a device X keeps out of this context, is open (`00-open-questions.md`, "Who chooses the re-seat KeyPackage"): every designation X can publish without group state names the context to any party that knows the context's identifier, which is the residual Alec rejected for the intake key.
   - **X's devices act first after a plain rotation.** After every plain rotation, whatever recoveries X's head chain holds below it, the trigger waits `LEAF_REPLACEMENT_GRACE` (86,400 seconds) from the `KeyPositionRecorded` leaf that recorded the rotation, so X's own devices replace their leaves by own-device requests before any member re-seats X (`09-security-model.md` §9.7.1, trigger item 6). The trigger skips the wait only when every leaf X holds is cut off by the latest recovery, because that recovery cut off devices X may not control. An earlier draft skipped the wait whenever the head chain held any `RootRecovery`, so after one recovery every later rotation of X let any member re-seat X at once, ahead of X's own devices.
   - **A reset without a window key.** The reset request (kind `0x05`) serves only a device whose last epoch closed within `REQUEST_ENTRY_WINDOW`, because only such a device holds a key of the window; it submits its reset under that key. Every other device that lost its group state, every device offline past the 7-day trigger of `23-sync-and-offline-strategy.md` §23.5.1 among them, submits nothing. X's standing root rotates the key that attested the device's leaf, the rotation `09-security-model.md` §9.12 prescribes for leaf-state compromise, and the device publishes KeyPackages under the new key. Learners record the rotation. Each device of X that still holds group state then replaces its own stale leaf by an own-device request, signed by a current leaf of X or, when X holds none, by X's current `#active` (decision 10). When X still holds no current leaf once the grace has passed, members re-seat X (`23-sync-and-offline-strategy.md` §23.5.2).
   - **No single party blocks it.** A learner records X's key event by a key-history request at its slot (`09-security-model.md` §9.7.1), and any member submits the re-seat at its slot. Only the committer can make the Welcome (RFC 9420 §12.4.3.1), so a committer that withholds or spoils it, or crashes before it sends it, would otherwise leave X holding a current leaf it cannot use, with the trigger closed. The re-seated leaf therefore stays provisional: the governance state records it with the t_b of the Commit that seated it, an item it signs that enters confirms it (any kind, a `SeatConfirmation` notice, kind `0x0C`, among them), and a provisional leaf that no entered item confirms within `RESEAT_CONFIRM_WINDOW` (7,200 seconds) of that t_b is not current. The trigger then holds for X again, the next re-seat removes that leaf with X's other stale leaves, and the failed re-seat does not count against "One re-seat per recovery". A hostile committer must win every such round, and the chain shows which committer seated each leaf that expired. No single member, no removed member, and the node only by refusing every member's submission (`09-security-model.md` §9.9.4), keeps X out. Both requests rest on some current member acting, the liveness assumption every request already carries. A reset re-add seats a provisional leaf by the same rule.
   - **The local gate.** Executing the re-seat is a verdict every member reaches from the queue, not a local act, so the `PendingReverify` gate of `09-security-model.md` §9.11 does not reach the re-seat Commit or the submission duty. §9.11 sets that gate only on a `RootRecovery` or a Contested verdict. On the recovery path, trigger item 4 requires the recorded `FallbackConfirmation` that R4 makes the social check of a recovery. On the rotation path, with no recovery, no gate is set, and a recorded rotation under X's current key needs no social check.
   - **The Welcome.** The committer delivers the Welcome HPKE-wrapped to the `scp_wrapping_key` that the KeyPackage's attestation binds, at X's invitations routing ID, as `05-contexts.md` §5.12.3.1 and §5.12.3.3 deliver every Welcome, so a party that fetched X's KeyPackages reads no `KeyPackageRef` from it.
   - **Costs.** A reset without a window key needs X's standing root to sign the rotation. Under a shared `#active`, that rotation retires the leaves of every device of X in every context X belongs to, so each of X's devices replaces its leaf by an own-device request in each context within the grace, or is re-seated. X is re-seated whether or not it wants to return, and leaves afterwards by a departure request. The queue bound rises by one slot per member, to k ≤ 26n_q + 1.
   - **Two rulings whose premise expired.** On 2026-10-21 Alec confirmed an agent's statement that a re-joiner whose Commit loses "rebuilds its Commit against the new epoch and sends it again"; his words were "Confirm. Why shouldn’t she be able to? Why even ask me or suggest something else?" On 2026-11-02 he answered "Ok" to the recommendation that a node receipt carry a `lost` flag for an external re-seater. Both premises were an external re-seat Commit that only the re-seater could build and that could lose an epoch. X now commits nothing, the members execute its re-seat, and X learns the outcome from the Welcome. "Rulings this design departs from" records both.
9. **A KeyPackage's lifetime is checked against the node's append time, with margins, and SCP carries no openmls patch.** The choice of decision 3 requires every member that processes a Commit to reach one verdict on it, and a lifetime check against each member's own clock gives two members two verdicts. Alec accepted the design below on 2026-11-02 ("Accepted."), except the margins and their sizes, which the reviews of 2026-11-02 added after his acceptance to close the case that the "Closed" bullet below names. It replaces the bound that followed his 2026-10-04 answer "no patch, bound, leave us a note for the happy future path", under which a member whose clock ran more than one hour ahead of the adder's, or a member that processed a Commit more than 7 days after it was sent, refused a Commit that the other members merged. Earlier on 2026-11-02 he had rejected an openmls patch: "you offered me a solution that did not rely on a patch. What has changed since then that makes you feel like we need one?"
   - **Append time.** The chain entry the node signs for each position carries `append_time`, the node's clock in Unix seconds when it appended the item (`09-security-model.md` §9.9.3). This is a requirement on the chain entry's format, whose bytes no spec defines yet. Each entry's `append_time` MUST be at least the previous entry's. A member that finds a lower one holds two signed entries that prove the node signed a bad time, processes nothing past the lower entry, and the members re-found the context (plan D9).
   - **Three times.** t_q is the `append_time` of entry q, the position at which a request entered. For a Commit at position p with cover position b, t_b is the `append_time` of entry b − 1, which the committer has seen when it builds, and t_p is the Commit's own `append_time`, which no one knows before the node appends it.
   - **The numbers.** T is the message lifetime, `MAX_BLOB_TTL`, 604,800 seconds: a request's signed `not_after` is at most T after t_q. P is the processing window, set to T: a member processes the Commit at p within P of t_p. L is 3,300 seconds, the clock lead a member may hold over the node (55 minutes). `ENTRY_SLACK` is 3,600 seconds, the time a Commit may take to reach the chain after t_b. σ is 300 seconds, the clock-skew tolerance of `09-security-model.md` §9.14. δ is 300 seconds, the submission delay an honest submitter allows.
   - **The check at entry.** A request that carries a KeyPackage enters the queue only when, against t_q, `t_q - not_before >= 3,000` seconds (50 minutes), the KeyPackage's `not_after` is at least the request's own `not_after` plus P + L (608,100 seconds) plus `ENTRY_SLACK`, `not_before < not_after`, `not_after - not_before` is no greater than `MAX_KEYPACKAGE_ATTESTATION_LIFETIME` (`09-security-model.md` §9.7.1, KeyPackage `Lifetime` checks), and openmls's public `KeyPackageIn` validation passes. Every member reads the same t_q, so every member reaches one entry verdict.
   - **The check at execution.** Every member checks each KeyPackage a Commit at p adds against t_p: `not_after - t_p >= P + L` (608,100 seconds, 7 days + 55 minutes) and `t_p - not_before >= 3,000`. A request the Commit executes is live, so its `not_after` is above t_b. When t_p − t_b < `ENTRY_SLACK`, the first margin holds by the entry check, because `kp.not_after - t_p > kp.not_after - not_after - ENTRY_SLACK >= P + L`, and the second holds because t_p >= t_q. A Commit that reaches the chain `ENTRY_SLACK` or more after t_b can fail the check; it is then invalid at every member, and the next committer picks a later cover position, so nothing freezes. The committer knows t_b and so knows every void it must list.
   - **The processable window.** openmls's own check passes at a member whose clock reads c when `not_before <= c < not_after`. A Commit that passes the execution check therefore passes openmls at every member that processes it within P of t_p with a clock at most L ahead of the node's and at most 3,000 seconds behind it: c < t_p + P + L <= `not_after`, and c >= t_p - 3,000 >= `not_before`.
   - **The requester.** A member that submits a request carrying a KeyPackage refuses the KeyPackage under its own clock c unless `not_before <= c < not_after`, `c - not_before >= KEY_PACKAGE_MIN_NOT_BEFORE_AGE` (3,300 seconds, the entry margin 3,000 plus σ), and the range is within the maximum. It sets the request's `not_after` at most 611,700 seconds (P + L + `ENTRY_SLACK`) before the KeyPackage's `not_after`, at most c + T − 600 and at least c + 600, where 600 seconds is σ + δ; it refuses a KeyPackage that leaves no such value. `KEY_PACKAGE_MIN_REMAINING_LIFETIME` is retired. A request whose submission takes longer than δ can fail entry; it changes no state, and the requester submits a new one with a higher counter.
   - **No caller time.** No SCP API takes a time from its caller for this check. The node-signed `append_time` is its only time input.
   - **Order of the checks.** The added KeyPackages travel inside the Commit's encrypted content, and openmls 0.9.0 decrypts and validates a Commit in one public call, `process_message`; its decrypt-only step, `unprotect_message`, is crate-private. SCP therefore runs the execution check on the staged Commit's Adds after `process_message` returns and before the merge. When `process_message` refuses the Commit with a lifetime error, the error names the bound that failed, `not_after` for `Expired` and `not_before` for `NotValidYet`, and the member applies the check to that bound: if the bound fails at t_p, the Commit is invalid, as it is at every member.
   - **The committer.** A committer that reaches its own Commit's position (decision 4) runs the same check against t_p before it merges its staged Commit, and discards the staged Commit when a KeyPackage fails, as every other member skips it.
   - **openmls stays unpatched.** openmls keeps its check against the system clock, at entry in `KeyPackageIn` validation and at execution in `process_message`. Outside the processable window that check can refuse a KeyPackage or a Commit that passes at t_q or t_p. Such a refusal is not a verdict. The member never skips that Commit, never takes a later Commit for that epoch and never drops that request from its queue. It stays at the state before and recovers into the group's state through Welcome-based fast-forward or a group state reset (`23-sync-and-offline-strategy.md` §23.4.1 source 3, §23.5), so it forks from no member.
   - **Closed: a Commit no honest member can process.** The open question of that name asked what happens when every member's openmls refuses a Commit that passed at the node's time, as when a member adds a KeyPackage that expires seconds after it. The margins make such a Commit invalid at every member. A valid Commit is processable by every member inside the window, and a member outside it recovers through a member inside it. When no member at the Commit's state is reachable inside the window, the members re-found the context from the last state they verified (plan D9).
   - **Cost.** A device outside the window recovers through fast-forward or a reset. Two rulings of Alec's cover the two causes, each its own. For a clock that diverges from the node's, Alec on 2026-11-02, on item 6 of the design as presented to him: "6 is extremely normal. When system clocks diverge from remote ones, users almost always expect to see issues, both at the operating system and application level. This is a normal thing; it's not a failure mode on our part. If anything, it's desirable." For state more than 7 days stale, Alec chose on 2026-10-24 the 7-day message lifetime, whose option text read in part: "a device offline more than 7 days resets on reconnect under spec 23 §23.5.1" (`09-security-model.md` §9.10.2, Rulings).
   - **Trust in the node's time.** Members trust the node's signed `append_time`, as they already trust the node for order. Alec on 2026-11-02: "Generally trust less but if we are doing trust it's in contexts not clients." A decreasing time is provable: two entries whose times decrease are two signed objects (`09-security-model.md` §9.9.3). A time far in the future is attributable to the node's signature but not provable, because no member's clock is a verdict input. Through the monotone rule it pins every later entry, so every fresh KeyPackage fails the entry check, every request that carries one is refused, and no Add executes. That is a context fault on the same footing as a node that refuses every submission (`09-security-model.md` §9.9.4): a member resubmits under §9.9.4, acts on its own observation that its submissions take no effect, and re-founds the context under §9.9.3. No check reads a member's clock.
   - **Where the 7 days come from.** Alec ruled on 2026-10-24: "Just use the max number the current design supports right now and design for a higher number as well". T is 7 days because the blob maximum `MAX_BLOB_TTL` is 7 days: no node keeps a message longer, the node keeps chain items for T, and a device offline longer than T cannot read what it missed, so it resets under `23-sync-and-offline-strategy.md` §23.5.1 trigger 1, whose 7 days are set to T. T bounds a request's `not_after`, and P is set to T. No protocol constant below T sets a request's lifetime: the requester chooses `not_after` up to T. A higher T needs nodes that keep blobs longer, a longer trigger 1, and a P equal to the new T. A KeyPackage for a request with the longest `not_after` needs 2T + 9,900 seconds of lifetime (T, then P + L, then `ENTRY_SLACK`, then the 3,000-second age), which fits inside `MAX_KEYPACKAGE_ATTESTATION_LIFETIME` (84 days + 1 hour) up to T = 3,625,650 seconds, just under 42 days; a larger T needs a longer attestation lifetime too (`09-security-model.md` §9.10.2, Rulings).
10. **The request queue.** Alec ruled on 2026-11-02, when the starvation case was presented to him as an open question: "the whole point of context driven commit ordering and processing is to STOP starvation. No." Members enforce that ruling in their verdict, and the node enforces nothing (`07-trust-validation-and-capabilities.md` §7.3.1.1, "Commit order"; `05-contexts.md` §5.9.1.1).
   - **Chain items.** The node appends every submission in the class order of decision 2 and signs each entry with its monotone `append_time`. A sealed item, request or notice, is a MessagePack array of four items: the sender's current `epoch` and the item's `class` in the clear, a 12-byte `nonce`, and the `ciphertext`, which is the plaintext padded to the buckets of `09-security-model.md` §9.10.3 and sealed with AES-128-GCM under the item's own key, `item_key = ExpandWithLabel(request_key_e, "SCP request item", nonce, 16)` (RFC 9420 §8), with the epoch and the class bound in the AAD (`05-contexts.md` §5.9.1.1). The nonce is `ExpandWithLabel(request_key_e, "SCP request nonce", signer_key_hash ‖ BE64(counter), 12)`, and an item whose clear nonce is not the one its opened signing key and counter derive fails entry. The floor lets each signing key's counter enter once, so no two items that enter share an item key, and a joiner given a pending item's key opens no other item that entered with it. A nonce the sender drew at random let a member copy an honest item's epoch and nonce into its own item and keep that item pending, so every Welcome carried the key that opens the honest item to a joiner that never held its epoch's key. The node reads the epoch and the class and nothing else. The epoch tells it nothing new, because RFC 9420 §6.3.1 leaves `epoch` in the clear on every `PrivateMessage`, Commits included. A member opens an item under the key of the epoch it names, with no trial decryption, and an item whose class is not its decrypted kind's fails entry.
   - **Request keys.** For each epoch e, the request key is `MLS-Exporter("SCP request key", group_id, 32)` in epoch e, a 32-byte secret from which each item's nonce and key derive. It is 32 bytes because `ExpandWithLabel` is HKDF-Expand, whose pseudorandom key RFC 5869 §2.3 requires to be at least the hash length; a 16-byte key fails `Hkdf::from_prk` in the hkdf crate that openmls's provider calls. Every member of e derives it, and a member a Commit removes derives no key of a later epoch. No GroupInfo carries the key of its own epoch; a Welcome's GroupInfo carries the keys of earlier epochs that the joiner needs (below). Only members seal items, so the key is symmetric and has no public half (starvation option 11).
   - **Every action is a request.** Every action that needs a Commit reaches the chain as a request or, for a governance action, as a proposal notice that enters the queue on its own: a departure, a reset (the subject's `ResetRequest` with its KeyPackage and the leaf it replaces), a re-seat, a key-history advance, an own-device replacement, a checkpoint, a revocation, an outlet change and a leaf removal (`05-contexts.md` §5.9.1.1). Each carries everything its execution needs, a `counter`, a `not_after`, and a signature over both under its signer's key (`09-security-model.md` §9.5.2, `SCP-REQUEST-V1:`). A departure is signed by a current leaf of the departing member. An own-device request is signed by a current leaf of its identity, or, when the identity holds no current leaf, by the identity's `#active` current at its recorded position, so a device whose leaf a recorded recovery or rotation took out of currency still replaces that leaf; a thief holding the old leaf holds no current key and signs nothing that enters. A reset is signed by the identity's `#active`; a re-seat is signed by the current leaf of the member that submits it. A key-history request is authenticated by the key event it carries. There is no update request and no filed governance request.
   - **Entry.** At position q, every member decides from its state at q whether the item at q enters; an item that fails a check is skipped and changes no state. (1) Its `epoch` e is current at q, or e's close time is at least t_q − `REQUEST_ENTRY_WINDOW` (3,600 seconds), it opens under its item key, and its nonce is the one its signing key and counter derive. The close time of e is the t_b of the Commit that closed e, which the governance state commits (below), so a joiner reads the same close time every other member reads. A flood of epochs cannot shrink a time window, and a node that holds an item past the window is a context fault its submitter observes as a refusal. (2) It decodes under canonical MessagePack, every ECDSA signature in it has a low s, and its clear `class` is its kind's. (3) Its signer is current at q; for an own-device request signed by `#active`, its identity also holds no current leaf at q. (4) Its `counter` is above `floor[k]` for its signing key k, and entry sets `floor[k]` to it; a replayed or malleated copy fails here and takes no place under any cap, and a floor is dropped when its key can no longer sign. (5) t_q < `not_after` ≤ t_q + T. (6) A KeyPackage it carries passes decision 9's entry check. (7) The caps have room. (8) Its kind's checks hold: a re-seat names an identity for which the re-seat trigger holds in the state with only executed requests applied, carries a KeyPackage whose credential names that identity and that passes the KeyPackage checks at the identity's recorded position, and no leaf holds that KeyPackage's keys; a reset's `leaf_key_hash` names a leaf of the identity in the tree; a key-history request advances an identity that holds a leaf, is the node-list governor, or is named by an `AddMember` proposal that is open or queued; a vote names a proposal that is open at q, and its voter is eligible to vote on that proposal at q: it holds `GovernanceVote` and, under `Threshold`, is a signer, or the action is `ExtendTtl` or `PromoteContext` and it is a member (`07-trust-validation-and-capabilities.md` §7.3.1.1 step 1.4). Before this clause any member could file a vote, so a member that holds no vote filled `pending` with votes that no tally counts.
   - **Caps, with no exemption.** An identity holds at most `MAX_PENDING_PER_IDENTITY` (8) requests in the queue, of every kind, counted against the identity that signs them, and at most one reset. A proposer holds at most 8 proposals that are open and have not entered. Each identity a key-history request advances holds one key-history slot, whoever submitted it; a newer advance for that identity replaces the queued one in place and keeps its position, so a thief's key event never blocks the recovery that cuts the thief off. Each identity a re-seat names holds one re-seat slot, whoever submitted it, and the re-seat takes no place under its submitter's cap; a second re-seat naming that identity fails entry while one is in the queue. Entry check 8 limits key-history slots to members, the node-list governor and identities an open or queued `AddMember` names, and re-seat slots to members. A queued governance action takes a place under its proposer's 8 (`07-trust-validation-and-capabilities.md` §7.3.1.1, "Caps"). With n_q members at q, the queue holds at most 8n_q requests of members, queued governance actions among them; n_q key-history slots of members; n_q re-seat slots; one key-history slot of the governor; and the key-history slots of the identities that `AddMember` proposals name, at most 16n_q, because a proposer holds at most 8 open proposals and at most 8 queued actions. So k ≤ 26n_q + 1 at entry, whatever enters later. Rule 5 of the rederived design counted 17n_q; the governor, the staging advances an `AddMember` needs before it executes, and the re-seat slots add the rest.
   - **Governance actions.** Proposals and votes are notices: sealed items under checks 1 to 5, with a `counter` and a `not_after`. A vote enters only when it names a proposal open at q (check 8). Each voter holds at most one open vote per proposal: a later vote of that voter for that proposal replaces the earlier one in `pending`, and the earlier one's item key is erased. A vote whose voter stops being eligible leaves `pending` when the Commit that made the change executes. Votes therefore number at most V per open proposal, where V is the members eligible at q to vote on it (check 8), at most n_q. A vote's `expires_epoch` is replaced by `not_after`, because a committer that burns epochs with empty Commits would otherwise expire votes before approval completes. A governance action enters the queue at the first position at which its approvals, counted in the state at that position, meet the requirement and its proposer's cap has room; no member files it. Approval is fixed at entry, and a `Withdraw` counts only before entry. The executing Commit carries the block proposal record and the votes that held at entry, which every member verifies against its state at the entry position.
   - **Pending state.** The queue, in entry order with each request's entry position, its `not_after` and, for a governance action, the vote digests held at entry; the open notices, each with its position; the floor table; the retained epoch request keys, each as its epoch, its close time and a commitment; the provisional leaves, each with the t_b that seated it; and the Commit's cover position b with t_b are governance state that `governance_state_hash` commits (`05-contexts.md` §5.13.3).
   - **The rule.** A Commit for epoch N at position p carries a cover position b in its block, with c_N < b ≤ p, where c_N is the position of the Commit that opened N, or 0 at genesis. D is the requests that entered before b, in entry order. Members walk D applying each executed request to a working state; a request is void when t_b ≥ its `not_after` or it fails a head check against the working state (its signer's or proposer's authority, its target's existence, the leaf checks of RFC 9420 §7.3, the conflict rule and the executed-proposal set). The Commit lists the voids and executes the longest run of live requests at the head of D that fits: a governance action only alone, otherwise consecutive requests of other kinds until the next would pass `MAX_REQUEST_BYTES_PER_COMMIT`, `MAX_KEY_ADVANCES_PER_COMMIT` or the block's leaf limits, or is a governance action. It carries nothing the queue did not supply, apart from the committer's UpdatePath and the GroupContextExtensions proposal. Every KeyPackage it adds passes decision 9's margins at t_p.
   - **Empty Commits.** A Commit whose D holds no live request executes nothing and is valid; while D holds a live request, an empty Commit is invalid. A member that wants the epoch advanced, such as the periodic refresh of an idle standing context, commits an empty Commit. An UpdatePath heals the committer's leaf; an author heals its own leaf by an own-device request, a Remove of its leaf and an Add of its new KeyPackage that the queue forces some committer to execute.
   - **Authority is the request's.** A Commit that executes a request reads the request's signer, or for a governance action its proposer and voters, for every authorization: the token's issuer or the context's creator for a revocation, the holder of `outlet:register` for an outlet, the checkpoint's creator for a checkpoint. The committer needs no capability. It stays the recorder: the log leaf carries its `actor_did` and its leaf signature, and the leaf's `timestamp` is the request's t_q, so every member derives the same leaf whoever commits. The UpdatePath keeps the committer's `signature_key` and credential and changes its HPKE `encryption_key`, so the committer re-issues its `0xFF03` attestation over the new `encryption_key` under the same `active_key` (`09-security-model.md` §9.7.3). A checkpoint request names its creator and carries the full quorum; `CheckpointPayload` gains `creator_did`, and `MAX_GOVERNANCE_LEAF_BYTES` grows from 15,616 to 15,680 bytes to hold it.
   - **Key retention and the joiner.** A Commit for epoch N with cover b lists in `retained_request_keys` every epoch e ≤ N that is N or whose close time is at least t_b − `REQUEST_ENTRY_WINDOW`, in ascending epoch order, each as `[e, close time, SHA-256("SCP-REQUEST-KEY-COMMIT" ‖ BE64(e) ‖ request_key_e)]`, N's close time being this Commit's own t_b. A member erases an epoch key when it merges a Commit whose list omits that epoch. It keeps the item key of each pending request and open notice, and erases it when the item leaves `pending`. It stores both kinds encrypted at rest, zeroizes each on erase, and deletes it under SQLite `secure_delete` (ADR-001 criterion 6, as this record amends it). The Welcome carries the listed epoch keys and the item key of each pending item in `scp_request_secrets` (`0xFF06`, `09-security-model.md` §9.18.7). The joiner checks each epoch key against its commitment, and each item it opens against the digest `pending` holds. It reads the bodies from the node's chain, which keeps them for T, and applies entry itself from the committed cover position b onward (`23-sync-and-offline-strategy.md` §23.7). Every input of entry check 1 is committed state, so the joiner's verdicts are the old members'. An adder that withholds keys causes an availability failure the joiner detects, never a fork, and a joiner opens no item of an epoch beyond what `pending` needs, because it holds no epoch key older than the window.
   - **The bound.** A request with k live requests ahead of it at entry executes, or is void at its own `not_after`, within k + 2 canonical Commits: one Commit to place c_N after its entry position, then at least one execution per Commit while D is not empty. The bound counts Commits, whatever rate any member commits at, holds for every kind, and k is fixed at entry by the caps. It needs some member to commit, and any member with a current leaf can, because executing a request needs no capability.
   - **Residuals.** Liveness: a request executes only when some member commits, and a re-seat or a reset waits on a current member to submit or commit it. A frozen context: when no current member is reachable, recovery is re-founding (plan D9). Welcome size: `0xFF06` holds at most `REQUEST_ENTRY_WINDOW` × the node's Commit rate + 1 epoch keys of 48 bytes each (epoch, close time and key), about 1.73 MB (36,001 × 48 bytes) for an hour of 10 Commits a second, and one 24-byte item key (position and key) per pending item: at most 26n_q + 1 requests, 8n_q open proposals and 8n_q × V votes, V at most n_q. With 50 members and 50 eligible voters that is 21,701 item keys, about 0.52 MB. The node's rate bounds the first term and the caps bound the second; both are availability costs, not forks. Class disclosure: the node and every chain reader learn each submission's class (decision 2). Admission: a flood delays honest submissions by the share of appends it captures and never stalls a class; it refuses an honest connection's submission for capacity only when it is spread over at least `NODE_CLASS_BACKLOG` connections that each hold no more of the class than that connection, opened before it (decision 2). A capacity refusal counts as a refusal, and a member re-founds on a refusal only when its submission has stayed out of the chain for `REQUEST_ENTRY_WINDOW` (`09-security-model.md` §9.9.4).

| I10 | An honest fork raises no equivocation alert | M16 | Does not arise: honest members never hold two logs. A log disagreement is the node equivocating, which two chain entries for one position prove (Q4). |
| I11 | Verdicts read no clock | SCP-357, the openmls patch | Protected by decision 9 with no patch. Entry reads t_q, voidness reads t_b, and the execution margins read t_p, all node-signed times every member holds, and no member's clock. A member whose openmls refuses a Commit outside the processable window recovers and takes no other Commit for that epoch. |
| I12 | A late release does not undo a merged re-join | Carried re-seat, M5a depth bound | Does not arise: the chain is append-only, so no Commit is released after members merged a later one. |
| I13 | A departure can be re-executed after the member uninstalls | M10 `DepartureRequest` | Does not arise: no losing branch exists to re-execute from. |
| I14 | A member a losing branch removed reads no message on the canonical path | M11 send barrier | Does not arise: a Remove is final when its Commit is canonical, at every member. |
| I15 | Members on two sides of a divergence see it | M15 `ForkNotice`, `GroupSplit` | Does not arise among honest members. A node that shows two chains signs two entries for one position, which any member can prove and act on by re-founding (plan D9). |
| I16 | Two devices of one person converge | M9 | Protected by decision 1: both devices read one chain from one node. |
| I17 | A restrictive-only change (a Remove, a revocation) is never dropped | G, restrictive-only class | Protected by decision 10: a Remove, a revocation and every other change is a request that every later Commit must reach in entry order until it executes or is void at its `not_after` or a head check. |
| I18 | A re-added member's KeyPackage is used once | M8 fresh KeyPackage | Does not arise: no Add is re-applied. |
| I19 | The tree's leaves match the governance membership | M8 tree reconciliation | Does not arise: one Commit per epoch changes both together, as before ADR-067. |

## Consequences

- Two members that commit at one epoch no longer split the group, and no member keeps parent states, branch stores, a PPRF over child init secrets, a journal, a key ledger or an epoch table.
- The governance block body has four items, and the body bound is 32,077 bytes. Spec 25's fork-merge vectors (§25.33, Vectors 71 to 79) are removed, and the `ForkNotice` and `DepartureRequest` preimages and the fork record leave `09-security-model.md` §9.5.2.
- A context with its node off makes no progress: no member can commit until the node returns (ruling 4).
- A malformed or stale Commit costs a chain position and changes no member's state. A malformed, replayed or malleated item fails entry, costs a chain position, and takes no place under any cap.
- A member trusts the node for liveness, for order, and for the `append_time` it signs, never for validity, because every member verifies every Commit and every item. A node that equivocates, drops a receipted submission, or signs a decreasing `append_time` is proven by its own signatures (Q4) and answered by re-founding (plan D9). A node that signs a time far in the future, or holds an item past the entry window, is attributable and answered as a node that refuses submissions (decision 9).
- The node admits submissions under per-class and per-connection backlogs and a per-connection rate, evicting from the connection that holds the most of a full class rather than refusing the arrival, and appends them in the class order, Remove first, then re-join, then other, with one append in four reserved for re-join and one for other while each holds a submission. It learns each submission's class and each sealed item's epoch, and nothing else of its content. Every Commit executes requests in entry order and nothing else, so no member's action waits on another member's choice to commit it, and the caps bound the requests one identity holds in the queue. An empty Commit is valid while no live request is due, so a member that wants an epoch advanced for post-compromise security commits one.
- Every chain entry carries the node-signed `append_time`, every member checks a request's KeyPackage at entry against t_q and each added KeyPackage at execution against t_p with decision 9's margins, and a device outside the processable window recovers through fast-forward or a reset (decision 9).
- A receipt names a position and carries no other verdict. Every submission carries its class outside the MLS message and the seal, and no external Commit is valid.
- The update request, the per-period request keys, `request_keys_hash`, the `0xFF05` GroupInfo extension, the digest check, the fixed W, the key-history cap exemption, the filed governance request (kind `0x03`) and the vote's `expires_epoch` do not exist; options 7 and 8 of the starvation options record why. `0xFF06` keeps its codepoint and now carries the retained epoch request keys and the pending items' keys to a joiner. The intake key, the HPKE request key pairs, and the re-seat request X signed itself with its own preimage do not exist either; starvation options 10 and 11 record why.
- Withdrawal of an approval is a `Withdraw` notice before the action enters (`05-contexts.md` §5.9.1). A member changes its `signature_key` only by an own-device request that removes its leaf and adds its new KeyPackage, because an UpdatePath keeps the committer's `signature_key`. `propose_update` (`crates/scp-mls/src/ratchet.rs`) passes `LeafNodeParameters::default()` and rotates no `signature_key` either.
- This record takes in the part of plan decision D6 it needs: governance proposals, governance votes and requests are sealed chain items that members read for as long as the node keeps them (decision 10). D6's ordering of the other application messages in place of ADR-051's causal DAG is not changed by this record, and the artifacts that describe the DAG stay as they are.

## Acceptance Criteria

The stories that implement this record list their criteria in `.docs/prds/main.json` under the gate `gate-node-order`: SCP-357 (a Commit kept staged and unmerged until its chain position, and the KeyPackage checks with margins against the node's `append_time` at entry and at execution), SCP-351 (the first valid Commit per epoch, the committer's merge at its own position, the sealed chain items and their keys, entry, each request kind, the caps, the governance notices, the Commit class, the cover-position rule, key retention and erasure, and spec 25's Vectors 96 to 100, the head record's among them), SCP-315 (decision 8: its criteria headed 'Sender hold', 'Re-seat request', 'Re-seat request, entry', 'Re-seat request, chain order', 'Own-device Commit', 'Provisional seat', 'Re-seat after a rotation' and 'Own-device request under `#active`') and SCP-361 (the `member:X` conflict key). The node side of decisions 1 and 2, the chain, its receipts and the submission transport, belongs to the execution of plan decision D1, which `~/.claude/plans/identity-artifacts/node-backend-execution-plan.md` records as having no story yet. Every story criterion is implementable now.

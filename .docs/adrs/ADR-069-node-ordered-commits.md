# ADR-069: Node-Ordered Commits

**Status:** Accepted 2026-11-02. Alec settled the design on 2026-10-25 (decisions D1 to D3 and question Q4 of the context-operation plan), and ruled "Rewrite now" on 2026-11-02 for the artifacts that ADR-067 had shaped. **Amended 2026-11-02:** decision 10 records Alec's ruling that the node's order must stop starvation and enforces it by pending requests that every member verifies; decision 2 moves D2's priority order from the node into that verdict; decision 8 makes the re-seat a request that members execute, and records that two of Alec's rulings expired with their premise; decision 9 records the design he accepted for checking a KeyPackage's lifetime against the time the node signs into the chain, and adds margins to that check so that every member inside a stated window can process every valid Commit.

**Supersedes:** ADR-067, Concurrent Commits and fork merge, in `phase-6.md`. ADR-067's status flips to `Superseded by ADR-069`, and its body stays as the historical record.

**Amends:** ADR-066 decisions 5, 10, 32 and 40 and its acceptance-criteria paragraph; ADR-031 §7 and its 2026-10-17 amendment; ADR-029 §5b, §5c and §6 step 4, and its rationale paragraph on conflict resolution; ADR-001 criterion 6; ADR-051 §3, "No trusted sequencer". Each amended record carries a dated amendment that names this record.

**Spec:** `07-trust-validation-and-capabilities.md` §7.3.1 and §7.3.1.1 ("Competing Commits", "The re-seat", "Commit order"), `05-contexts.md` §5.9.1, §5.12.3.4, §5.12.3.5 and §5.13.3 ("Governance state", rules 11, 13, 15 and 16), `09-security-model.md` §9.5.2, §9.7.1, §9.7.2, §9.9.3, §9.10.2, §9.14 and §9.18, `10-infrastructure-and-self-hosting.md` §10.2, §10.3 and §10.8.1, `23-sync-and-offline-strategy.md` §23.4, §23.5 and §23.7, and `25-test-vectors.md` §25.33.

## Context

`admit_and_merge_own_commit` (`crates/scp-mls/src/group.rs`) merges the committer's own Commit before anyone orders it. When two members commit at one epoch N, each merges its own Commit, and each member's openmls then rejects the other Commit as `WrongEpoch`, so the group splits.

ADR-066 decision 5 had answered the race with "Epoch order decides conflicts": a Commit that loses the race for an epoch never merges, and its executor re-checks it and sends it again. That rule needs one order of Commits that every member reads. On 2026-10-24 no party could give that order, because Alec had ruled "We can’t rely on daily presence" and the relays were untrusted store-and-forward services. ADR-067 therefore let every member keep both children of an epoch, choose one by the least (rank, cth), and re-apply the losing child's changes through fork records, imports and a merge function G. It needed sixteen mechanisms (M1 to M16), a fifth body item `merge` in the governance block, a journal, a key ledger, an epoch table, branch retention for up to seven days, a PPRF over child init secrets, two signed notice records, and ten accepted residuals.

On 2026-10-25 Alec settled how a context runs (`~/.claude/plans/context-operation-plan.md`, rulings 1 to 10):

- "Contexts most certainly have operators, and in fact, require them." "Nodes are pieces of infrastructure that operate many things. One of those things could absolutely be a specific context."
- "if a context runs on a single node and that node is off then the context is unreachable. Se la vie."
- "The context is the engine for finalization." "Why would governance be involved? It's an immutable part of the protocol".
- D1: "One node runs a context and is its MLS Delivery Service: it totally orders submitted Commits, one per epoch. Members verify every Commit."
- D2: members submit Commits directly to the context's node, with the priority class (Remove, re-join, other) as a field of the submission outside the MLS message, ordered "Remove first, then re-join, then arrival." Decision 2 keeps that order and moves it from the node's append order into the verdict every member reaches, and the submission carries no class field.
- D3: "The node holds no MLS group secrets."
- Q4: the node signs each chain entry (position, item hash, previous hash) and returns a signed receipt that assigns a position to each accepted submission, under a key the operator binds to its identifier when the context is instantiated.

A node that runs the context is online whenever the context is reachable, so the ordering party that ADR-067 lacked exists. The question this record answers is how members turn the node's order into one Commit per epoch when the node cannot read a Commit.

## Options considered

1. **Keep ADR-067 and add the node as a relay.** Every mechanism of ADR-067 stays, and the node only delivers. This lost because the node already gives a total order, so every mechanism that exists to merge two orders protects against a case the node removes. ADR-067's own necessity table justifies each mechanism by a fork, and a fork needs two Commits that two members both merge.
2. **The node accepts one Commit per epoch and refuses the rest.** The node reads the epoch from the `PrivateMessage` framing (RFC 9420 §6.3, which leaves `epoch` in the clear), appends the first Commit it receives for the current epoch, refuses the rest, and then expects the next epoch. This lost because of D3: the node holds no group secret, so it cannot verify a Commit. A member that sends a malformed Commit for epoch N would take epoch N's place, every member would reject it, and the node would refuse every honest Commit for epoch N as stale, so the context would stop. The node also cannot prove a refusal (Q4: "Refusal to accept is provable to no one").
3. **The node orders every submission, and members take the first valid Commit for each epoch.** The node appends every submission it accepts to one signed chain. Every member processes the chain in position order, and the canonical Commit for epoch N is the first Commit in the chain that names epoch N and passes the member's verification at epoch N. This option is chosen. It meets D1 ("one per epoch", "Members verify every Commit") and D3, because the choice reads only the chain order and each member's own verdict, and ADR-066 decision 10 makes that verdict a function of the Commit's bytes and the state every member holds identically.
4. **A member quorum or a witness set signs the order.** Members or witness nodes would sign which Commit comes first. This lost because Alec ruled on 2026-10-25 that "The context is the engine for finalization" and asked of governance "Why would governance be involved? It's an immutable part of the protocol", so no member, witness or governance action takes part in ordering.

### Options considered for starvation (decision 10, 2026-11-02)

A Remove built for epoch N loses to a committer's Commit for N, is rebuilt for N + 1, and arrives after the committer's Commit for N + 1, because the committer builds its next Commit while the remover is still processing the chain. Any rule under which a Remove or a re-join reaches the chain only as a Commit that its own author rebuilds after each loss lets a fast committer win every epoch.

1. **Append on arrival, with no rule.** The node holds at most one submission at a time, so D2's priority order never acts, and a member that commits nonstop starves every Remove. This lost to Alec's ruling of 2026-11-02.
2. **A fixed batching window on the node's clock.** The node collects submissions for a fixed time and appends each batch in D2's order. This lost because the window bounds nothing: a remover whose rebuild takes longer than the window loses every epoch to a committer whose next Commit arrives inside the window.
3. **A member-verified rule over request items.** A Remove-class or re-join-class action enters the chain as a request item that carries its own authority: the proposal and its approvals, or the signed request of the member it concerns. Every member treats a Commit for epoch N as valid only when it executes the first pending request that a Commit at N could execute, and any member may commit that request. This option is chosen. The node reads nothing it cannot read (D3) and orders on arrival. A committer that wants to starve a Remove can commit only by executing it. The bound counts requests ahead and epochs and reads no clock (decision 10). An earlier form of this option made a lost Remove-class Commit, not a request, the thing every later Commit had to respect, and it lost for two reasons that request items remove. Members would have verified a Commit at an epoch they had merged past, so they would have kept that epoch's secrets after the merge, against RFC 9420's deletion schedule (invariant I8); a request is verified at the position it enters, against the state every member holds there. A remover that never rebuilt would have blocked every other Commit forever; a request needs no rebuild, because any member executes it, and a request that can no longer execute leaves the pending set by a rule every member applies.
4. **A hold that any later priority submission ends.** The node cannot verify a submission's class, so a committer that wants to starve a Remove sends one malformed Remove-class submission, the hold ends, and the committer's Commit is appended first. This lost for that reason.
5. **A hold that only the lost submission's own submitter ends, capped by the node's clock.** The node would hold lower-class submissions after a Remove-class or re-join-class submission lost its epoch, until the submitter revealed the secret behind a commitment the lost submission carried, or until `PRIORITY_HOLD_SECS` (120 seconds) passed on the node's clock. The first draft of this amendment chose it, and the review of that draft on 2026-11-02 replaced it with option 3 for four reasons. The node cannot verify a class, so each malformed Remove-class submission delays every lower-class submission by 120 seconds, and nothing bounds how many one sender submits. The class field tells the node which submissions remove or re-join members. The bound held only for a remover or re-seater whose rebuilt Commit arrived within 120 seconds. The hold secret was a bearer secret, so whoever learned it could open the hold.

## Decision

1. **The node orders.** The node that runs a context is its MLS Delivery Service, the role RFC 9750 (the MLS architecture) gives the service that orders and delivers a group's messages. A member sends each Commit it makes to that node, at the address the context's `ContextNodeAddress` names. The node appends every submission it accepts to the context's chain at the next position, signs the chain entry, and returns a signed receipt naming the position (Q4). The node holds no MLS group secret (D3), so it never decides whether a Commit is valid.
2. **Priority is a member verdict.** D2's order, Remove first, then re-join, then the rest, decides which action a Commit must execute, and every member enforces it in its verdict (decision 10). A submission carries the MLS `PrivateMessage` bytes and nothing else, so the node learns no class and appends every submission in arrival order. An action is Remove-class when it derives a `MemberLeft` leaf, and re-join-class when it derives a `MemberReset` leaf or is a re-seat (decision 8). This departs from the letter of D2, which carried the class as a field outside the MLS message: a node that holds no group secret cannot verify that field (option 4 of the starvation options), the field tells the node which submissions remove or re-join members, and the order acts only on what members verify.
3. **One Commit per epoch.** Every member processes the chain in position order. At epoch N, the canonical Commit is the first chain item that is a Commit naming epoch N and that passes steps 1 and 2 of `07-trust-validation-and-capabilities.md` §7.3.1.1 "Receiver processing" in the member's state at epoch N. A member skips every other Commit: a Commit naming an epoch below its current one, and a Commit naming its current epoch that fails verification. A skipped Commit changes no member's state. Because every member's verdict reads only the Commit, the `append_time` of the Commit's chain entry (decision 9), and state that every member holds identically (ADR-066 decision 10), every honest member merges the same Commit at every epoch.
4. **The committer merges its own Commit at its position.** A committer keeps its own Commit staged and unmerged after it submits it. It merges the staged Commit when, processing the chain in order, it reaches the position its receipt names, its group is still at the epoch the Commit names, and the Commit passes steps 1 and 2 at that position. Otherwise it discards the staged Commit, which changed no member's state. The protocol obliges no rebuild and no resubmit. A Remove-class or re-join-class action is a pending request, which any later Commit executes until it executes or becomes void (decision 10). Any other action is a proposal message in the chain, and a member that wants it executed commits it again or reports it `Invalidated`, as ADR-066 decision 5 states. The committer never merges a Commit that the chain does not hold.
5. **Conflicts are serial.** "Committed first" in ADR-031 §7 means earlier in the chain. The conflict rule of §7.3.1.1 "Conflicts" and the executed-proposal set settle every pair of actions in chain order, and no merge function runs. The `member:X` conflict key stays: the governance state keeps `change_seq["member:X"]` after X leaves, so an Add of X anchored before a Remove of X loses to that Remove, and an invitation anchored after the Remove passes.
6. **The governance block has four body items.** The body loses item 5, `merge`, and with it the fork record, the import leaves, the re-applications and the carried re-seats. The body bound returns to 1 + 168 + 3 + 16,224 + 15,616 + 1 = 32,013 bytes, 751 bytes below the 32,764-byte body (`07-trust-validation-and-capabilities.md` §7.3.1.1, "Why 32,768 bytes and 96 approvals").
7. **No merge state.** The journal, the key ledger, the epoch table, the fork records, the imports, the pending joiners and the re-seat windows leave the governance state, and `merge_state_hash` leaves the `0xFF02` extension (`05-contexts.md` §5.13.3 rule 16 is withdrawn). A Commit with an empty block again carries the GroupContextExtensions proposal with its extensions unchanged (ADR-066 decision 32).
8. **The re-seat is a request.** A member X every one of whose leaves its latest recorded `RootRecovery` cut off returns by a re-seat request (`05-contexts.md` §5.12.3.5; `09-security-model.md` §9.7.1, "The re-seat"). X signs a `ReseatRequest` that carries one KeyPackage of X. A member that verifies the request sends it to the node as a request item, where it becomes pending (decision 10). The first canonical Commit at whose epoch it is executable removes every leaf of X the recovery cut off, Adds the request's KeyPackage, and sends X the Welcome, whoever commits it. X learns the outcome from that Welcome, so it reads no receipt and no chain item before it joins. Every external Commit is rejected, and the external re-seat, the re-seat wrap, `ReseatGroupInfo`, the GroupInfo with `external_pub`, rank −1, the re-seat window, the `D_MAX` send rule, the carried re-seat and the unused carried seat are removed.
   - **Two rulings that expired with their premise.** On 2026-10-21 Alec ruled that the re-joiner "rebuilds its Commit against the new epoch and sends it again". Its premise was a re-joiner that commits its own re-seat as an external Commit and loses an epoch to another Commit. X now commits nothing, and the members execute its request, so no re-seat of X loses an epoch and nothing is rebuilt. On 2026-11-02 Alec answered "Ok" to the recommendation: "the node's signed receipt tells [the external re-seater] 'your submission lost; the epoch is now N+1', and the re-seater retries. Every member already trusts the node for ordering, and a lying node can only delay the re-seater, because the re-seater's next attempt is verified in full." Its premise was an external re-seater that holds no secret of the epoch it joins and so cannot see in the chain that it lost. X now learns the outcome from the Welcome, so the receipt carries no `lost` flag. Neither ruling is reversed: each governs the external re-seat, and that construct no longer exists.
9. **A KeyPackage's lifetime is checked against the node's append time, with margins, and SCP carries no openmls patch.** The choice of decision 3 requires every member that processes a Commit to reach one verdict on it, and a lifetime check against each member's own clock gives two members two verdicts. Alec accepted the design below on 2026-11-02 ("Accepted."), except the margins in "The check", which the review of 2026-11-02 added after his acceptance to close the case that the "Closed" bullet below names. It replaces the bound that followed his 2026-10-04 answer "no patch, bound, leave us a note for the happy future path", under which a member whose clock ran more than one hour ahead of the adder's, or a member that processed a Commit more than 7 days after it was sent, refused a Commit that the other members merged. Earlier on 2026-11-02 he had rejected an openmls patch: "you offered me a solution that did not rely on a patch. What has changed since then that makes you feel like we need one?"
   - **Append time.** The chain entry the node signs for each position carries `append_time`, the node's clock in Unix seconds when it appended the item (`09-security-model.md` §9.9.3). This is a requirement on the chain entry's format, whose bytes no spec defines yet. Each entry's `append_time` MUST be at least the previous entry's. A member that finds a lower one holds two signed entries that prove the node signed a bad time, processes nothing past the lower entry, and the members re-found the context (plan D9).
   - **The check.** For the Commit at position p, every member checks each KeyPackage the Commit adds against t, the `append_time` of chain entry p: `not_after - t >= 608,100` seconds (7 days + 55 minutes), `t - not_before >= 3,000` seconds (50 minutes), `not_before < not_after`, and `not_after - not_before` no greater than `MAX_KEYPACKAGE_ATTESTATION_LIFETIME` (`09-security-model.md` §9.7.1, KeyPackage `Lifetime` checks). The check belongs to step 1 of `07-trust-validation-and-capabilities.md` §7.3.1.1 "Receiver processing", so it is part of the verdict decision 3 uses. Every member reads the same t, so every member reaches one verdict. A Commit that adds a KeyPackage that expires within 7 days and 55 minutes of t is invalid at every member, which closes the case of a member that commits an Add of an expired, leaked or nearly expired KeyPackage itself.
   - **The processable window.** openmls's own check passes at a member whose clock reads c when `not_before <= c < not_after`. A Commit that passes the check therefore passes openmls at every member that processes it within 7 days of t with a clock at most 55 minutes ahead of the node's and at most 50 minutes behind it: c < t + 7 days + 55 minutes <= `not_after`, and c >= t - 3,000 seconds >= `not_before`.
   - **The honest adder.** An adder still requires `KEY_PACKAGE_MIN_REMAINING_LIFETIME` (608,400 seconds, 7 days + 1 hour) and `KEY_PACKAGE_MIN_NOT_BEFORE_AGE` (3,300 seconds) under its own clock, which leaves 300 seconds on each margin for the time between its check and t and for the 5-minute clock-skew tolerance of `09-security-model.md` §9.14. `KEY_PACKAGE_MIN_REMAINING_LIFETIME` now has this reason: it is the 7-day processing window plus 55 minutes of clock lead plus those 300 seconds. Its earlier reason, the 7-day relay TTL of an InvitationBundle plus the republish rule of `09-security-model.md` §9.10.2, sized it for a copy of a Commit that a relay holds; the check against t makes the processing window, not a relay copy, the quantity it bounds.
   - **No caller time.** No SCP API takes a time from its caller for this check. The node-signed `append_time` is its only time input.
   - **Order of the checks.** The added KeyPackages travel inside the Commit's encrypted content, and openmls 0.9.0 decrypts and validates a Commit in one public call, `process_message`; its decrypt-only step, `unprotect_message`, is crate-private. SCP therefore runs the check on the staged Commit's Adds after `process_message` returns and before the merge. When `process_message` refuses the Commit with a lifetime error, the error names the bound that failed, `not_after` for `Expired` and `not_before` for `NotValidYet`, and the member applies the check to that bound: if the bound fails at t, the Commit is invalid, as it is at every member.
   - **The committer.** A committer that reaches its own Commit's position (decision 4) runs the same check against t before it merges its staged Commit, and discards the staged Commit when a KeyPackage fails, as every other member skips it.
   - **openmls stays unpatched.** openmls keeps its check against the system clock. Outside the processable window that check can refuse a Commit whose KeyPackages pass at t. Such a refusal is not a verdict. The member never skips that Commit and never takes a later Commit for that epoch. It stays at the epoch before the Commit and recovers into the group's state through Welcome-based fast-forward or a group state reset (`23-sync-and-offline-strategy.md` §23.4.1 source 3, §23.5), so it forks from no member.
   - **Closed: a Commit no honest member can process.** The open question of that name asked what happens when every member's openmls refuses a Commit that passed at t, as when a member adds a KeyPackage that expires seconds after t. The margins make such a Commit invalid at every member. A valid Commit is processable by every member inside the window, and a member outside it recovers through a member inside it. When no member at the Commit's state is reachable inside the window, the members re-found the context from the last state they verified (plan D9).
   - **Cost.** A device outside the window recovers through fast-forward or a reset. Two rulings of Alec's cover the two causes, each its own. For a clock that diverges from the node's, Alec on 2026-11-02, on item 6 of the design as presented to him: "6 is extremely normal. When system clocks diverge from remote ones, users almost always expect to see issues, both at the operating system and application level. This is a normal thing; it's not a failure mode on our part. If anything, it's desirable." For state more than 7 days stale, Alec chose on 2026-10-24 the 7-day message lifetime, whose option text read in part: "a device offline more than 7 days resets on reconnect under spec 23 §23.5.1" (`09-security-model.md` §9.10.2, Rulings).
   - **Trust in the node's time.** Members trust the node's signed `append_time`, as they already trust the node for order. Alec on 2026-11-02: "Generally trust less but if we are doing trust it's in contexts not clients." A bad time is attributable: the node's signature on each entry proves which time it stated, and two entries whose times decrease prove a bad time.
   - **Message lifetime.** Alec ruled on 2026-10-24: "Just use the max number the current design supports right now and design for a higher number as well". The maximum stays 7 days, `MAX_BLOB_TTL`, and its derivation changes. A device offline more than 7 days resets under `23-sync-and-offline-strategy.md` §23.5.1 trigger 1 and reads nothing sent before its re-add, and a Commit is processable within 7 days of its `append_time`, so a message kept longer than 7 days reaches no reader the design admits (`09-security-model.md` §9.10.2).
10. **Pending requests.** Alec ruled on 2026-11-02, when the starvation case was presented to him as an open question: "the whole point of context driven commit ordering and processing is to STOP starvation. No." Members enforce that ruling in their verdict, and the node enforces nothing (`07-trust-validation-and-capabilities.md` §7.3.1.1, "Commit order").
   - **Chain items.** This record takes in the part of plan decision D6 it needs: the node appends governance proposals, governance votes and request messages to the chain as it appends Commits, each a bare MLS `PrivateMessage` (`05-contexts.md` §5.9.1). The readable window decides which non-Commit items a member reads: an item that names the epoch the member's group is at when it reaches the item's position. Every member is at the same epoch at every position, so every member reads the same items. A member skips every other non-Commit item, even one that names an earlier epoch whose secrets it still holds, by that one rule.
   - **Requests.** A request is a chain item that carries a Remove-class or re-join-class action and its own authority: a governance request (a proposal and the votes that meet its requirement, or in `SingleAdmin` a proposal whose proposer holds the capability), a departure request sent by the departing member's own leaf, a reset request carrying the member's `ResetRequest` and one KeyPackage, or a re-seat request carrying the member's `ReseatRequest`. A request becomes pending at its position when it passes there every check a Commit executing it would apply, its KeyPackage passes decision 9's margins at its own entry's `append_time`, and the cap admits it.
   - **The cap.** At most one departure, reset or re-seat request per identity is pending at once, except one reset request per leaf the identity holds, and at most one governance request per `proposal_id`. Every member skips a request the cap refuses, so one sender cannot fill the pending set.
   - **The rule.** A Commit for epoch N at position p is valid only when it executes the first pending request, in D2's order and then by position, that a Commit at N could execute with t the `append_time` of entry p, and when it executes nothing that is not a pending request. When no pending request is executable, the rule constrains nothing. Any committer discharges the rule, and a Commit that executes a request needs no capability of its committer, because the request carries its authority.
   - **Leaving.** A request leaves the pending set when a canonical Commit executes it or when it becomes void by a rule every member applies: its subject is no longer a member, its proposal executed, its approvals lapsed, its KeyPackage fails the margins, its reset counter is at or below the floor, or its re-seat trigger fails. A request is therefore pending only while the `append_time` of the entries keeps its KeyPackage inside the margins, and no client time is an input.
   - **Group state.** The pending set, as the SHA-256 digests of the pending requests in chain order, is a member of the governance state that `governance_state_hash` commits (`05-contexts.md` §5.13.3). A Welcome joiner fetches the request bytes with the leaves it rebuilds (`23-sync-and-offline-strategy.md` §23.7) and accepts each by its digest under the confirmed group context, so no key and no byte that the adder alone supplies decides what the joiner holds pending.
   - **The bound.** A Remove-class request that becomes pending at position q executes by the (k + 1)-th canonical Commit after q at whose epoch it is executable, where k counts the Remove-class requests pending ahead of it at q. A re-join-class request executes by the (k + j + 1)-th, where k counts the requests pending ahead of it at q and j, the Remove-class requests that become pending after q, is at most the number of members at q, because no Add merges while a request is executable and each Remove removes a distinct member. The bound counts requests and epochs, whatever rate another member commits at, and reads no clock. It needs some member to commit, and the proposer of a governance Remove and a departing member can each commit their own request.

## Invariants ADR-067 protected

Each row names an invariant ADR-067 protected and where it now lives, or why it no longer arises.

| I1 | Two Commits at one epoch never split the group | M1, M2, M9 | Protected by decisions 1 and 3: every member reads one chain and merges the same first valid Commit per epoch. |
| I2 | Every device of every member chooses the same Commit | M2, M2r | Protected by decision 3; the choice reads the chain position and a verdict that ADR-066 decision 10 makes identical at every member. |
| I3 | A committer never merges a Commit that loses | M14 | Protected by decision 4: the committer merges only at its own chain position. |
| I4 | No change is lost silently, and its author learns the outcome | M3 obligations 1 and 3, `ConcurrentChangeExpired` | Protected by decisions 4 and 10: a Commit that is not first executed nothing. Any other action is a proposal in the chain, which a member commits again or reports `Invalidated` (ADR-066 decision 5). |
| I5 | A re-joiner and a Remove are not starved by a member that commits nonstop | M2r, M4 rank-0 window, rank −1 | Protected by decisions 2 and 10: every valid Commit executes the first executable pending request in D2's order, so a Remove executes within k + 1 and a re-join within k + j + 1 canonical Commits at which it is executable, whatever rate another member commits at. |

| I15 | Members on two sides of a divergence see it | M15 `ForkNotice`, `GroupSplit` | Does not arise among honest members. A node that shows two chains signs two entries for one position, which any member can prove and act on by re-founding (plan D9). |
| I16 | Two devices of one person converge | M9 | Protected by decision 1: both devices read one chain from one node. |
| I17 | A restrictive-only change (a Remove, a revocation) is never dropped | G, restrictive-only class | Protected by decision 10 for every action that derives a `MemberLeft` leaf: it is a pending request that every later Commit must execute until it executes or becomes void. Any other restrictive change is a proposal in the chain that a member commits again or reports `Invalidated` (decision 4). |
| I18 | A re-added member's KeyPackage is used once | M8 fresh KeyPackage | Does not arise: no Add is re-applied. |
| I19 | The tree's leaves match the governance membership | M8 tree reconciliation | Does not arise: one Commit per epoch changes both together, as before ADR-067. |

## Consequences

- Two members that commit at one epoch no longer split the group, and no member keeps parent states, branch stores, a PPRF over child init secrets, a journal, a key ledger or an epoch table.
- The governance block body has four items, and the body bound is 32,013 bytes. Spec 25's fork-merge vectors (§25.33, Vectors 71 to 79) are removed, and the `ForkNotice` and `DepartureRequest` preimages and the fork record leave `09-security-model.md` §9.5.2.
- A context with its node off makes no progress: no member can commit until the node returns (ruling 4).
- A malformed or stale Commit costs a chain position and changes no member's state.
- A member trusts the node for liveness, for order, and for the `append_time` it signs, never for validity, because every member verifies every Commit and every request. A node that equivocates, drops a receipted submission, or signs a decreasing `append_time` is proven by its own signatures (Q4) and answered by re-founding (plan D9), and a node that signs a false time is attributable by the signature on that time.
- The node appends every submission in arrival order and learns no class. A malformed request or Commit costs a chain position, and members skip it. While a pending request is executable, every Commit executes only pending requests, so other changes wait for Removes and re-joins, and the cap bounds the pending requests per identity and per proposal.
- Every chain entry carries the node-signed `append_time`, every member checks each added KeyPackage against it with decision 9's margins, and a device outside the processable window recovers through fast-forward or a reset (decision 9).
- A receipt names a position and carries no other verdict. No submission carries a field outside the MLS message, and no external Commit is valid.
- Withdrawal and re-signing by key rotation (`05-contexts.md` §5.9.1, ADR-066 decision 10) still rest on an Update that rotates `signature_key`, and `propose_update` (`crates/scp-mls/src/ratchet.rs`) passes `LeafNodeParameters::default()`, so no Update rotates it yet.
- This record takes in the part of plan decision D6 it needs: governance proposals, governance votes and request messages are chain items read inside the readable window (decision 10). D6's ordering of the other application messages in place of ADR-051's causal DAG is not changed by this record, and the artifacts that describe the DAG stay as they are.

## Acceptance Criteria

The stories that implement this record list their criteria in `.docs/prds/main.json` under the gate `gate-node-order`: SCP-357 (a Commit kept staged and unmerged until its chain position, and the KeyPackage check with margins against the node's `append_time`), SCP-351 (the first valid Commit per epoch, the committer's merge at its own position, the readable window, and the pending-request rule) and SCP-361 (the `member:X` conflict key). The node side of decisions 1 and 2, the chain, its receipts and the submission transport, belongs to the execution of plan decision D1, which `~/.claude/plans/identity-artifacts/node-backend-execution-plan.md` records as having no story yet.
